# Zinc & Lithium Mining — Annotated Reading List & Study Plan
### Companion resource guide to the main curriculum. Expands and supersedes the scattered resource mentions in the main document.

**How to use this.** Part 1 is the *sequenced study plan* — what to read, in what order, tied to the curriculum phases, with checkpoints. Part 2 is the *full annotated library* — every resource with a complete citation, a level tag, a free/paid flag, and a one-line reason it's here. Part 3 is a *minimum-viable fast track* for ~4–6 weeks if you can't do the full program. Part 4 is *calibration on the resources themselves* (what's authoritative and current vs. older-but-canonical vs. promotional). Part 5 is a practical guide to reading the two documents that build the most credibility fastest: a technical report and a quarterly.

**Tags used throughout:** `[Intro]` accessible/first-pass · `[Working]` builds real fluency · `[Advanced]` deep/technical · `[Ref]` reference to dip into, not read cover-to-cover. Access: **(free)**, **(paid)**, **(freemium)** = free tier + paid data.

**A note on how to weight this.** The fastest credibility per hour comes not from textbooks but from (a) the free government data (the numbers), (b) a handful of company presentations and quarterlies (how insiders actually frame things), and (c) two or three podcasts (current market color and vocabulary). The textbooks are for the depth that lets you *follow* a technical argument, not for the surface fluency that lets you *start* one. Read accordingly.

## Part 1 — The sequenced study plan

### Phase 0 — Orientation (Week 1)
**Read first, in this order:**
1. USGS *Mineral Commodity Summaries 2025* — the two-page **zinc** and **lithium** chapters. (free) — orients you on production, reserves, and who mines what, with authoritative numbers.
2. IEA *Global Critical Minerals Outlook 2025*, Executive Summary. (free) — the single best free framing of the macro demand/supply narrative that investors reference.
3. One mining-lifecycle primer (Resource Capital Funds "Phases of Mining" or Investing News Network's lifecycle series). (free) — the exploration→closure arc.

**Set up your standing feeds** (see Part 2, §I): Mining.com, The Northern Miner, the Australian *Resources and Energy Quarterly*, and two podcasts (Money of Mine + the Global Lithium Podcast). Start listening now; the vocabulary sinks in passively.

**Checkpoint:** Can you state, from memory, roughly how much zinc and lithium the world mines per year, the top three producing countries for each, and name the exploration→closure stages?

### Phase 1 — Shared foundations (Weeks 2–6)
This is ~40% of the value. Do not skip to the commodity chapters.

**Geology & deposits (Week 2):**
- Robb, *Introduction to Ore-Forming Processes* (2nd ed.) — read Ch. 1 (intro), the hydrothermal chapters, and the sedimentary/basinal chapters. `[Working]` (paid) — gives you the deposit-genesis vocabulary (magmatic vs. hydrothermal vs. sedimentary) that underlies both the zinc SEDEX/MVT/VMS story and lithium pegmatites.
- Free alternative/supplement: USGS deposit-model publications (Cox & Singer; the mineral-deposit-models circulars). (free)

**Reporting codes (Week 3) — read the actual documents, they're short and free:**
- The JORC Code (2012). (free)
- CIM Definition Standards / an NI 43-101 explainer. (free)
- Skim SEC S-K 1300. (free) — so you know the US, Australian, and Canadian codes and the Competent/Qualified Person concept.

**Processing & metallurgy (Weeks 4–5):**
- Wills & Finch, *Wills' Mineral Processing Technology* (8th ed.) — read the introduction, the comminution chapter, and the froth-flotation chapter closely; skim leaching and dewatering. `[Working]`→`[Advanced]` (paid) — the flotation surface-chemistry and comminution energy sections reward a physics reader and are exactly where operators test whether you actually understand processing.
- Free supplement: the 911 Metallurgist website/YouTube for plain-language processing walk-throughs. (free)

**Economics, valuation & investing (Week 6):**
- Rudenno, *The Mining Valuation Handbook* (4th ed.) — read the chapters on resources/reserves, feasibility studies, cost estimation, and valuation/NAV. `[Working]` (paid) — the investor-facing half; this is where AISC, cost curves, NAV, and P/NAV become second nature.

**Checkpoint:** Read one real NI 43-101 or JORC technical-report *summary* and explain cut-off grade, the resource-vs-reserve ladder, and which study stage it is. Separately, build a back-of-envelope AISC and a one-line NAV story for any producer.

### Phase 2 — Zinc deep dive (Weeks 7–9)
- BGS *Mineral Profile: Zinc*. (free) — concise, authoritative on geology, processing, and uses.
- Sinclair, *The Extractive Metallurgy of Zinc* (Spectrum Series Vol. 13, AusIMM) — read Parts A–B and the roast-leach-electrowin chapters. `[Ref]`→`[Working]` **(free PDF from AusIMM)** — the definitive zinc-metallurgy reference, and it's free; this is your edge on the technical/operator side.
- Investor presentations + latest quarterly for **Teck** and **Hindustan Zinc**; skim **Glencore** and **Boliden**. (free) — how the zinc business is actually framed to the market (TC/RCs, byproduct credits, concentrate vs. metal).
- ILZSG and International Zinc Association pages for supply/demand and end-use context. (free/freemium)

**Checkpoint:** Explain TC/RCs and why zinc concentrate was tight in 2024–2026; the RLE route end-to-end; the ~85% payable / ~8-unit-deduction concentrate terms; and why a polymetallic zinc mine's *net* zinc cash cost can be very low.

### Phase 3 — Lithium deep dive (Weeks 10–13)
- USGS *Professional Paper 1802*, lithium chapter (Bradley et al.). (free) — the authoritative free source on lithium deposit types (pegmatite vs. brine vs. clay).
- International Lithium Association (ILiA) primers, including their DLE material. (free)
- IEA *Global Critical Minerals Outlook 2025*, full lithium sections + the Critical Minerals Data Explorer. (free)
- For DLE, read **both** a proponent and a skeptic: ILiA / Lilac Solutions material **and** Wood Mackenzie's "Direct lithium extraction: is the hype justified?" plus a Resources for the Future DLE cost analysis. (mixed free/paid) — so you can argue both sides.
- Benchmark Mineral Intelligence and Fastmarkets free insights + the Global Lithium Podcast (Joe Lowry). (freemium/free) — current pricing structure, spodumene SC6, carbonate vs. hydroxide, and how the market actually talks.
- Investor presentations + latest quarterly for **Pilbara Minerals**, **Albemarle**, and **SQM**; skim **Arcadium/Rio Tinto** and **Ganfeng**. (free)

**Checkpoint:** Argue both sides of DLE; explain the LFP-vs-NMC and carbonate-vs-hydroxide demand linkage; and explain why lithium is priced by reporting agencies rather than a deep exchange.

### Phase 4 — ESG & tailings (Week 14, integrate with both)
- The Global Industry Standard on Tailings Management (GISTM). (free) — skim the 15 principles; know upstream vs. downstream dams and why this is a board-level issue.
- ICMM Mining Principles and, optionally, the IRMA Standard. (free)

**Checkpoint:** Explain what a TSF is, why Brumadinho changed the industry, and what GISTM requires.

### Phase 5 — AI/ML in mining (Weeks 15–16, keep light — plays to your background)
- One or two mineral-prospectivity ML review papers (see §G). (mostly free)
- Rio Tinto "Mine of the Future" and one autonomous-haulage case study; a KoBold Metals write-up. (free)

**Checkpoint:** Name three real, deployed use cases and honestly bound where the hype outruns reality.

## Part 2 — The annotated resource library

### A. General mining engineering & the lifecycle
- **Hartman, H. L. & Mutmansky, J. M.** *Introductory Mining Engineering*, 2nd ed. Wiley, 2002. `[Intro]` (paid) — the standard first textbook; surface and underground methods, the lifecycle, unit operations. Start here if you want one general mining book.
- **Darling, P. (ed.)** *SME Mining Engineering Handbook*, 3rd ed. Society for Mining, Metallurgy & Exploration (SME), 2011. `[Ref]` (paid) — the encyclopedic industry reference (two volumes); dip into specific chapters, don't read cover-to-cover.
- **Hustrulid, W., Kuchta, M. & Martin, R.** *Open Pit Mine Planning and Design*, 3rd ed. CRC Press, 2013. `[Advanced]` (paid) — the reference for open-pit design, strip ratios, and pit optimization; relevant to most large zinc and hard-rock lithium mines.
- **Hartman, H. L. & Bullock, R. L. (eds.)** *SME Mining Reference Handbook* and **Hustrulid & Bullock (eds.)** *Underground Mining Methods*, SME, 2001. `[Ref]` (paid) — for underground methods (stoping, caving) if you go deep on the operator side.
- *Free primers:* Resource Capital Funds "Phases of Mining"; Investing News Network lifecycle series; Prospectors & Developers Association (PDAC) explainers. (free) — good enough for lifecycle vocabulary without buying a textbook.

### B. Mineral processing / beneficiation
- **Wills, B. A. & Finch, J. A.** *Wills' Mineral Processing Technology: An Introduction to the Practical Aspects of Ore Treatment and Mineral Recovery*, 8th ed. Butterworth-Heinemann (Elsevier), 2016. ISBN 978-0-08-097053-0. `[Working]`→`[Advanced]` (paid) — the definitive processing reference for 40+ years; strong on comminution and flotation. **The single most useful technical book for this project.**
- **Gupta, A. & Yan, D. S.** *Mineral Processing Design and Operations: An Introduction*, 2nd ed. Elsevier, 2016. `[Working]` (paid) — more design- and calculation-oriented than Wills; good for a quantitative reader who wants the equations.
- **Napier-Munn, T. J., Morrell, S., Morrison, R. D. & Kojovic, T.** *Mineral Comminution Circuits: Their Operation and Optimisation.* Julius Kruttschnitt Mineral Research Centre (JKMRC), 1996 (repr.). `[Advanced]` (paid) — the deep comminution reference; grinding is the most energy-intensive step and a natural physics entry point.
- **Fuerstenau, M. C. & Han, K. N. (eds.)** *Principles of Mineral Processing.* SME, 2003. `[Ref]` (paid) — strong on the surface chemistry and physics of flotation (contact angle, double layer, collector adsorption).
- **Bulatovic, S. M.** *Handbook of Flotation Reagents: Chemistry, Theory and Practice.* Elsevier, 3 vols, 2007–2015. `[Ref]` (paid) — reference for flotation reagent chemistry, including the differential Pb/Zn flotation relevant to zinc ores.
- *Free:* **911 Metallurgist** (David Michaud) website and YouTube. (free) — practical, plain-language mineral-processing explainers; excellent for building intuition fast.

### C. Economic geology & ore deposits
- **Robb, L.** *Introduction to Ore-Forming Processes*, 2nd ed. Wiley-Blackwell, 2020. ISBN 978-1-119-96750-7. `[Working]` (paid) — the best single modern text on how deposits form; covers magmatic, hydrothermal, and sedimentary systems. Author is an Oxford economic geologist and past SEG president. **Start here for geology.**
- **Ridley, J.** *Ore Deposit Geology.* Cambridge University Press, 2013. `[Working]` (paid) — clear, deposit-type-organized; complements Robb.
- **Pohl, W. L.** *Economic Geology: Principles and Practice.* Wiley-Blackwell, 2011. `[Ref]` (paid) — comprehensive reference bridging geology and the resource industry.
- **Evans, A. M.** *Ore Geology and Industrial Minerals: An Introduction*, 3rd ed. Blackwell Science, 1993. `[Intro]` (paid) — older but very accessible; fine as a cheaper used entry point.
- **Guilbert, J. M. & Park, C. F.** *The Geology of Ore Deposits.* W. H. Freeman, 1986. `[Ref]` (paid) — a dated classic; useful for deposit-type descriptions.
- *Free:* Society of Economic Geologists (SEG) resources and USGS mineral-deposit-model publications (Cox & Singer 1986; the deposit-models circulars). (free) — authoritative deposit-type descriptions including SEDEX, MVT, VMS, and lithium pegmatites/brines.

### D. Extractive metallurgy, hydrometallurgy & refining
- **Habashi, F.** *Handbook of Extractive Metallurgy*, 4 vols. Wiley-VCH, 1997. `[Ref]` (paid) — the comprehensive reference across pyro- and hydrometallurgy.
- **Rosenqvist, T.** *Principles of Extractive Metallurgy*, 2nd ed. Tapir Academic Press, 2004 (orig. McGraw-Hill, 1983). `[Working]` (freemium — often available free as PDF) — thermodynamics-first treatment; good for a physics background.
- **Free, M. L.** *Hydrometallurgy: Fundamentals and Applications.* Wiley/TMS, 2013. `[Advanced]` (paid) — leaching, solvent extraction, and electrowinning fundamentals; directly relevant to zinc RLE and lithium chemical conversion.
- **Gupta, C. K.** *Chemical Metallurgy: Principles and Practice.* Wiley-VCH, 2003. `[Ref]` (paid) — solid reference on the chemistry of extraction.

### E. Mining economics, valuation & investing
- **Rudenno, V.** *The Mining Valuation Handbook: Mining and Energy Valuation for Investors and Management*, 4th ed. Wiley, 2012. ISBN 978-0-730-37707-8. `[Working]` (paid) — **the investor-side bible**: feasibility studies, resources/reserves, cost estimation, commodity forecasting, NAV and valuation, with worked examples. Author holds a mining-engineering degree and a PhD in mining economics.
- **Runge, I. C.** *Mining Economics and Strategy.* SME, 1998. `[Working]` (paid) — cost structures, cut-off-grade theory, and strategy; more conceptual than Rudenno.
- **Torries, T. F.** *Evaluating Mineral Projects: Applications and Misconceptions.* SME, 1998. `[Working]` (paid) — DCF, real options, and common valuation errors; short and sharp.
- **Wellmer, F.-W., Dalheimer, M. & Wagner, M.** *Economic Evaluations in Exploration*, 2nd ed. Springer, 2008. `[Working]` (paid) — how exploration and resource economics tie together.
- *Free / freemium:* broker "mining 101" and commodity primers (RBC, BMO, Canaccord, Macquarie — occasionally public); Crux Investor's free "how to value a mining company" explainers; CFA-style DCF/NAV tutorials. (freemium) — practical, current framing of NAV, P/NAV, and cost curves.

### F. Resource/reserve reporting codes & standards (all free — read the primary documents)
- **JORC Code (2012).** jorc.org. (free) — Australasian standard; the "Competent Person" regime.
- **CIM Definition Standards** and **NI 43-101** (Canadian). (free) — the "Qualified Person" regime; NI 43-101 technical reports are the documents you'll actually read.
- **SEC Regulation S-K, subpart 1300 (S-K 1300).** (free) — the US standard (since 2021).
- **SAMREC** (South Africa), **PERC** (Europe), and the **CRIRSCO International Reporting Template.** (free) — the family these codes belong to.
- **VALMIN Code.** (free) — the companion code for *valuing* mineral assets, useful on the investor side.

### G. ESG, tailings & responsible mining (mostly free)
- **Global Industry Standard on Tailings Management (GISTM), 2020.** globaltailingsreview.org / ICMM. (free) — 15 principles, 77 requirements; the post-Brumadinho standard.
- **ICMM Mining Principles** and performance expectations. (free)
- **IRMA Standard for Responsible Mining.** responsiblemining.net. (free) — the most demanding third-party responsible-mining standard.
- **Global Tailings Portal** (Grid-Arendal / investor-led disclosure). (free) — searchable data on individual tailings facilities.

### H. Zinc-specific resources
- **Sinclair, R. J.** *The Extractive Metallurgy of Zinc.* Spectrum Series Vol. 13, The Australasian Institute of Mining and Metallurgy (AusIMM), 2005, 297 pp. `[Ref]`→`[Working]` **(free PDF from AusIMM)** — the definitive zinc-metallurgy text: roasting, leaching, iron/jarosite residue handling, purification, electrowinning, and the ISP. Free and authoritative — a genuine edge for the operator side.
- **British Geological Survey, *Mineral Profile: Zinc*.** BGS. (free) — concise authoritative primer on geology, processing, and uses.
- **International Lead and Zinc Study Group (ILZSG).** ilzsg.org. (freemium) — the intergovernmental body for supply/demand statistics and forecasts; the "refined metal deficit/surplus" numbers insiders quote.
- **International Zinc Association (IZA).** zinc.org. (free) — galvanizing and end-use technical resources; useful for the demand side.
- **London Metal Exchange — zinc contract specs and education.** lme.com. (free) — contract size, delivery, and warehousing basics.
- **USGS zinc commodity page + annual MCS zinc chapter.** (free) — production, reserves, and price history.
- *Company reporting (free):* Teck, Glencore, Hindustan Zinc (Vedanta), Boliden, Nexa Resources, Korea Zinc, MMG, and Nyrstar/Trafigura — investor presentations, quarterlies, and technical reports. The best free source for how the zinc business is actually framed.

### I. Lithium-specific resources
- **USGS Professional Paper 1802**, "Critical Mineral Resources of the United States" (Schulz, DeYoung, Seal & Bradley, eds., 2017) — the **lithium chapter** (Bradley, Stillings, Jaskula, Munk & McCauley). (free) — the authoritative free reference on lithium deposit types.
- **International Lithium Association (ILiA).** ilia.org. (free) — industry primers on resources, processing, and Direct Lithium Extraction.
- **IEA *Global Critical Minerals Outlook 2025*** and the **Critical Minerals Data Explorer.** iea.org, published May 2025, CC BY 4.0. (free) — the most-cited free demand/supply outlook; lithium demand projected to grow ~5x by 2040 in the STEPS scenario, near-term well-supplied.
- **Chagnes, A. & Świątowska, J. (eds.)** *Lithium Process Chemistry: Resources, Extraction, Batteries, and Recycling.* Elsevier, 2015. `[Advanced]` (paid) — a rare proper reference on lithium extraction and process chemistry.
- **Benchmark Mineral Intelligence.** benchmarkminerals.com. (freemium) — the leading lithium/battery price-reporting agency; free articles, podcast, and YouTube; paid assessments. Its SC6 spodumene and battery-grade carbonate/hydroxide prices are the market references.
- **Fastmarkets.** fastmarkets.com. (freemium) — the other major lithium price-reporting agency; strong free "insights" pieces on pricing mechanics (spot vs. contract, CIF CJK basis).
- **Direct Lithium Extraction — read both sides:** ILiA's DLE primer and Lilac Solutions material (proponent) vs. **Wood Mackenzie**, "Direct lithium extraction: is the hype justified by the reality?" (2024) and **Resources for the Future** DLE cost analyses (skeptic). (mixed) — for balanced command of the hottest contested topic.
- **The Faraday Institution** resources and **Argonne National Laboratory** (Linda Gaines et al.) on batteries and recycling. (free) — for the NMC-vs-LFP and carbonate-vs-hydroxide battery linkage.
- *Company reporting (free):* Albemarle, SQM, Pilbara Minerals, Arcadium Lithium (now Rio Tinto), Ganfeng, Tianqi, Mineral Resources, Sigma Lithium — presentations and quarterlies.

### J. AI / machine learning in mining (keep light)
- **Mineral prospectivity mapping reviews:** survey papers in *Ore Geology Reviews* and *Natural Resources Research* on ML for prospectivity (e.g., random forests / gradient boosting / neural nets on geophysical–geochemical layers). (mostly free abstracts; some OA) — enough to reference the methods credibly. This is directly adjacent to your tree-model / feature-importance background.
- **Company case studies (free):** Rio Tinto "Mine of the Future" (autonomous haulage, AutoHaul rail, the Centre for Mine Automation); BHP and Fortescue autonomy programs; KoBold Metals write-ups (ML-driven exploration, the Mingomba discovery). (free)
- **Vendors to know by name:** KoBold Metals, Earth AI, GoldSpot Discoveries, Minerva Intelligence, SenseOre (exploration); Caterpillar MineStar and Komatsu (autonomy); Motion Metrics/Weir and IMDEX (sensing/geodata). — name-recognition, not deep study.
- **Conference tracks:** SME Annual, AusIMM, and the "AI in Mining"/data-science tracks now standard at both. (freemium) — where the current practitioner conversation happens.
- *Reality check:* the academic literature here is thin and fast-moving; treat vendor claims skeptically. Your ML credibility is an asset, but only when paired with understanding of the physical process it's applied to.

### K. Free online courses, MOOCs & lecture material
- **911 Metallurgist** (YouTube + site). (free) — practical mineral-processing and metallurgy explainers; the fastest way to build processing intuition.
- **Benchmark Mineral Intelligence** and **the Faraday Institution** (YouTube/webinars). (free) — lithium/battery supply-chain lectures.

## Part 3 — The minimum-viable fast track (~4–6 weeks)
If you can't do the full program, these give the most conversational credibility per hour. Everything here is free except two books you can read selectively (borrow or skim).

**Week 1 — the numbers and the macro story (all free):**
- USGS MCS 2025 zinc + lithium chapters.
- IEA *Global Critical Minerals Outlook 2025* executive summary + the lithium sections.
- Start Money of Mine + the Global Lithium Podcast (keep them running throughout).

**Weeks 2–3 — the vocabulary (mix free + selective book reading):**
- Rudenno, *Mining Valuation Handbook* — resources/reserves, cost, and valuation chapters only (investor vocabulary).
- Wills & Finch — intro + flotation chapter only (processing vocabulary).
- Skim the JORC Code and one NI 43-101 technical-report summary (free).

**Weeks 4–5 — the two commodities in the market's own words (all free):**
- Zinc: BGS Zinc profile + Teck's latest investor presentation and quarterly. Skim Sinclair (free AusIMM PDF) for the RLE route.
- Lithium: ILiA primers + USGS PP 1802 lithium chapter + Pilbara Minerals' and Albemarle's latest presentations and quarterlies. Read one DLE bull and one DLE bear.

**Week 6 — consolidate:**
- Build the ~50-term glossary from the main curriculum's appendix.
- Do the "read a technical report + a quarterly" exercise below.

**The single highest-leverage habit:** track the current LME zinc price and the current Benchmark/Fastmarkets lithium carbonate/hydroxide and SC6 assessments every week. Being current on price is the fastest tell of an insider — and being out of date is the fastest tell of a tourist.

## Part 4 — Calibration on the resources themselves
- **Free and authoritative — lead with these:** USGS (MCS + PP 1802), BGS profiles, IEA outlook, the reporting codes, GISTM, and the AusIMM Sinclair zinc PDF. These are primary or quasi-primary and carry no promotional bias.
- **Current (2024–2026) vs. older-but-canonical:** market structure, prices, and the company landscape must come from *current* sources (company filings, price-reporting agencies, IEA 2025, podcasts) — they go stale fast. The textbooks (Wills & Finch 2016, Robb 2020, Rudenno 2012, Sinclair 2005, Habashi 1997) are canonical for *fundamentals* that don't change; do not rely on any of them for current prices, costs, or corporate facts.
- **Price-reporting agencies (Benchmark, Fastmarkets) are the lithium "exchange."** Because lithium lacks a deep exchange contract, these agencies' assessments *are* the reference prices. Free tiers give you the narrative; the actual numbers sit behind paid data. Know what each index specifies (grade, basis such as CIF China-Japan-Korea vs. FOB Australia).
- **Company and vendor material is useful but not neutral.** Investor presentations are the best free framing of how the business works, but they are marketing — cross-check any hard claim against USGS/BGS/filings. The same applies doubly to DLE vendor material and AI-vendor claims; always pair a proponent source with a skeptic.
- **Podcasts are for vocabulary and color, not facts.** They're the fastest way to learn how insiders actually talk and what they're arguing about this month — but verify specific numbers elsewhere.
- **Journals are optional.** Unless you go deep on a specific technical point (a flotation mechanism, a DLE sorbent, a prospectivity-ML method), you don't need the primary literature to hold a credible conversation.

## Part 5 — Practical: how to read the two documents that build the most credibility
Nothing signals insider familiarity faster than having actually read the primary documents insiders reference. Do this once for a zinc name and once for a lithium name.

**A technical report (NI 43-101 or JORC/DFS):** read for (1) the resource/reserve statement — tonnes, grade, category (inferred/indicated/measured; probable/proven) and the cut-off grade used; (2) the study stage (PEA/PFS/DFS) and therefore the confidence level; (3) the mining method and strip ratio or underground method; (4) the processing flowsheet and expected recovery; (5) the capex, opex, AISC, and the headline NAV/IRR with the assumed commodity price and discount rate; and (6) the red flags — a "resource" quoted with no reserves, an economic case that only works at a price well above spot, or heavy reliance on inferred material. Suggested pair: a **Teck** (zinc) and a **Pilbara Minerals** (lithium) document.

**A quarterly / results release:** read for production vs. guidance, unit costs (C1/AISC) and their trend, realized price vs. benchmark, and management's commentary on the market. This is where you pick up the current framing — for zinc, TC/RCs and concentrate availability; for lithium, spodumene SC6 pricing, carbonate-vs-hydroxide mix, and inventory. Doing this for one zinc and one lithium producer per quarter keeps you permanently current.
