# English Grammar Learning Plan

## Overview

A structured progression from accessible theoretical foundations to comprehensive reference mastery. Estimated total: **12–16 weeks** at 5–8 hours/week.

---

## Phase 1: Foundation (Weeks 1–6)

### Primary Text
**A Student's Introduction to English Grammar**  
*Huddleston, Pullum & Reynolds* (2nd ed., Cambridge, 2022) — 400 pages

**Why start here:**
- Designed for progressive chapter-by-chapter reading
- Uses the same rigorous framework as the full Cambridge Grammar
- Covers all core areas: word classes, phrases, clauses, tense/aspect, morphology
- Chapters map directly to the reference grammar for later deep dives

**Chapter progression:**
1. Introduction & grammatical concepts
2. Word classes (parts of speech)
3. Verbs, tense, and aspect ← *covers your tense requirement*
4. Clause structure
5. Nouns and noun phrases
6. Adjectives and adverbs
7. Prepositions and preposition phrases
8. Negation and related phenomena
9. Clause types (interrogatives, imperatives, exclamatives)
10. Subordination and content clauses
11. Relative clauses
12. Adjuncts (new in 2nd edition)
13. Coordination
14. Information packaging

**Estimated time:** ~25–30 hours reading

---

## Phase 2: Reference Depth (Weeks 7–12)

### Primary Reference
**The Cambridge Grammar of the English Language**  
*Huddleston & Pullum* (Cambridge, 2002) — 1,842 pages

**How to use it:**
Not cover-to-cover reading. After Phase 1, you'll have the framework to use this as targeted deep reference. Prioritise chapters based on interest:

**Recommended reading order for key topics:**

| Priority | Chapter | Topic | Pages |
|----------|---------|-------|-------|
| 1 | Ch. 3 | The verb (tense, aspect, mood) | ~120 pp |
| 2 | Ch. 4 | The clause: complements | ~80 pp |
| 3 | Ch. 5 | Nouns and noun phrases | ~100 pp |
| 4 | Ch. 19 | Lexical word-formation (morphology) | ~50 pp |
| 5 | Ch. 2 | Syntactic overview | ~50 pp |
| 6 | Ch. 8 | The clause: adjuncts | ~80 pp |
| 7 | Ch. 11 | Relative clauses | ~50 pp |
| 8 | Ch. 9 | Negation | ~40 pp |

**Estimated time for priority chapters:** ~40–50 hours

---

## Phase 3: Empirical Complement (Weeks 13–16, Optional)

### Supplementary Text
**Longman Grammar of Spoken and Written English**  
*Biber, Johansson, Leech, Conrad & Finegan* (Pearson, 1999) — 1,204 pages

**Why add this:**
- Corpus-based frequency data the Cambridge Grammar lacks
- Shows how grammatical features distribute across registers (conversation, academic, news, fiction)
- Uses more traditional terminology — useful for comparing frameworks

**Selective reading — focus on:**
- Ch. 6: Variation in the verb phrase (tense/aspect frequencies)
- Ch. 9: Complex sentences
- Ch. 14: The grammar of conversation

**Estimated time:** ~15–20 hours selective reading

## Alternative Track: Traditional Terminology

If you find the Cambridge Grammar's departures from traditional terminology frustrating (e.g., their treatment of subordinating conjunctions as prepositions), consider this parallel:

| Instead of... | Use... |
|---------------|--------|
| *A Student's Introduction* | **A Student's Grammar of the English Language** (Greenbaum & Quirk, 1990) — 490 pp |
| *Cambridge Grammar* | **A Comprehensive Grammar of the English Language** (Quirk et al., 1985) — 1,779 pp |

Same structure, more familiar terminology, slightly dated but still authoritative.

---

## Quick Reference Additions (Optional)

For quick lookups alongside main study:

- **Oxford Modern English Grammar** (Aarts, 2011) — 375 pp  
  Shorter, bridges traditional and modern analysis

- **The Linguistic Structure of Modern English** (Brinton & Brinton, 2010) — 426 pp  
  If you want phonology/semantics context beyond pure grammar

---

## Summary: Minimum Viable Path

If time is limited, this sequence gives comprehensive theoretical coverage:

```
1. A Student's Introduction to English Grammar (2022)
   └── Read cover-to-cover: ~25–30 hours

2. The Cambridge Grammar of the English Language (2002)
   └── Ch. 3 (Verb/Tense) + Ch. 19 (Morphology) + Ch. 4–5 (Clauses/NPs)
   └── Targeted reading: ~30 hours

Total: ~55–60 hours for solid theoretical grounding
```
