# Research Dossier: A Graduate Curriculum on Military Strategy and Its Evolution

## TL;DR
- The field has a stable canonical spine — the two indispensable anchor surveys are Hal Brands (ed.), *The New Makers of Modern Strategy* (Princeton, 2023, 1,200 pp.) and Lawrence Freedman, *Strategy: A History* (Oxford, 2013) — and edition/translation choices matter enormously (Howard & Paret for Clausewitz; Griffith or Sawyer for Sun Tzu; Strassler's *Landmark Thucydides*; Olivelle for Kautilya).
- Roughly half the canon is genuinely SETTLED (primary texts, edition choices, campaign facts), while the interpretive layer is heavily CONTESTED (Clausewitz vs. Jomini, Liddell Hart's "indirect approach" distortion of Clausewitz, the Blitzkrieg-as-doctrine myth, the RMA debate, "new wars," strategic-bombing effectiveness) and several modern strands require HYPE-WATCH tags (Gerasimov "doctrine," cyber war, autonomous-weapons/AI).
- The quantitative layer should be taught for conceptual literacy, not predictive faith: Lanchester's laws are elegant but empirically unvalidated for real combat; Schelling's game theory is foundational; OR/wargaming history (Blackett, RAND, Kriegsspiel, Monte Carlo) is settled and richly documented.

## Key Findings

**The curriculum has a natural chronological-plus-thematic architecture.** Historical and modern strategy can be given equal weight across ~10–12 phases, anchored by two survey spines and populated with primary texts in their best editions.

**Edition and translation choices are not cosmetic — they carry interpretive weight.** The Howard/Paret Clausewitz is the standard but stresses readability over fidelity; the Griffith Sun Tzu is the professional-military favorite while Sawyer offers the best historical apparatus; the *Landmark* series transforms Thucydides for students; Olivelle's Arthashastra "clearly supplants" all prior English translations. Getting these right is a core deliverable.

**The two "Makers of Modern Strategy" editions are complementary, not redundant.** The 1986 Paret edition remains superb on Machiavelli-to-nuclear-age European strategy; the 2023 Brands edition expands to the ancient world, non-Western strategy, and the digital age. A curriculum should use both.

**The modern strands demand explicit calibration tags** because much of the popular literature is hype-driven (cyber war, AI, "Gerasimov doctrine") or historiographically contested (RMA, new wars, Blitzkrieg).

## Details by Strand

### A. Anchor Surveys and Curriculum Spines (SETTLED as canon; each has bias critiques)

- **Hal Brands (ed.), *The New Makers of Modern Strategy: From the Ancient World to the Digital Age* (Princeton University Press, 2023).** 1,200 pages (ISBN 9780691204383); the Wall Street Journal's Dominic Green noted Brands "gathers a college of 45 such experts," and Brands has recounted that his editor warned that beyond 1,199 pages the spine would literally fail. Expands the franchise from the ancient world (Sun Tzu, Thucydides, Alexander) through the digital age (cyber, AI). Lawrence Freedman is the only scholar to contribute to both the 1986 and 2023 editions ("Strategy: A History of an Idea"). Includes Hew Strachan's essay arguing that the Howard translation of Clausewitz distorted his argument about war and policy. New topics: Tecumseh, Kabila/Congo, 17th-century strategies of equilibrium, revolutions in military affairs. **Best single modern spine.**
- **Peter Paret (ed.), *Makers of Modern Strategy from Machiavelli to the Nuclear Age* (Princeton, 1986).** The prior standard; contained twenty-two wholly new essays, with three reprinted from the 1943 Earle edition and four extensively revised. Deeper on Cold War / nuclear-age European strategy than the 2023 volume. Follows the original Edward Mead Earle (ed.), *Makers of Modern Strategy: Military Thought from Machiavelli to Hitler* (1943). Use for its classic essays (e.g., Palmer on Frederick/Guibert/Bülow).
- **Lawrence Freedman, *Strategy: A History* (Oxford University Press, 31 Oct 2013).** xvi + 751 pp. (OUP hardback listed as 768 pp., ISBN 9780199325153); named a Financial Times Best Book of 2013 and winner of the Political Studies Association's W.J.M. Mackenzie Book Prize. Encyclopedic; ranges from primate conflict and Homer through Sun Tzu, Machiavelli, Jomini/Clausewitz, Marx, corporate and social-science strategy. Best narrative spine for the *idea* of strategy.
- **Beatrice Heuser, *The Evolution of Strategy: Thinking War from Antiquity to the Present* (Cambridge, 2010)**; and **Heuser, *The Strategy Makers: Thoughts on War and Society from Machiavelli to Clausewitz* (Praeger, 2010)**; plus **Heuser, *Reading Clausewitz* (Pimlico, 2002).** Strong corrective to Anglo-American Clausewitz readings; less Eurocentric than most.
- **Azar Gat, *A History of Military Thought: From the Enlightenment to the Cold War* (Oxford, 2001)** — collects his earlier *The Origins of Military Thought: From the Enlightenment to Clausewitz* (Clarendon, 1989) and *The Development of Military Thought: The Nineteenth Century* (Clarendon, 1992). The deepest intellectual history of the theorists.
- **Antulio J. Echevarria II, *Military Strategy: A Very Short Introduction* (Oxford, 2017)** — ideal MVP fast-path primer.
- Other spine candidates surfaced across professional reading lists: **Geoffrey Parker (ed.), *The Cambridge History of Warfare*; Michael Howard, *War in European History*; Archer Jones, *The Art of War in the Western World*; John Keegan, *The Face of Battle* and *A History of Warfare*; Williamson Murray, MacGregor Knox & Alvin Bernstein (eds.), *The Making of Strategy: Rulers, States, and War* (Cambridge, 1994); Hew Strachan's essays.** *Bias note:* most of these are critiqued for Eurocentrism/"Western way of war" framing (a debate crystallized by Victor Davis Hanson's thesis and its critics); the 2023 Brands volume and Heuser partially correct this.

### B. Canonical Theory Across Eras — Primary Texts and Best Editions

**Ancient / Classical**
- **Sun Tzu, *The Art of War* (c. 5th–3rd c. BCE).** Translation guide: **Samuel B. Griffith (Oxford, 1963)** — the professional-military favorite, UNESCO series, translator was a USMC brigadier general; **Ralph D. Sawyer (Westview/Basic, 1994)** — richest historical context and para-text; **Roger T. Ames (Ballantine, 1993)** — based on the Yinqueshan bamboo-slip archaeological texts, philologically excellent; **John Minford (Viking/Penguin, 2002)** — most literary but interprets through Western discourse; **Lionel Giles (1910)** — public-domain scholarly workhorse. Recommendation: Griffith as core; Sawyer or Ames as scholarly companion.
- **Thucydides, *History of the Peloponnesian War* (c. 400 BCE).** Best teaching edition: **Robert B. Strassler (ed.), *The Landmark Thucydides* (Free Press, 1996)** — revised Richard Crawley translation with maps, marginal notes, appendices; caveat that Crawley base is Victorian (1874) and can read densely. Alternative modern translation: **Jeremy Mynott (Cambridge, 2013).** Focus readings: Melian Dialogue, Pericles' Funeral Oration, Sicilian Expedition.
- **Kautilya, *Arthashastra* (c. 3rd c. BCE–2nd c. CE).** Best scholarly edition: **Patrick Olivelle, *King, Governance, and Law in Ancient India: Kauṭilya's Arthaśāstra* (Oxford, 2013)** — H-Net's reviewer states it "clearly supplants all other translations of this work into English, including those of Kangle (1977) and L. N. Rangarajan (1992)." Accessible alternative: **L. N. Rangarajan (Penguin, 1992)** (topically rearranged; "adequate but unsatisfactory" per scholars). Olivelle also has a shorter Hackett Classics selection.
- **Vegetius, *Epitoma rei militaris* / *De re militari* (c. 400 CE).** Standard scholarly translation: **N. P. Milner, *Vegetius: Epitome of Military Science*, 2nd rev. ed. (Liverpool University Press, Translated Texts for Historians vol. 16, 1996; 1st ed. 1993).**

**Byzantine**
- **Maurice's *Strategikon* (c. 600 CE).** Standard translation: **George T. Dennis, *Maurice's Strategikon: Handbook of Byzantine Military Strategy* (University of Pennsylvania Press, 1984)** — now reissued with a foreword by Conor Whately (2023). In print.
- **Leo VI, *The Taktika* (c. 900 CE).** Standard: **George T. Dennis (text, translation, commentary), *The Taktika of Leo VI* (Dumbarton Oaks Texts 12, 2010; revised paperback edition later).** Facing Greek/English; companion volume John Haldon, *A Critical Commentary on the Taktika of Leo VI.*

**Renaissance / Early Modern**
- **Niccolò Machiavelli, *The Art of War* (1521)** and the military chapters of ***The Prince* (1513/1532)** and **Discourses on Livy**. Best translation of *Art of War*: **Christopher Lynch (University of Chicago Press, 2003)** — the authoritative modern scholarly English translation with interpretive essay.
- **Maurice de Saxe, *Mes Rêveries* (*Reveries on the Art of War*, 1732/publ. 1757).** Translation by Thomas R. Phillips (a standard).
- **Frederick the Great** — *Instructions to His Generals* (*Die General-Principia vom Kriege*, 1748); read via Jay Luvaas, *Frederick the Great on the Art of War.*
- **Comte de Guibert, *Essai général de tactique* (1772).** Read via Palmer's *Makers* essay; select translated excerpts.

**The Napoleonic Transformation and the Central Dichotomy**
- **Carl von Clausewitz, *Vom Kriege* / *On War* (posthumous, 1832).** Best/standard English edition: **Michael Howard & Peter Paret (eds./trans.), *On War* (Princeton University Press, 1976; rev. 1984)** — includes Paret's genesis essay, Howard's influence essay, and Bernard Brodie's commentary. Caveats to flag: (1) it "stresses readability at the cost of fidelity" and the trinity/pendulum passage is rendered clumsily (per Christopher Bassford / clausewitzstudies.org); (2) the **O. J. Matthijs Jolles (1943)** translation is more literal/accurate — serious students should cross-check; (3) public-domain **Graham (1873)/Maude (1908)** on Project Gutenberg. Best abridgment discussion: avoid the **Anatol Rapoport Penguin (1968)** abridgment (omits Books 5–7 entirely; Howard was "scathing"). Companion: **Hew Strachan, *Clausewitz's On War: A Biography* (Atlantic, 2007)** and **Michael Howard, *Clausewitz: A Very Short Introduction* (Oxford, 1983).**
- **Antoine-Henri Jomini, *Précis de l'art de la guerre* / *The Art of War* (1838).** Standard English: **G. H. Mendell & W. P. Craighill (1862; Lippincott)** — public domain (Project Gutenberg #13549, archive.org). Frame as the Clausewitz "rival": geometry, lines of operation, decisive points, "principles" vs. Clausewitz's dialectical, political conception.

**19th–Early 20th Century**
- **Ardant du Picq, *Études sur le combat* / *Battle Studies* (posthumous, 1880).** Human/moral factors in combat.
- **Sea power (the classic contrast):** **Alfred Thayer Mahan, *The Influence of Sea Power upon History, 1660–1783* (1890)** (public domain) — decisive battle, command of the sea, sea power as national greatness; also *Naval Strategy Compared and Contrasted* (1911). **Julian S. Corbett, *Some Principles of Maritime Strategy* (1911; Naval Institute Press reprint, 1988)** — Clausewitzian, limited war, maritime communications, combined operations. Teach as complementary rather than strictly opposed (Mahan = grand-strategic/political level and sea power as national greatness; Corbett = operational/tactical, limited war, and the defensive value of the "fleet in being" and command of the sea "in various states and degrees").
- **Air power:** **Giulio Douhet, *Il dominio dell'aria* / *The Command of the Air* (1921; English trans. Dino Ferrari, 1942).** Note: the English "Command of the Air" volume actually bundles five Douhet works (1921 ed., 1927 2nd ed., 1928 *Probable Aspects of Future War*, 1929 *Recapitulation*, 1930 *The War of 19—*). In-print editions: **free Air University Press reprint (2019)** and **University of Alabama Press (2009, Fire Ant Books).** **William "Billy" Mitchell, *Winged Defense: The Development and Possibilities of Modern Air Power—Economic and Military* (G. P. Putnam's Sons, 1925)** (Dover and Alabama reprints). **Hugh Trenchard** wrote no treatise — his doctrine survives in official memoranda (1919 RAF permanent-organization memorandum; 1928 Air Staff "War Object of an Air Force" memorandum). Attach the strategic-bombing effectiveness debate here (CONTESTED).

**Interwar Mechanization & the Indirect Approach**
- **J. F. C. Fuller** — *The Foundations of the Science of War* (1926); *Plan 1919* (armored deep-strike plan). 
- **B. H. Liddell Hart, *Strategy: The Indirect Approach* (1954; expanded from *The Decisive Wars of History*, 1929; retitled editions through 1967).** Teach WITH its central CONTESTED critique — Liddell Hart caricatured Clausewitz as "the Mahdi of mass and mutual massacre" and was accused of (a) distorting Clausewitz and (b) prompting captured German generals to overstate his paternity of Blitzkrieg, plus promoting the "Rommel myth" and "clean Wehrmacht" narrative.

**Soviet / Operational Art**
- **Aleksandr Svechin, *Strategy* (1927; English ed. Kent Lee, ed., East View, 1992, with Kokoshin/Larionov/Kipp essays)** — the "Soviet Clausewitz," coined operational art as the level between tactics and strategy, favored attrition/defense-in-depth.
- **Mikhail Tukhachevsky** ("Red Bonaparte"), **Vladimir Triandafillov**, and **Georgii Isserson, *The Evolution of Operational Art* (1937; U.S. Army Press translation)** — deep battle / deep operations. Secondary anchor: **David M. Glantz, *Soviet Military Operational Art: In Pursuit of Deep Battle* (Frank Cass, 1991)**; **Andrei Kokoshin, *Soviet Strategic Thought, 1917–91* (MIT, 1998).** Note the Svechin–Tukhachevsky debate: attrition/protracted war vs. decisive destruction.
- **Heinz Guderian, *Achtung–Panzer!* (1937)** and *Panzer Leader* (memoir).

**Revolutionary / Insurgency / Maneuver**
- **Mao Zedong, *On Guerrilla Warfare* (1937)** and *On Protracted War* (1938) — best via **Samuel B. Griffith translation.**
- **David Galula, *Counterinsurgency Warfare: Theory and Practice* (Praeger, 1964; PSI reprint with John Nagl foreword)** — the population-centric COIN classic; eight-step method; "the fish swimming in the water." **David Kilcullen, *The Accidental Guerrilla* (Oxford, 2009)** and *Counterinsurgency* (2010). Companions: Roger Trinquier, *Modern Warfare*; the U.S. Army/USMC **FM 3-24 *Counterinsurgency* (2006)** (Petraeus). Attach the COIN-efficacy debate (CONTESTED/OPEN).
- **John Boyd** — never published a book; primary artifacts are his briefings **"Patterns of Conflict" (first presented 1976)**, "A Discourse on Winning and Losing," and the **OODA loop.** Read via **Frans Osinga, *Science, Strategy and War: The Strategic Theory of John Boyd* (Routledge, 2007)** and Grant Hammond, *The Mind of War.* Frame OODA as a cybernetic/feedback control concept.

**Nuclear Theorists**
- **Bernard Brodie (ed.), *The Absolute Weapon* (1946)** and *Strategy in the Missile Age* (1959).
- **Thomas C. Schelling, *The Strategy of Conflict* (Harvard, 1960)** and ***Arms and Influence* (Yale, 1966)** — foundational; focal points ("Schelling points"), credible commitment, brinkmanship, deterrence vs. compellence, "threats that leave something to chance." Nobel Memorial Prize in Economics 2005. (Schelling & Halperin, *Strategy and Arms Control*, 1961.) Nobel laureate Roger Myerson called *Strategy of Conflict* one of the most important books in social theory.
- **Herman Kahn, *On Thermonuclear War* (1960)** and *On Escalation* (1965; 44-rung escalation ladder).
- **Albert Wohlstetter, "The Delicate Balance of Terror" (*Foreign Affairs*, 1959).**
- **Glenn Snyder, *Deterrence and Defense* (1961)** — deterrence-by-denial vs. by-punishment; the stability–instability paradox.
- Attach nuclear-deterrence rationality debate (CONTESTED).

**Contemporary Theorists**
- **Edward Luttwak, *Strategy: The Logic of War and Peace* (Harvard, 1987)** — paradoxical logic; and *The Grand Strategy of the Roman Empire* (1976).
- **Martin van Creveld, *Supplying War: Logistics from Wallenstein to Patton* (Cambridge, 1977)**; *The Transformation of War* (1991); *Command in War* (1985).
- **Colin S. Gray, *Modern Strategy* (Oxford, 1999)** and *The Strategy Bridge* (2010).
- **Lawrence Freedman, *The Evolution of Nuclear Strategy* (1981; 3rd ed. 2003)** and *The Future of War: A History* (2017).
- **Mary Kaldor, *New and Old Wars* (1999)** — the "new wars" thesis (CONTESTED; critics: Stathis Kalyvas, Edward Newman).
- **Rupert Smith, *The Utility of Force: The Art of War in the Modern World* (2005)** — "war amongst the people."
- **John Arquilla & David Ronfeldt, *Networks and Netwars* (RAND, 2001)** and "Cyberwar Is Coming!" (1993); swarming.

### C. Grand Strategy & Economics/Politics of War
- **Paul Kennedy, *The Rise and Fall of the Great Powers* (Random House, 1987; 677 pp.)** — economic base determines military power; "imperial overstretch"; won the Wolfson History Prize 1988. Also **Kennedy (ed.), *Grand Strategies in War and Peace* (Yale, 1991).**
- **John Lewis Gaddis, *On Grand Strategy* (Penguin, 2018)** — the fox/hedgehog framing; reads Clausewitz critically. Also *Strategies of Containment* (1982; rev. 2005).
- **Liddell Hart** on grand strategy (final chapters of *Strategy*).
- **Barry Posen, *The Sources of Military Doctrine* (1984)** and *Restraint: A New Foundation for U.S. Grand Strategy* (Cornell, 2014).
- **Hal Brands, *What Good Is Grand Strategy?* (Cornell, 2014).**
- Logistics-as-strategy anchor: van Creveld, *Supplying War* (above).

### D. Operational Art / Campaign Case Studies (illustrating evolution)
Cannae & battles of annihilation (→ Hans Delbrück, *History of the Art of War*; Schlieffen's Cannae obsession); the Mongol system; Napoleonic campaigns (Austerlitz, 1812); the American Civil War; **Franco-Prussian War & the Prussian/German General Staff (Moltke the Elder)** — anchor: **Robert M. Citino, *The German Way of War* (2005)** and *The Path to Blitzkrieg* (1999); WWI attrition & operational stalemate; **Blitzkrieg / Fall Gelb 1940** — anchor: **Karl-Heinz Frieser, *The Blitzkrieg Legend* (Naval Institute Press, 2005)**, central to the "Blitzkrieg-as-doctrine myth" debate (CONTESTED — Frieser argues 1940 was an improvised operational gamble, not a pre-baked doctrine); Eastern Front deep operations (Glantz); Pacific naval war (Inchon; Midway); Vietnam; **1973 Yom Kippur War**; **Desert Storm 1991 & the RMA**; Iraq/Afghanistan COIN; 2006 Lebanon War; **Russo-Ukrainian War 2014–present** (drones, loitering munitions, precision strike, reconnaissance-strike complex realized).

### E. Modern & Contemporary Strategy (calibration-tag heavy)
- **Nuclear revolution / deterrence / limited war** — SETTLED as theory, CONTESTED on rationality (Section B nuclear theorists; Robert Jervis, *The Meaning of the Nuclear Revolution*, 1989).
- **Revolution in Military Affairs (RMA)** — CONTESTED. Primary: **Cebrowski & Garstka, "Network-Centric Warfare: Its Origin and Future," *U.S. Naval Institute Proceedings* (Jan. 1998)**; Alberts, Garstka & Stein, *Network Centric Warfare* (CCRP, 1999). Skeptics: **Stephen Biddle, *Military Power* (2004)**; critiques that NCW is "evolution not revolution."
- **Precision-guided munitions & reconnaissance-strike complex** — origin in Soviet Marshal Ogarkov's writings (1980s); realized in 1991 and again in Ukraine.
- **Hybrid / gray-zone conflict & the "Gerasimov doctrine"** — HYPE-WATCH / CONTESTED. Primary: Gerasimov's speech reprinted February 2013 in the Military-Industrial Courier (*Voyenno-Promyshlennyy Kurier*), which stated a "perfectly thriving state can, in a matter of months and even days, be transformed into an arena of fierce armed conflict." **Mark Galeotti coined "Gerasimov doctrine" on his blog (2014) and publicly retracted it** in *Foreign Policy* (5 Mar. 2018): "I'm sorry for creating the 'Gerasimov doctrine'... There's one small problem. It doesn't exist." He noted the term was "a placeholder" and "it certainly isn't a doctrine," and that the translated Gerasimov speech was actually the work of Robert Coalson of Radio Free Europe/Radio Liberty. See also his "The mythical 'Gerasimov Doctrine' and the language of threat," *Critical Studies on Security* (2018). The concept is widely judged a Western misreading; Gerasimov was *describing* what he saw as Western hybrid war, not proclaiming Russian doctrine. Companion: **Frank Hoffman, *Conflict in the 21st Century: The Rise of Hybrid Wars* (2007).**
- **Cyber conflict** — HYPE-WATCH. **Thomas Rid, *Cyber War Will Not Take Place* (Hurst/Oxford, 2013)** — cyber = espionage + sabotage + subversion, not "war"; **Ben Buchanan, *The Cybersecurity Dilemma* (Hurst/Oxford, 2016)**; **Jon R. Lindsay** (e.g., work on Stuxnet and on the RMA/COIN debate). Add: P. W. Singer & Allan Friedman, *Cybersecurity and Cyberwar* (2014).
- **Space as a domain; drones/UAS & loitering munitions; autonomous weapons & military AI / "algorithmic warfare"** — HYPE-WATCH throughout; treat claims about AI decisiveness as speculative. Primary/serious: Paul Scharre, *Army of None* (2018) and *Four Battlegrounds* (2023).

### F. Technical / Quantitative Context (moderate depth; SETTLED vs. CONTESTED flagged)
- **Lanchester's laws (linear vs. square law).** Origin: **Frederick W. Lanchester (1916)** and independently **M. Osipov (1915).** Square law → aggregate firepower scales with the square of numbers (concentration); linear law → ancient/unaimed-fire or one-to-one combat. **CONTESTED empirically:** multiple independent historical validation attempts (Fain WWII engagements; Hartley & Helmbold; Lucas & Turkes on Kursk and the Ardennes; the Inchon–Seoul test) "could not establish a Lanchester square law and really could not establish the Lanchester linear law" (Dupuy Institute). Teach as coupled ODEs / a dynamical-systems intuition and as the mathematical dress of the "concentration of force" principle — NOT as a predictive combat model. Critiques: John W. R. Lepingwell, "The Laws of Combat? Lanchester Reexamined," *International Security* (1987).
- **Game theory in strategy & deterrence.** Schelling (above); zero-sum vs. non-zero-sum, credible commitment, the security dilemma (Jervis, "Cooperation Under the Security Dilemma," *World Politics*, 1978). SETTLED as framework; debated in application.
- **Operations research & systems analysis history.** SETTLED and well-documented. WWII: **P. M. S. Blackett** ("Blackett's Circus"), convoy-size optimization (large convoys preferred), depth-charge settings, ASWORG, search theory; Morse & Kimball, *Methods of Operations Research* (1951). Postwar: **RAND Corporation** and systems analysis; **the McNamara era** and its critiques (over-quantification, "whiz kids," the body-count fallacy in Vietnam).
- **Wargaming & simulation.** **Kriegsspiel** — Prussian, **Georg von Reisswitz (the Younger), 1824**, endorsed by von Müffling. **Peter P. Perla, *The Art of Wargaming* (Naval Institute Press, 1990)** — the foremost professional treatment (2nd ed. ed. John Curry, 2011). **Philip Sabin, *Simulating War* (Continuum, 2012).** **Matrix games** — invented by **Chris Engle (January 1988).** **Monte Carlo methods** — conceived by **Stanislaw Ulam (1946)** at Los Alamos, developed with **John von Neumann** and **Nicholas Metropolis** (named for the casino); first publication Metropolis & Ulam, "The Monte Carlo Method," *JASA* (1949).
- **Cybernetics & the OODA loop** — feedback/control concept (Boyd; Osinga situates it in cybernetics, thermodynamics, and Gödel/Heisenberg).
- **Network science / graph-theoretic views** — netwar, dark networks, insurgency (Arquilla & Ronfeldt; Sean Everton, *Disrupting Dark Networks*, 2012).
- **Nuclear exchange / stability modeling** — first-strike stability, cost-exchange ratios, counterforce vs. countervalue (Wohlstetter; Glenn Kent's stability analyses at RAND).

## Major Debates (CONTESTED/OPEN tags for the curriculum)
1. **Clausewitz vs. Jomini** — SETTLED that both matter, CONTESTED which better explains modern war.
2. **Did Liddell Hart distort Clausewitz?** — CONTESTED; strong scholarly consensus that his "Mahdi of mass" caricature was unfair and self-serving.
3. **"German way of war" / Blitzkrieg-as-doctrine myth** — CONTESTED; Frieser and Citino argue 1940 was improvised operational excellence, not a codified doctrine.
4. **RMA / network-centric warfare** — CONTESTED (Biddle vs. Cebrowski school).
5. **"Gerasimov doctrine"** — largely SETTLED as a Western misreading (Galeotti's own retraction), yet still widely misused (HYPE-WATCH).
6. **Strategic-bombing effectiveness** — CONTESTED (US Strategic Bombing Survey vs. revisionists).
7. **Nuclear deterrence rationality** — CONTESTED/OPEN (rational-deterrence theory vs. organizational/psychological critics: Sagan vs. Waltz, *The Spread of Nuclear Weapons*).
8. **"New wars" thesis** — CONTESTED (Kaldor vs. Kalyvas/Newman).
9. **Lanchester empirical validity** — effectively SETTLED as *unvalidated* for real combat.
10. **Cyber "war"** — HYPE-WATCH (Rid's thesis vs. maximalists).

## Recommendations (phased build plan)

**Stage 0 — MVP Fast-Path (4–6 weeks, mostly free):** Echevarria, *Military Strategy: A VSI*; selections from Sun Tzu (Griffith); Clausewitz *On War* Book 1 (Howard/Paret, free USMC PDF); Thucydides' Melian Dialogue + Funeral Oration (*Landmark*); one modern essay each from *The New Makers of Modern Strategy* (2023) and one War on the Rocks / TNSR article on Ukraine. Deliverable: a 2-page "levels of war + ends/ways/means" memo.

**Stage 1 — Foundations & the Idea of Strategy:** Freedman, *Strategy* (Parts I–II) or Brands (ed.) intro; Heuser, *Evolution of Strategy.*

**Stage 2 — Ancient & Non-Western Origins:** Sun Tzu, Thucydides, Kautilya (Olivelle selections), Vegetius (Milner selections).

**Stage 3 — Medieval/Byzantine & Renaissance:** Strategikon (Dennis), Leo VI (Dennis selections), Machiavelli (Lynch), Maurice de Saxe, Frederick, Guibert.

**Stage 4 — The Napoleonic Transformation & the Great Dichotomy:** Jomini (Mendell/Craighill) vs. Clausewitz (Howard/Paret, full Books 1–3, 6, 8) with Strachan's *Biography*; du Picq.

**Stage 5 — Sea & Air Power:** Mahan vs. Corbett; Douhet, Mitchell, Trenchard (memoranda) + strategic-bombing debate.

**Stage 6 — Industrial-Age Operational Art:** Moltke/German General Staff (Citino); WWI attrition; Fuller & Liddell Hart (with distortion critique); Svechin, Tukhachevsky, Isserson, Guderian; Frieser's *Blitzkrieg Legend.*

**Stage 7 — Revolutionary War, COIN & Maneuver:** Mao (Griffith), Galula, Kilcullen, FM 3-24; Boyd/OODA (Osinga).

**Stage 8 — The Nuclear Revolution & Deterrence (+ game theory module):** Brodie, Schelling (both books), Kahn, Wohlstetter, Snyder; Sagan/Waltz debate; light game-theory formalization.

**Stage 9 — Grand Strategy & the Economics of War:** Kennedy, Gaddis, Posen, Brands; van Creveld's *Supplying War.*

**Stage 10 — Contemporary & Future War (+ quantitative module):** Luttwak, van Creveld, Gray, Kaldor, Rupert Smith, Arquilla/Ronfeldt; RMA/NCW debate; cyber (Rid, Buchanan, Lindsay); Gerasimov-myth readings; drones/AI (Scharre) with HYPE-WATCH; Ukraine case study. Quantitative capstone: Lanchester ODEs + OR/wargaming (Perla, Sabin) + a simple matrix game or Monte Carlo attrition exercise.

**Light analytical exercises to thread through:** a Clausewitzian "trinity" analysis of a current war; a Lanchester square-law spreadsheet with an explicit "why this fails empirically" write-up; a Schelling bargaining/brinkmanship two-player exercise; a matrix-game seminar; an ends-ways-means grand-strategy audit of one great power.

**Thresholds that change the plan:** If the learner's goal is professional-military exam prep, front-load the CGSC/Naval War College lists and Echevarria; if it's academic IR/strategic studies, weight Freedman, Gat, Heuser, and the journal literature; if it's quant/analysis, expand Stage 10's OR/wargaming/Lanchester module into two phases.

## Caveats
- **Interpretation is contested even where texts are canonical.** Every theorist above carries a live interpretive dispute; teach the debate, not a settled "answer."
- **Edition currency:** Howard/Paret Clausewitz, Griffith/Sawyer Sun Tzu, *Landmark* Thucydides, Olivelle Arthashastra, Milner Vegetius, Dennis Strategikon/Taktika, Lynch Machiavelli, Naval Institute Press Corbett are all current/in-print; several primary texts (Mahan, Jomini, Giles' Sun Tzu, Graham/Maude Clausewitz) are free public-domain but in older translations — flag the tradeoff.
- **Douhet edition precision:** the free 2019 reprint is Air University Press; the University of Alabama Press edition is 2009. Both use Ferrari's translation (itself dating to 1942, not 1921; 1921 is the original Italian). The English volume bundles five Douhet works.
- **Hype risk is concentrated in the modern strands** (cyber, AI/autonomy, "Gerasimov doctrine," RMA). Several popular sources are marketing- or speculation-driven; the report has tagged the serious primary literature.
- **Quantitative models are for literacy, not prediction:** Lanchester's laws in particular should be taught explicitly as empirically unvalidated for real combat.
- **Eurocentrism:** most classic survey spines under-weight non-Western strategy; the 2023 Brands volume, Heuser, and dedicated Sun Tzu/Kautilya/Mao readings are the correctives built into the plan.
- A few secondary facts here rest on single secondary sources (e.g., certain reading-list contents and reprint dates); verify against the live publisher/library pages before finalizing the curriculum.
