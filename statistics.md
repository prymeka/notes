# Comprehensive Classical Statistics Foundations Curriculum (Verified Resource Dossier)

## TL;DR
- The strongest dependency-ordered spine is: **measure-theoretic probability (Durrett, free)** → **math-stat core (Casella & Berger)** → **frequentist depth (Lehmann & Casella; Lehmann & Romano)** → **Bayesian foundations (Hoff → Gelman BDA3, free PDF)** → **linear-model/GLM theory (Christensen; McCullagh & Nelder)** → **nonparametrics (Wasserman, free)** → **categorical (Agresti)** → **experimental design (Montgomery / Box-Hunter-Hunter)** → **sampling (Lohr / Cochran)**.
- Every canonical edition was verified: Durrett 5th (2019, free PDF on Duke site), Casella & Berger 2nd (2002; 2024 CRC reissue is identical content), Lehmann & Casella *TPE* 2nd (1998), Lehmann & Romano *TSH* 4th (2022, two volumes), Gelman *BDA3* (2013, free PDF), Agresti 3rd (2013), Montgomery 10th (2019), Lohr 3rd (2022), McCullagh & Nelder 2nd (1989).
- Free anchors are real and posted: Durrett PDF, Wasserman's *All of Statistics* (2005 ISBA DeGroot Prize winner) and *All of Nonparametric Statistics* PDFs, MIT OCW 6.436 / 18.650 / 18.655 (18.675 is the current MIT number, materials mostly on instructor pages), NPTEL and LibreTexts. The physics background (measure intuition, statistical mechanics, Monte Carlo, Fourier/characteristic functions) materially accelerates Phases 1, 4, and 6.

## Details

### Phase 0 — Orientation & fast-path bridge (short; optional for this learner)
- **Goal:** Map the whole landscape and calibrate notation before the measure-theoretic climb.
- **Primary:** Wasserman, *All of Statistics* (skim Parts I–II).
- **Python lab:** Reproduce empirical CDFs, plug-in estimators, and a first bootstrap CI on a toy dataset.
- **Physics accelerator:** The learner can likely skip most probability review; treat this as a vocabulary/notation pass only.
- **Relative time:** ~0.5 unit (shortest phase).

### Phase 1 — Measure-theoretic probability
- **Prereqs:** Real analysis (sequences, limits, Riemann integration), basic set theory. The learner's real-analysis and statistical-mechanics background covers this.
- **Goals:** σ-algebras, measures, Carathéodory extension; measurable functions; Lebesgue integration and convergence theorems (MCT, Fatou, DCT); product measures and Fubini; independence; laws of large numbers (weak/strong); weak convergence and characteristic functions; central limit theorems; conditional expectation; martingales and optional stopping.
- **Primary readings (Durrett 5th):** Ch. 1 (measure theory), Ch. 2 (LLN, Borel–Cantelli), Ch. 3 (CLT, characteristic functions, weak convergence), Ch. 4 (martingales). Chapters 5–8 (Markov chains, ergodic theory, Brownian motion) are optional depth.
- **Supplementary:** Billingsley (Anniversary ed.) for a slower analysis-first treatment; MIT OCW 6.436J notes; Dembo's Stanford Math 230/Stat 310 notes (referenced by MIT 18.675).
- **Python lab:** Empirically verify LLN and CLT convergence rates (histogram of standardized partial sums vs. normal; track sup-norm error vs. n); visualize characteristic functions and confirm the inversion/continuity theorem intuition via FFT.
- **Physics accelerator:** Statistical mechanics gives strong intuition for measures on large product spaces, concentration, and the ensemble/limit-theorem viewpoint; Fourier analysis makes characteristic functions immediate; Monte Carlo experience makes the LLN/CLT labs trivial to build.
- **Relative time:** ~2 units (longest single phase; the rigor bar is highest here).

### Phase 2 — Mathematical-statistics core (calculus-based)
- **Prereqs:** Phase 1 (or at least comfort with expectation, convergence, and distributions).
- **Goals:** Data reduction (sufficiency, minimal sufficiency, ancillarity, completeness, factorization theorem); exponential families; likelihood; point estimation (MoM, MLE); unbiasedness and UMVUE via Rao–Blackwell and Lehmann–Scheffé; Cramér–Rao/information inequality; hypothesis testing (Neyman–Pearson lemma, UMP tests, LRT); interval estimation (pivots, inversion of tests); basic asymptotics (consistency, asymptotic normality of MLE, delta method).
- **Primary (Casella & Berger 2nd):** Ch. 5 (properties of random samples), Ch. 6 (data reduction/sufficiency), Ch. 7 (point estimation), Ch. 8 (hypothesis testing), Ch. 9 (interval estimation), Ch. 10 (asymptotics).
- **Anchor papers:** Fisher (1922); Rao (1945); Neyman–Pearson (1933); Lehmann–Scheffé (1950); Blackwell (1947).
- **Supplementary:** MIT 18.650 (Rigollet) notes/videos; Rice 3rd ed. for more applied worked examples.
- **Python lab:** Simulate estimator bias/variance across sample sizes; empirically check that an MLE attains the Cramér–Rao bound in an exponential-family example and fails to in a non-regular one; implement Neyman–Pearson likelihood-ratio tests and plot power curves.
- **Physics accelerator:** Fermi–Dirac/statistical-mechanics familiarity with exponential-family (Gibbs) forms and partition functions maps directly onto sufficiency, natural parameters, and cumulant/log-partition derivatives.
- **Relative time:** ~1.5 units.

### Phase 3 — Frequentist depth (decision theory & advanced testing/estimation)
- **Prereqs:** Phase 2.
- **Goals:** Decision-theoretic framing (loss, risk, admissibility, minimaxity, Bayes rules); equivariance; UMVU theory in depth; shrinkage/Stein estimation; complete-class theorems; UMP unbiased and invariant tests; large-sample testing; the likelihood principle and conditioning debates.
- **Primary:** Lehmann & Casella, *TPE* 2nd ed. (Ch. 1–2 decision theory & unbiasedness; Ch. 4 equivariance; Ch. 5 asymptotics; new Bayesian chapters); Lehmann & Romano, *TSH* 4th ed. Vol. I (finite-sample: NP lemma, UMP, unbiased, invariant tests), Vol. II (large-sample).
- **Companion:** Cox & Hinkley, *Theoretical Statistics* (1974) for conditioning, ancillarity, and the likelihood principle; Wald (1950) for decision-theory origins.
- **Python lab:** Monte Carlo risk comparison of James–Stein vs. MLE in the multivariate normal mean problem (demonstrate inadmissibility of the MLE for dimension ≥ 3); simulate size/power of UMP vs. LRT tests.
- **Physics accelerator:** Monte Carlo fluency makes risk-surface estimation and admissibility experiments straightforward.
- **Relative time:** ~1.5–2 units.

### Phase 4 — Bayesian foundations
- **Prereqs:** Phase 2 (Phase 3 decision theory helps for the decision-theoretic Bayes material).
- **Goals:** Bayes' theorem for inference; conjugate priors and exponential-family conjugacy; noninformative/Jeffreys/reference priors; posterior summaries and credible intervals; hierarchical models; model checking (posterior predictive); Bayesian decision theory; computation (Gibbs, Metropolis–Hastings) as a supporting tool.
- **Primary:** Hoff (2009), Ch. 1–7 (one-parameter models, normal model, Gibbs, MCMC), Ch. 8–11 (hierarchical, regression); then Gelman et al. *BDA3* (2013), Parts I–III (fundamentals, single/multiparameter, hierarchical, model checking, computation).
- **Depth:** Robert, *The Bayesian Choice* 2nd ed. (decision-theoretic foundations, admissibility links to frequentist theory).
- **Anchor:** Dempster–Laird–Rubin (1977) for EM as a bridge between likelihood and Bayesian computation.
- **Python lab:** From-scratch MCMC — Gibbs sampler for a conjugate normal/hierarchical model, then a Metropolis–Hastings sampler for a non-conjugate posterior; assess convergence (trace plots, R-hat) and compare to analytic conjugate results.
- **Physics accelerator:** Statistical-mechanics/Monte Carlo background makes Metropolis–Hastings, detailed balance, Gibbs sampling, and equilibration diagnostics extremely natural — this phase should go fast.
- **Relative time:** ~1.5 units.

### Phase 5 — Linear-model & GLM theory
- **Prereqs:** Phase 2 (distribution theory, MLE); linear algebra (projections, quadratic forms).
- **Goals:** Least squares as orthogonal projection; Gauss–Markov theorem; distribution theory of quadratic forms; the general linear hypothesis and F-tests; estimability and generalized inverses; then the GLM framework — exponential-family responses, link functions, iteratively reweighted least squares, deviance, quasi-likelihood.
- **Primary:** Christensen, *Plane Answers* 5th ed. (projection/theory-first treatment) or Seber & Lee 2nd ed.; then McCullagh & Nelder 2nd ed. (Ch. 1–2 GLM structure, Ch. 4 binary data, Ch. 6 log-linear, Ch. 9 quasi-likelihood).
- **Anchor:** Box & Cox (1964) for response transformations.
- **Python lab:** Implement OLS from scratch via QR/projection; verify Gauss–Markov numerically; then fit a logistic and a Poisson GLM via IRLS from scratch and compare to `statsmodels`.
- **Physics accelerator:** Fourier/linear-algebra fluency makes projection geometry and quadratic-form distribution theory intuitive.
- **Relative time:** ~1.5 units.

### Phase 6 — Nonparametric statistics
- **Prereqs:** Phase 2.
- **Goals:** Empirical distribution function and Glivenko–Cantelli; rank-based tests (sign, Wilcoxon signed-rank and rank-sum, Kruskal–Wallis); Kolmogorov–Smirnov; density estimation (kernels, bandwidth); nonparametric regression basics; bootstrap/jackknife as supporting inference tools.
- **Primary:** Wasserman, *All of Nonparametric Statistics* (free PDF); Hollander/Wolfe/Chicken 3rd ed. or Conover 3rd ed. for classical rank methods.
- **Anchor:** Efron (1979) bootstrap.
- **Python lab:** Implement bootstrap percentile and BCa confidence intervals from scratch; compare to jackknife variance estimates; build kernel density estimators and study bandwidth/bias–variance tradeoff empirically.
- **Physics accelerator:** Resampling is computationally identical in spirit to Monte Carlo the learner already knows; characteristic-function/Fourier intuition helps with kernel smoothing.
- **Relative time:** ~1 unit.

### Phase 7 — Categorical data analysis
- **Prereqs:** Phase 5 (GLM theory).
- **Goals:** Contingency tables; odds ratios and relative risk; chi-squared and exact (Fisher) tests; logistic regression; multinomial/ordinal models; log-linear models and their equivalence to Poisson GLMs; sparse-data and exact inference.
- **Primary:** Agresti, *Categorical Data Analysis* 3rd ed. (Ch. 1–3 tables/inference, Ch. 4–6 logistic, Ch. 8–9 multinomial/ordinal, Ch. 9–10 log-linear).
- **Python lab:** Fit log-linear and logistic models on real contingency tables; test independence and conditional independence; reproduce an odds-ratio analysis with profile-likelihood CIs.
- **Relative time:** ~1 unit.

### Phase 8 — Experimental design (data collection I)
- **Prereqs:** Phase 5 (linear models/ANOVA).
- **Goals:** Principles (randomization, replication, blocking); completely randomized, randomized-block, Latin-square designs; factorial and fractional-factorial designs; confounding and aliasing; response-surface methodology; ANOVA decomposition and contrasts.
- **Primary:** Montgomery, *Design and Analysis of Experiments* 10th ed. (Ch. 2–5 foundations/ANOVA, Ch. 6–8 factorials and fractional factorials, Ch. 11 response surfaces); Box/Hunter/Hunter 2nd ed. for the discovery-oriented philosophy.
- **Anchor:** Fisher's design principles; Box & Cox (1964) for transformations in designed experiments.
- **Python lab:** Design and analyze a 2^k factorial experiment (simulate or use classic data); compute main effects and interactions; construct a half-normal plot; fit and diagnose a response-surface model.
- **Physics accelerator:** Experimental-physics instincts about controls, systematic error, and factor screening transfer directly.
- **Relative time:** ~1 unit.

### Phase 9 — Sampling & survey methodology (data collection II)
- **Prereqs:** Phase 2.
- **Goals:** Simple random, stratified, cluster, systematic, and multistage sampling; ratio and regression estimators; unequal-probability sampling and the Horvitz–Thompson estimator; design effects, weighting, nonresponse, and calibration.
- **Primary:** Lohr, *Sampling: Design and Analysis* 3rd ed. (Ch. 2–6 core designs, Ch. 7 complex surveys, Ch. 8 nonresponse); Cochran, *Sampling Techniques* 3rd ed. for classical theory.
- **Anchor:** Horvitz–Thompson (1952).
- **Python lab:** Simulate a finite population; implement SRS, stratified, and PPS designs; compute Horvitz–Thompson estimators and their design-based variances; empirically verify unbiasedness and compare design efficiencies.
- **Relative time:** ~1 unit.

### Optional adjacent classical modules (bolt-ons, not core)
- **Time series:** Brockwell & Davis, *Time Series: Theory and Methods*; or Shumway & Stoffer. (Fourier background is a major accelerator.)
- **Survival analysis:** Anchored by Cox (1972); text: Klein & Moeschberger, *Survival Analysis: Techniques for Censored and Truncated Data*.
- **Multivariate analysis:** Anderson, *An Introduction to Multivariate Statistical Analysis*; or Mardia, Kent & Bibby, *Multivariate Analysis*.

### Dependency map
- Phase 1 (measure-theoretic probability) → Phase 2 (math-stat core), which is the hub.
- Phase 2 → Phases 3 (frequentist depth), 4 (Bayesian), 5 (linear/GLM), 6 (nonparametrics), 9 (sampling).
- Phase 5 → Phases 7 (categorical) and 8 (design).
- Phase 3 decision theory feeds Phase 4's Bayesian decision theory (soft dependency).
- Bolt-ons: time series/multivariate depend on Phases 1–2 and linear algebra; survival depends on Phases 2 and 5.

### MVP compressed fast-path
For working competence fastest, in order:
1. **Wasserman *All of Statistics*** (whole book) — covers probability, inference, some nonparametrics/bootstrap in one pass.
2. **Casella & Berger** Ch. 5–10 — the inference core done properly.
3. **Durrett** Ch. 1–3 only — measure, LLN, CLT (skip martingales/Markov for MVP).
4. **Hoff** Ch. 1–7 + **BDA3** Parts I–II — Bayesian working knowledge.
5. **McCullagh & Nelder** Ch. 1–4 + **Agresti** Ch. 1–5 — GLM/categorical.
6. One design chapter set (Montgomery Ch. 2–6) and one sampling chapter set (Lohr Ch. 2–6).

Skip Lehmann volumes, Christensen, Robert, and bolt-ons in MVP; add them for true mastery.

### Calibration on foundational debates (settled vs. contested)
- **Frequentist vs. Bayesian foundations — genuinely contested (philosophically), largely reconciled in practice.** Both frameworks are internally coherent; most working statisticians are pragmatic pluralists. Not a "settled" question, but no longer a live methodological war.
- **The likelihood principle and conditioning — genuinely contested.** Birnbaum's theorem (that sufficiency + conditionality imply the likelihood principle) is mathematically valid but its interpretation and force remain debated; frequentist procedures routinely violate the LP. Read Cox & Hinkley alongside Robert for both sides.
- **p-values and statistical significance — actively contested and formally addressed.** The ASA's 2016 statement (Wasserstein & Lazar) and the 2019 "world beyond p<0.05" special issue reflect genuine institutional concern; the direction of reform (de-emphasize the bright-line 0.05, report effect sizes/intervals, avoid "statistical significance" language) is broad consensus, but no single replacement is settled. The 2016 statement's Principle 3 is explicit: *"Scientific conclusions and business or policy decisions should not be based only on whether a p-value passes a specific threshold."*
- **Cramér–Rao, Rao–Blackwell, Lehmann–Scheffé, Neyman–Pearson — settled mathematics.** These are theorems, not debates; the learner should be able to prove them.

## Recommendations
1. **Start with Durrett Ch. 1–4 (free PDF) plus MIT 6.436J notes.** Benchmark to advance: you can state and prove the DCT, the SLLN via Borel–Cantelli, and the Lindeberg–Feller CLT, and construct conditional expectation as an L² projection. If proofs feel slow, drop to Billingsley for the measure chapters, then return.
2. **Do Casella & Berger Ch. 5–10 as the backbone**, reading the four anchor papers (Fisher 1922, Rao 1945, Neyman–Pearson 1933, Lehmann–Scheffé 1950) in parallel. Benchmark: you can derive the UMVUE via Lehmann–Scheffé and construct the UMP test for a one-parameter exponential family unaided.
3. **Split into two tracks after the core:** frequentist depth (Lehmann ×2) and Bayesian (Hoff → BDA3). Given the PayPal fraud-prevention context and your Monte Carlo strength, front-load the Bayesian MCMC lab — it will be the fastest, highest-transfer phase. Benchmark to move on: a from-scratch Metropolis–Hastings sampler that matches a known conjugate posterior.
4. **Then linear/GLM → categorical → design → sampling.** For fraud work, prioritize GLM theory (logistic regression foundations) and categorical data analysis; treat design and sampling as competence-completing rather than urgent.
5. **Use the MVP fast-path if you need working fluency within a constrained window**, then backfill Lehmann, Christensen, and Robert for genuine mastery.
6. **Thresholds that change the plan:** if you cannot verify proofs in Durrett within reasonable effort, insert a dedicated real-analysis refresher (Rudin *Principles of Mathematical Analysis* Ch. 1–7) before continuing. If your goal narrows to fraud modeling specifically, you may defer Phases 8–9 and the bolt-ons indefinitely.

## Caveats
- **18.675 vs. 18.175:** MIT's current graduate probability course number is 18.675 (a renumbering of 18.175). OCW hosts the older **18.175 (Spring 2014)**; the 18.675 artifacts are primarily on instructor course pages (which assign Durrett) rather than a dedicated OCW mirror. Verify exactly which materials are posted before relying on it.
- **Casella & Berger "current edition":** the 2nd edition (2002) is the definitive one; the 2024 Chapman & Hall/CRC printing is a re-imprint of identical content, not a new edition. Do not pay a premium expecting new material.
- **Hoff has only one edition (2009)** despite occasional vendor "2nd edition" mislabels.
- **Free PDFs:** Durrett, Wasserman (both books), and Gelman BDA3 are author/publisher-sanctioned free downloads. Other "free PDF" hits (Scribd, libgen mirrors) are not authorized and were not relied upon here.
- **Cox 1972 page range:** the article proper is pp. 187–202; citations giving 187–220 include the published discussion.
- **Cramér 1946** independently derived the information inequality in *Mathematical Methods of Statistics*; the bound is jointly named Cramér–Rao though Rao (1945) predates the book.
- **Time estimates are relative units, not calendar time**, per your no-fixed-budget instruction; Phase 1 is the heaviest at ~2 units and Phase 0 the lightest at ~0.5.
