# French Self-Study Resource Dossier: Verified Materials for a Spanish-Leveraged, Read-High/Speak-Solid Curriculum

## TL;DR
- **The canonical spine is verifiable and current:** a CEFR-aligned French *méthode* (Édito from Didier, or Cosmopolite from Hachette, both A1→C1) for structure, backed by CLE International's *Grammaire progressive du français* (graded, A1→B2/C2; current 4th ed. at intermediate) and one English-language reference grammar — **Hawkins & Towell (with Lamy), *French Grammar and Usage*, Routledge, 5th ed., 2023** — as the "Hammer/Butt-&-Benjamin" equivalent. All confirmed in print in 2026.
- **The Spanish accelerator is real and has dedicated tooling:** CLE's *Grammaire contrastive pour hispanophones* (Béacco et al.) exists in A1/A2 and B1/B2 volumes with rules written in Spanish, and Assimil publishes a genuine Spanish-base French course (*El francés*). The six key structural traps (partitive article, obligatory subject pronouns, ne…pas, si-clause mood, être/avoir auxiliary, and Spanish↔French faux amis) are well-documented and should be tagged explicitly throughout.
- **The asymmetric target (read B2 / speak B1) is realistic and evidence-backed:** the US State Department's Foreign Service Institute puts French in Category I — "around 24-30 weeks or 600-750 class hours to reach S-3/R-3 proficiency" — and a Romance head start typically cuts that "by 20–30%, since core knowledge transfers directly" (Alliance Française de Lyon). DELF B1 then DELF B2 are the correct certification milestones; Germany has a dense DELF/DALF network — the Institut français d'Allemagne states it operates "un réseau de 24 centres d'examen agréés par la FEI," with sessions "5 fois par an dans 21 centres," including Frankfurt.

## Key Findings

**1. Coursebook spine (the *méthode*).** For a disciplined adult autodidact, the strongest current French method series are **Édito** (Éditions Didier) and **Cosmopolite** (Hachette FLE), both spanning A1 to C1, both built on the communicative/action-oriented approach mandated by the CEFR, both with downloadable audio. **Alter Ego+** (Hachette) is the older standard — still solid but its documents and staged audio have aged; buy Édito or Cosmopolite fresh in 2026. Cosmopolite leads with culture and spends slightly less on grammar drilling, so it pairs best with a grammar workbook (a standard combination: Cosmopolite/Édito for communication, *Grammaire progressive* for the machinery). All three are nominally classroom methods but work for a self-learner who supplements. For a true A0 beginner easing into all-French pages, an English-medium bridge (e.g., *Easy French Step-by-Step*) or Assimil before entering Édito at A1–A2 is sensible sequencing.

**2a. Graded grammar with exercises.** The **CLE International *Grammaire progressive du français*** series is the canonical self-study grammar-with-drills, organized "lesson on the left page, exercises on the right." Levels and current editions: Débutant complet (A1.1), Débutant (A1), **Intermédiaire (A2/B1) — 4th edition** (ISBN 9782090381030; includes downloadable audio + web-app with ~300 interactive online exercises and ~150 self-correcting tests), **Avancé (B1/B2)** (ISBN 9782090363173; book + downloadable audio, 72 chapters, 180 self-correcting online tests), and Perfectionnement (B2/C2, ISBN 9782090395136). This is the workhorse for explicit grammar and maps cleanly onto A1→B2.

**2b. Comprehensive English-language reference grammar.** The direct analogue to Butt & Benjamin (Spanish) / Hammer (German) is **Hawkins & Towell, *French Grammar and Usage*, Routledge Reference Grammars**. The **5th edition (2023)** adds Marie-Noëlle Lamy as co-author (Towell, Lamy & Hawkins; ISBN 9781032444635); a companion **Practising French Grammar: A Workbook, 5th ed.** (ISBN 9781032441405) provides related exercises with model answers. It targets intermediate-to-advanced learners and teachers and is "long trusted as the most comprehensive, up-to-date and user-friendly grammar available." The 4th edition (2015, ISBN 9781138851115, 444/470 pp.) is also widely held and cheaper secondhand. A scholarly alternative is **Batchelor & Chebli-Saadi, *A Reference Grammar of French* (Cambridge, 2011, ISBN 9780521145114, 800 pp.)** — lively and wide-ranging (register, pronunciation, regional variation across France/Québec/Belgium/Switzerland) but older.

**3. Verb/conjugation.** The standard reference is the **Bescherelle** conjugation volume — historically *L'art de conjuguer*, now titled ***La conjugaison pour tous*** in France (Éditions Hatier); recent Hatier editions cover ~10,000–12,000 verbs across roughly 95–105 model tables (edition-dependent), and the most recent edition was published in 2025. In Québec it remains *L'art de conjuguer* (Éditions Hurtubise). Digital tools: **Le Conjugueur** and **Reverso Conjugator** (both free). Graded practice: CLE's **Conjugaison progressive du français** (A1/A2 and A2/B1 workbooks). For English speakers, Barron's **501 French Verbs** is a common alternative.

**4. Reading progression.** Graded readers by CEFR level from established FLE publishers:
- **Hachette *Lire en français facile*** — A1 to B2, original stories + simplified classics, difficult words glossed at page bottom, audio on many titles.
- **CLE International** — 300+ graded readers A1→B2 (rebranded *Lectures Découverte*, formerly *Collection Découverte*); its easiest A1.1 tier is unusually gentle for true beginners.
- **Didier**; **Black Cat/Cideb** (with activities + answer keys); and **Mondes en VF** (mondesenvf.fr), praised as more "adult" in tone even at low levels.
- **Bridge into authentic literature (B1→B2):** ***Le Petit Nicolas*** (Sempé-Goscinny) as a first authentic book; **Maupassant** short stories; **Camus, *L'Étranger*** (short chapters); **Delphine de Vigan, *No et moi*** (recommended at B2). Expect graded B1 stories to begin introducing the *passé simple*, which the reader will also meet in literature.
- **Press for a B1→B2 reader:** start with **RFI *Journal en français facile*** (free, savoirs.rfi.fr — daily ~10-min audio bulletin *with transcript*, designed for learners, A2–B1) and **1jour1actu** (children's news, clear French, defines its own hard words — accidentally excellent for adults); progress to **20 Minutes** (free, concise), **franceinfo** (free public-service, text+audio+video, B1–B2), and **France 24**; then at B2 approach **Le Monde**, **Le Figaro**, **Libération** (newspapers of record, generally B2–C1). Le Monde needs ~95% word coverage, which arrives around B2 — begin with its short reporting/explanatory pieces, not opinion columns.
- **Free legal public-domain French texts:** **Project Gutenberg** (French-language section), **Gallica** (BnF digital library), **Bibebook**, and **Wikisource**.

**5. Listening/audio/media.** Structured learner podcasts, roughly by level:
- **Coffee Break French** (beginner; slow/clear teacher-student format; transcripts require premium).
- **InnerFrench** (host Hugo) — the strongest bridge from beginner lessons to authentic content: slow-but-adult treatment of real topics (society, history, psychology), entirely in French; ~B1–B2. Transcripts require sign-up.
- **News in Slow French** (graded news by level; free tier + paid), **Français Authentique** (Johan; idioms/personal development, intermediate), **One Thing in a French Day** / **Little Talk in Slow French** (intermediate immersion).
- Authentic media as the learner climbs A2→B2: **RFI**, **France Inter**, **franceinfo**, **France 24**, and **TV5MONDEplus** (free global VOD, French with subtitles). The highest-yield technique is the three-pass loop: listen without transcript → read along → listen again.

**6. Pronunciation & phonetics.** For explicit French phonology (nasal vowels [ɑ̃ ɛ̃ ɔ̃ œ̃], the uvular /R/, /y/ vs /u/, liaison, enchaînement, elision, silent letters, prosody):
- **Sons et Lettres: A pronunciation method for intermediate-level French** — a **free open textbook** (Open Textbook Library) with web exercises; praised for consistently presenting oral vs. nasal vowels in binary contrasts.
- CLE's **Phonétique progressive du français** (graded débutant/intermédiaire/avancé, with audio) — the print standard.
- Free: **University of Texas "Français interactif"** phonetics materials; Lawless French liaison/enchaînement lessons.
- **Spanish/Slavic-L1 orientation:** French With Frederic's *Le français pour les hispanophones* offers pronunciation drills built on "una dificultad concreta que tienen los hispanohablantes en francés" — targeting exactly the sounds Spanish speakers (and, transferably, a Polish L1) struggle with: the French /R/, /y/, /ʒ/, nasal vowels, the silent *-e*, and the liaison/enchaînement system. No dedicated Polish-L1 French phonetics text surfaced as canonical; the Spanish-contrastive drills plus IPA-based method are the best fit.

**7. Spanish→French contrastive resources (the accelerator).**
- **CLE International, *Grammaire contrastive pour hispanophones*** (collection *Contrastive – Le français au contact des langues*, dir. Jean-Claude Béacco), authored by Marcella Beacco di Giura, Jean-Claude Béacco & Javier Suso López (the B1/B2 volume adds Mariana Solano Rojas). **Two volumes, both confirmed available on cle-international.com in 2026:** **A1/A2** (EAN **978-2-09-038027-9**; ISBN-10 2090380276; ~€23.50) and **B1/B2** (ISBN **978-2-09-038023-1**; ~€24.40). Crucially, the grammar rules are presented **in Spanish** ("Une présentation simple et en espagnol des règles grammaticales"), with exercises illustrating "les différences et les similitudes entre le français et l'espagnol." Each volume contains **80 graded thematic sheets, 450 exercises with answer keys, two quick assessment tests, and an audio CD with 100 sound files.** These are grammar-reference/exercise books, not full courses. (Note: the malformed ISBN "9782090380276" sometimes cited for A1/A2 is invalid — order 9782090380279.)
- **Assimil, *El francés*** (French course *for Spanish speakers*), by Anthony Bulger & Belén Cabal — a genuine Spanish-base method (source language Spanish, target French), current edition ~2023, book ISBN 9782700509038, ~800 pp / 100 lessons, reaching roughly **B2**. Sold book-only (~€29.90; stock fluctuates on Assimil's own store) or as a download pack / superpack with audio (~€49.90–€65.90). The older *Nuevo francés sin esfuerzo* (Bulger/Chérel) is a distinct earlier edition.
- **The six main structural traps for a Spanish speaker** (tag these explicitly in the curriculum):
  1. **Partitive article** (du/de la/des) — Spanish has no partitive; learners drop it ("J'ai chance" → "J'ai *de la* chance") and omit plural *des* (Spanish has no plural indefinite article).
  2. **Obligatory subject pronouns** — French is not pro-drop; Spanish speakers omit je/tu/il ("En français, ce n'est pas le cas… les apprenants hispanophones auront tendance à oublier le pronom").
  3. **Two-part negation ne…pas** — Spanish single "no" leads to dropping "pas," and to over-adding "pas" with jamais/personne/rien.
  4. **si-clause mood** — "Si j'aurais su" (conditional after *si*) is the classic Spanish-driven error; correct is "Si j'avais su" (plus-que-parfait). Also passé composé vs. Spanish pretérito, and divergent subjunctive usage.
  5. **Auxiliary être vs. avoir** + past-participle agreement — Spanish uses only *haber*, so auxiliary selection and agreement (especially with être and with a preceding direct object) are persistent interference points. The CLE B1/B2 contrastive grammar is the authoritative pedagogical treatment.
  6. **qui/que** (Spanish *que* covers both subject and object relatives; e.g., cleft "C'est moi qui *ai* fait" is mis-agreed as 3rd person) and **faux amis Spanish↔French** — e.g., *embarazada*=enceinte (not embarrassé), *constipado*=enrhumé (not constipé), *salir*=sortir (not "to dirty"), *subir*=monter (not "to suffer"), *quitar*=enlever (not quitter), *entender*=comprendre (not entendre=oír), *contestar*=répondre (not contester), *discutir*=se disputer, *carta*=lettre (not carte), *largo*=long (not large=ancho), *nombre*=nom (not "number"), *gato*=chat (not gâteau), *equipaje*=bagages (not équipage), *habitación*=chambre (not habitation). Academic grounding: a University of Salamanca dissertation on typology of hispanophone errors (gredos.usal.es) documents examples such as *carpeta*/*carpette* and preposition-government errors ("Tu lui intéresses" → "Tu l'intéresses").

**8. Dictionaries & reference tools (free unless noted).**
- **Monolingual, learner-friendly:** **Le Robert Dico en ligne** (dictionnaire.lerobert.com) is the best starting point for contemporary French and is free — its homepage advertises "145 000 définitions, 200 000 synonymes, 100 000 prononciations, la conjugaison, la grammaire… Le Robert, dictionnaire de référence de la langue française." **Larousse** monolingual (free, strong on expressions/proverbs) is the natural alternative.
- **Scholarly monolingual:** **TLFi (Trésor de la langue française informatisé)** via **CNRTL** (cnrtl.fr) and **ATILF** — free; etymologies, historical senses, synonyms/antonyms, literary citations. Best from C1; overwhelming before then.
- **Bilingual:** **Collins-Robert French Dictionary** (free online), **WordReference**, **Linguee** and **Reverso Context** (corpora of real usage), **Oxford-Hachette** (print standard).
- **Corpora/aggregators:** **Lexilogos** (portal to many dictionaries), **Forvo** (crowd-sourced pronunciation selectable by country). Use **DeepL** to check your own writing, not to produce it.

**9. Speaking practice & output (method-level).** To build B1 speaking as a self-learner: (a) **structured 1-on-1 tutoring** on marketplace platforms (italki, Preply) for regular guided output and correction — the single highest-leverage paid investment for speaking; (b) **conversation exchange** (Tandem, HelloTalk) for free reciprocal practice; (c) **shadowing** — listen-and-imitate over authentic audio with transcript — to build prosody and fluency; (d) **output loops** — speak on a topic, self-record, compare to a model, repeat. The recurring evidence-based warning across sources: listening/reading build *passive* French, but without a deliberate output loop, speaking plateaus. Frankfurt's Institut français / Alliance française conversation courses are an optional in-person supplement.

**10. Free/open educational resources — quality-graded.**
- **TV5MONDE "Apprendre le français" (now TV5MONDE EDU)** — genuinely useful: "4,000 exercises to learn French with videos, at all levels… created by French teachers at Alliance Française centres," aimed at "beginners (A1) to advanced learners (B2)," and "Everything is free." Includes TCF prep. **High value.**
- **RFI "Français facile"/Savoirs** (savoirs.rfi.fr) — *Journal en français facile*, daily graded audio-news with published transcript; free. **High value for listening + press bridge.**
- **FUN MOOC (France Université Numérique)** — free national MOOC platform hosting real French-language courses, including "Vivre en France" (A1, A2) and university B1/B2 FLE courses (e.g., Paris-Saclay). **Useful; catalog sessions rotate — check current openings.**
- **LibreTexts French / OER** — e.g., *Livre Libre – Beginning French* (Diamond Wilson, 2020) and UT-Austin "Français interactif"; free, solid for beginners/intermediate. **Useful.**
- **Lawless French / Kwiziq ("Progress with Lawless French")** — free grammar lessons (lawlessfrench.com) plus an adaptive CEFR-based quiz engine (Kwiziq) with a limited free tier and paid subscription; a placement test assigns a CEFR level and builds a personalized study plan with micro-quizzes and progress tracking. **High value for a disciplined, metrics-driven learner; the paid tier is worth considering.**
- **Duolingo / Anki** — Duolingo is shallow for a serious reader targeting B2 (acceptable only as a streak/warmup); **Anki** (spaced-repetition flashcards) is genuinely valuable for vocabulary and verb forms, especially with self-made or curated decks.
- **Verdict:** TV5MONDE EDU, RFI Savoirs, Kwiziq/Lawless, and FUN MOOC are the four free/near-free pillars worth a serious learner's time; Duolingo is not.

**11. Frankfurt / Germany context & certification.**
- **Institut français d'Allemagne** is the France-designated DELF/DALF exam operator in Germany ("centre d'examen DELF DALF en Allemagne désigné par… France Éducation international (FEI) et l'Ambassade de France"), running **DELF/DALF tout public "5 fois par an dans 21 centres,"** within "un réseau de 24 centres d'examen agréés par la FEI" — its own antennae, Franco-German cultural centers, and language schools, explicitly including **Frankfurt** and Heidelberg. **Alliance française** and Institut français branches offer optional courses.
- **DELF B1 and DELF B2 are the correct milestones** for this asymmetric target (B1 speaking, B2 reading). DELF comprises four *independent* diplomas A1–B2 (you can register for B2 without holding B1); DALF covers C1–C2. Diplomas are **valid for life** ("Les diplômes du DELF/DALF sont valables à vie").
- **Structure:** each level tests all four CEFR skills — listening (compréhension orale), reading (compréhension écrite), writing (production écrite), speaking (production orale). Pass = **50/100 overall with a minimum 5/25 per skill**; a *note éliminatoire* below 5/25 fails the entire exam even if the total exceeds 50. DELF B1 ≈ 1h45 written + an individual (one-to-one) speaking test; DELF B2 total ≈ 2h30. Since 2020 a new format (multiple-choice listening/reading) has been phased in and **coexists with the old format through at least 2026** — prepare for both. DELF B2 substitutes for language tests for French university admission and is a common professional threshold; first-attempt pass rates are commonly cited around 35–40%, so structured preparation is essential. There is no carry-over: failing means retaking the full exam.

## Details

**Pedagogy debate — what's settled, contested, and hype.**
- **Broadly settled:** (1) The **communicative / action-oriented approach** underpins the CEFR and every major method (Édito, Cosmopolite, Alter Ego). (2) **Comprehensible input** — large volumes of listening/reading slightly above current level — is essential and chronically under-supplied by learners. (3) For **adult learners, explicit grammar instruction helps** (adults leverage analytical capacity), especially when paired with communicative use; grammar-only and input-only extremes both underperform a blend. (4) **Deliberate output practice is necessary** for speaking; passive skills don't transfer automatically to production.
- **Genuinely contested:** the optimal *ratio* and *sequencing* of explicit grammar vs. pure input; how much early speaking (silent/input-first period vs. early production); and the role of translation/grammar-translation — somewhat rehabilitated for adults and especially apt for a Romance-to-Romance contrastive approach, but not defensible as a primary method.
- **Hype / low-value:** gamified apps (Duolingo) marketed as sufficient for advanced proficiency; "fluency in X weeks" claims; AI-chat tutors positioned as replacements for human correction. For a B2-reading target these are, at best, warmups.

**Sequencing the four skills & realistic timelines.**
- **FSI** classifies French as **Category I: "around 24-30 weeks or 600-750 class hours to reach S-3/R-3 proficiency"** (loosely B2–C1), the same tier as Spanish, Italian, Portuguese, Romanian, and Dutch. Commonly cited Cambridge/Council-of-Europe guided-hour bands: A1 ~80–100h, A2 ~180–200h cumulative, B1 ~350–400h, B2 ~550–600h, C1 ~750–800h+.
- **Romance head start:** a B1 Spanish speaker "may see that figure drop by 20–30%, since core knowledge transfers directly" (Alliance Française de Lyon). For this learner, defensible self-study estimates: **A0→B1 speaking ≈ 250–400 focused hours**; **A0→B2 reading ≈ 350–500 hours** — reading reaches B2 faster than speaking because cognate density and transfer are highest in reading and lowest in spontaneous production. At ~1 hour/day, B1 in roughly 9–15 months and B2-reading within ~18–24 months is a reasonable plan; heavier daily loads compress this. (His Polish L1 and Russian reading add case-system intuition but little Romance vocabulary transfer — Spanish is the dominant accelerator.)
- **Skill sequencing:** front-load listening + reading (comprehensible input) alongside explicit grammar and phonetics *from day one* (fix the /R/, /y/, and nasal vowels before they ossify); layer in speaking output as soon as A1 structures are stable — don't defer it or it lags; keep writing tied to grammar consolidation. Given the asymmetric goal, allocate proportionally more time to graded→authentic reading and press while keeping speaking at a steady B1-sufficient cadence (regular tutoring/exchange).

## Recommendations

**Phase 0 — setup (weeks 1–2).** Establish the spine: choose **one** method (recommend **Édito**, Didier) + **CLE *Grammaire progressive* Débutant/A1** + **Bescherelle** + **Assimil *El francés*** (Spanish-base, for the contrastive jump-start and daily input) + **CLE *Grammaire contrastive pour hispanophones* A1/A2**. Install **Anki**; bookmark **Le Robert**, **WordReference**, **RFI Savoirs**, **TV5MONDE EDU**, **Lawless French/Kwiziq**. Do a phonetics primer (*Sons et Lettres* free + *Phonétique progressive*) immediately.

**Phase 1 — A1→A2.** Method + *Grammaire progressive* Intermédiaire (A2/B1) in parallel; Assimil daily; CLE/Hachette A1–A2 graded readers; RFI *Journal en français facile* + Coffee Break French for listening; begin weekly italki/Tandem speaking as soon as basic structures land. **Attach contrastive tags** from the *Grammaire contrastive* A1/A2 at every relevant grammar point (partitive, subject pronouns, negation, être/avoir). *Benchmark:* pass a Kwiziq A2 check; read A2 readers comfortably.

**Phase 2 — A2→B1.** *Grammaire progressive* Avancé (B1/B2); *Grammaire contrastive* B1/B2 volume; graded readers up to B1, then *Le Petit Nicolas* / Maupassant; InnerFrench + franceinfo; escalate speaking frequency; add Hawkins & Towell as a lookup reference. ***Milestone: sit DELF B1*** at an Institut français center (Frankfurt/Germany) once mock scores clear ~60/100. *Benchmark:* hold a clear everyday conversation; read *20 Minutes* fluently.

**Phase 3 — B1→B2 reading push.** Shift weight to authentic literature (*L'Étranger*, *No et moi*) and press (*Le Monde*, starting with short reporting/explanatory pieces); *Grammaire progressive* Perfectionnement for fine points; sustain B1 speaking via tutoring/exchange. ***Milestone: DELF B2*** — prep is reading-focused, but train all four skills because of the 5/25 note éliminatoire. *Benchmark:* read a novel and a *Le Monde* long-read with ~95% coverage.

**Thresholds that change the plan:** If speaking outpaces reading, reallocate to more reading volume. If DELF B2 mock reading is <70% while speaking is comfortable at B1, hold speaking steady and add daily authentic reading. If grammar accuracy plateaus, intensify Kwiziq/*Grammaire progressive* drilling plus targeted review of the six Spanish-interference traps. If listening lags, intensify the three-pass shadowing loop.

## Caveats
- **Quality varies within graded-reader series and by individual title;** adapted classics lose authorial voice. Preview before buying where possible.
- **Prices and stock fluctuate,** especially Assimil book-only editions and imported CLE/Didier titles bought in Germany; confirm current EAN and price at the retailer. The A1/A2 contrastive-grammar ISBN sometimes circulated as "9782090380276" is **malformed** — order **9782090380279** (A1/A2) or **9782090380231** (B1/B2).
- **FSI/CEFR hour estimates are institutional averages, not guarantees;** many are repeated across sites without transparent methodology. Treat the 20–30% Romance discount as a reasoned estimate, not a measured figure.
- **DELF format is mid-transition** (new multiple-choice comprehension format coexists with the old through at least 2026); prepare for both and confirm the format with your Frankfurt-area center at registration. No carry-over on partial fails.
- **Many "best resource" listicles are SEO/marketing;** this dossier prioritizes publisher pages (CLE, Routledge, Hachette, Assimil), the CNRS/ATILF, Institut français d'Allemagne, France Éducation international, and the FSI. Where only pedagogical/teaching-site sources were available (some contrastive traps, some pricing), that is flagged above.
- **Le Monde/Le Figaro/Libération are largely paywalled;** budget for one subscription at Phase 3, or rely on franceinfo/France 24/20 Minutes (free) plus public-domain literature (Gallica, Gutenberg, Bibebook, Wikisource).
- **No canonical Polish-L1 French resource surfaced;** the Spanish-contrastive materials are the right accelerator, with the reference grammar in English serving the learner's professional English. The Polish/Russian background aids case/aspect intuition but transfers little vocabulary — lean on Spanish.
