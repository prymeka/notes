# Self-Education Curriculum Blueprint: Copper Mining & Aluminum Production

## TL;DR
- This blueprint gives you everything to build a phased markdown curriculum: shared foundations (lifecycle, reporting codes, processing, mining economics/valuation, ESG, LME), then commodity deep dives on copper (porphyry geology → smelting/electrorefining or SX-EW → the electrification demand thesis) and aluminum (bauxite → Bayer → Hall-Héroult electrolysis → China dominance), each with SETTLED/CONTESTED/HYPE-WATCH tags, free-vs-paid resource flags, primary sources, glossaries, and a fast-track path.
- The single most important conceptual split to master: copper's story is a MINING/GEOLOGY story (grade decline in porphyries, TC/RCs, supply-gap debate) while aluminum's story is an ENERGY/CHEMISTRY story (bauxite mining is trivial; the value and complexity sit in the Bayer refining and the electricity-hungry Hall-Héroult smelting, ~13–15 kWh/kg, ~55–60% Chinese).
- Current market anchors (2025–2026) you must be able to reference: copper set records on both LME (3-month copper hit $11,200/t on Oct 29, 2025, breaking the May 2024 $11,104.50 high, then $11,210.50/t on Nov 28, 2025) and COMEX (>$5.95/lb July 2025) with a US-tariff-driven arbitrage; copper concentrate TC/RCs collapsed to negative territory (Fastmarkets assessed the concentrates TC index cif Asia Pacific at −$66.60/t on Oct 10, 2025, and the 2026 annual benchmark settled at $0/t, the first zero on record); aluminum trades ~$2,900–3,300/t with China pinned against a 45 Mt production cap; the US Midwest premium spiked to records on 50% Section 232 tariffs; and ELYSIS started up a commercial-size (450 kA) inert-anode cell on Nov 13, 2025 (still HYPE-WATCH for full commercialization).

## Key Findings

**Structure the curriculum in three phases.** Phase 0 = shared base-metal foundations (reusable from the zinc/lithium curriculum). Phase 1 = copper deep dive. Phase 2 = aluminum deep dive. Each metal splits into (a) chemistry/physics, (b) geology/raw material, (c) processing/metallurgy, (d) end-uses/demand, (e) market structure/companies, (f) a light AI/ML layer.

**The two audiences need different emphases.** For operators/geologists/metallurgists: process detail, deposit models, flowsheets, plant vocabulary. For investors/analysts: cost curves (C1/AISC), NAV/P-NAV valuation, TC/RCs, premiums, supply-demand balances, company league tables. The physics background is the natural bridge on both sides via electrolysis energy balances, Faraday's law, and conductivity.

**Epistemic calibration is cleanly separable.** SETTLED: the porphyry deposit model, flash smelting, electrorefining, the Bayer and Hall-Héroult processes, aluminum's recycling energy advantage. CONTESTED: the size/timing of the copper supply gap, durability of "green" metal premiums, whether China's aluminum cap truly holds. HYPE-WATCH: inert-anode commercialization timelines, the most aggressive electrification/AI-data-center demand curves, "supercycle" language.

## Details

### PHASE 0 — SHARED FOUNDATIONS (reusable base-metal core)

**0.1 The mining/production lifecycle.** Exploration → resource definition (drilling, assays) → scoping/PEA → pre-feasibility (PFS) → definitive/bankable feasibility (DFS) → permitting → construction/development → operation → closure/rehabilitation. Insiders speak in study tiers (PEA/PFS/DFS) with tightening confidence and cost-estimate accuracy. Crucial framing for aluminum: the "mining" stage (bauxite) is simple surface strip-mining; the intellectual and capital complexity sits downstream in alumina refining and smelting. For copper, the opposite — the mine and its declining ore grade dominate the economics.

**0.2 Resource/reserve reporting codes.** JORC (Australasia), NI 43-101 (Canada), SK-1300 (US SEC, since 2021). Master the resource-vs-reserve hierarchy: Inferred → Indicated → Measured (resources); Probable → Proven (reserves, i.e., the economically mineable part demonstrated by at least a PFS). A "qualified person"/"competent person" signs off. This directly feeds valuation (only reserves anchor a base-case NAV; resources are optionality). Especially relevant for large low-grade copper porphyries and thick bauxite blankets.

**0.3 Shared mineral processing.** Comminution: crushing → grinding in SAG (semi-autogenous) and ball mills; increasingly HPGR (high-pressure grinding rolls) for energy efficiency. Concentration by froth flotation (the workhorse for copper sulphides — hydrophobic sulphide particles attach to air bubbles, gangue sinks). Hydrometallurgy: leaching (heap/tank), solid-liquid separation (thickeners, filters), solvent extraction. Comminution is the single biggest energy consumer in a sulphide concentrator, a good physics entry point (Bond work index, energy-size relationships).

**0.4 Shared economics & industry structure.**
- Cost metrics: **C1 cash cost** (direct cash cost per pound/tonne, net of by-product credits) and **AISC** (all-in sustaining cost, adds sustaining capex, G&A, royalties). Copper has no single enforced AISC standard (unlike gold's World Gold Council definition); most copper miners report C1 plus separate sustaining capital. As of 2025, copper C1 costs generally run roughly $1.30–$2.80/lb before by-product credits, and net C1 can fall below $1.00/lb for polymetallic operations with big gold/moly credits. Real 2025 examples: BHP's Escondida cut unit cash costs 18% to $1.19/lb (FY25) on higher feed grades; Rio Tinto's copper C1 net unit cost fell 53% to $0.67/lb on higher volumes and gold credits; Codelco's C1 rose to ~$2.09/lb after the El Teniente seismic disruption.
- Cost curves & quartiles: assets ranked by cost; first-quartile producers survive downturns. By-product credits (gold, silver, moly for copper) can distort/mask true costs — a key analyst caveat, and net C1 can even go negative for polymetallic mines.
- Capex vs opex; commodity price cycles; offtake agreements; royalties and streaming (e.g., Franco-Nevada, Wheaton, Royal Gold model); majors vs juniors; project financing and equity dilution risk.
- **Equity valuation:** NAV (sum-of-the-parts DCF of each mine's after-tax life-of-mine cash flows, discounted ~5% real for low-risk precious, ~8–10% for riskier base-metal/jurisdiction), then **P/NAV** (the dominant multiple, typically ~0.5–1.5x); **EV/EBITDA** (supplementary, for steady producers, but volatile because mining EBITDA swings with prices); **EV per resource tonne/pound** (crude, for early-stage). Juniors trade below NAV (single-asset, dilution risk); seniors near/above NAV (optionality, liquidity). Note: 2024 mining M&A reached ~$26.5bn across 62 major deals, and exploration success rates have fallen (~5% in 2024 vs ~10% in 2010), pushing majors toward acquisition-based reserve replacement.

**0.5 ESG, permitting, water, tailings, carbon, community.** Water use (acute in Atacama copper country — desalination and pipelines now standard), tailings management under the **Global Industry Standard on Tailings Management (GISTM)**, carbon footprint (Scope 1/2/3), and community/Indigenous relations and social licence (FPIC — free, prior and informed consent). GISTM was published in August 2020, co-convened by the ICMM, UN Environment Programme (UNEP), and Principles for Responsible Investment (PRI) via the Global Tailings Review, in direct response to the January 25, 2019 Brumadinho tailings dam collapse in Brazil (272 killed). It comprises 6 topics, 15 principles, and 77 auditable requirements, with ICMM-member conformance deadlines of August 2023 ("extreme"/"very high" consequence facilities) and August 2025 (remaining facilities); the Global Tailings Management Institute (GTMI) launched January 2025 to manage third-party certification.

**0.6 The London Metal Exchange.** How base metals price and trade: LME cash and 3-month contracts, warrants, warehousing, the ring, contango/backwardation, physical premiums layered on top of the exchange price. COMEX (US copper) and SHFE (Shanghai) as the other poles. This is shared across all base metals.

**0.7 Insider vocabulary (shared).** Grade, cut-off grade, strip ratio, recovery, throughput, concentrate, tailings, gangue, NSR (net smelter return), payability, ramp-up, nameplate capacity, brownfield vs greenfield, life-of-mine (LOM).

### PHASE 1 — COPPER

**1.1 Chemistry & physics.** The defining property is electrical conductivity (second only to silver; ~59.6 MS/m, ~101% IACS) and high thermal conductivity — this is *why* copper is the metal of electrification and a natural physics hook. Oxidation states +1 (cuprous) and +2 (cupric). Ore minerals: sulphides — chalcopyrite (CuFeS₂, the dominant ore), bornite, chalcocite (Cu₂S), covellite (CuS); oxides/carbonates — malachite, azurite, chrysocolla, cuprite. Sulphides go the smelting route; oxides go the leach/SX-EW route.

**1.2 Deposit geology.** **Porphyry copper deposits** dominate — about 60% of world copper production and ~65% of resources, per USGS; large tonnage (hundreds of Mt to billions of t), low grade (2008 average ~0.44% Cu; typical range ~0.3–1%), associated Mo/Au/Ag, formed in subduction-related magmatic arcs. Master the USGS porphyry model (SIR 2010-5070-B) and Richard Sillitoe's work. Other types: **sediment-hosted stratabound** copper (Central African Copperbelt in DRC/Zambia; Kupferschiefer in Poland/Germany), **IOCG** (iron-oxide-copper-gold, e.g., Olympic Dam), **VMS**, and **skarn**. Producing countries (2025): Chile #1 (~5.3 Mt, ~23% of world), then DRC (~3.3 Mt), Peru (~2.7 Mt, ~12%), China (~1.8 Mt), USA, Russia, Zambia, Australia, Indonesia, Mexico. Chile+DRC+Peru ≈ half of world output; top 10 ≈ 80%. Reserves are similarly concentrated: Chile (~180 Mt), Australia, Peru (~85 Mt), DRC, and Russia hold >55% of the global total.

**1.3 Processing — two routes.**
- **Sulphide (pyrometallurgical) route:** flotation → concentrate (~25–30% Cu) → smelting (flash smelting, e.g., Outotec/Outokumpu and Inco designs, plus bath smelting: Isasmelt, Ausmelt, Noranda, Teniente, Mitsubishi) → matte → converting (Peirce-Smith or continuous) to blister copper (~98–99%) → fire refining → anode copper (~99.5%) → **electrorefining** to 99.99% cathode. Big SO₂ management → sulphuric acid byproduct. The definitive text is Schlesinger, King, Sole & Davenport, *Extractive Metallurgy of Copper* (5th ed., 2011, Elsevier).
- **Oxide/low-grade route:** heap leaching (sulphuric acid) → **solvent extraction–electrowinning (SX-EW)** → cathode directly, bypassing smelting. Electrowinning uses an inert anode; a good Faraday's-law example.
- **TC/RCs (treatment and refining charges):** the fees miners pay smelters to convert concentrate to metal, deducted from the LME-linked payable-copper value. They fall when concentrate is tight, rise when abundant. In 2025 they collapsed to record lows and even *negative* spot levels — Fastmarkets assessed the copper concentrates TC index (cif Asia Pacific) at −$66.60/t on Oct 10, 2025 (its eighth consecutive weekly decline), and the 2026 annual benchmark settled at $0/t, the first zero on record. Wood Mackenzie's Julian Kettle called the charges "not sustainable" and "not healthy for the industry." The driver: per the IEA, "since 2005, China has accounted for over 90% of growth in global copper smelter output, lifting its share from around 15% to half of global supply in 2025" — smelting capacity has outrun mine supply. Japan, Spain and South Korea issued a rare joint statement in October 2025 warning current TC/RCs are unsustainable. This is a structurally important, currently CONTESTED-in-implication datapoint.
- Byproducts: molybdenum, gold, silver, rhenium, sulphuric acid. Concentrate market vs refined cathode market are distinct.

**1.4 End uses & demand.** Electrical/power infrastructure (dominant), construction, electronics, transport, plumbing. The electrification/energy-transition thesis: EVs (roughly 2.5–4x the copper of an ICE car), grids, renewables, and now AI data centers (S&P estimates data centers alone could consume 250–550 kt/yr by 2030, ~1–2% of demand). "Dr. Copper" — the macro bellwether nickname. Recycling is already ~20% of refined supply (~4.5 Mt secondary in 2023).

**1.5 Market & companies.** LME copper contract, COMEX, "Dr. Copper." **2024–2026 price action:** LME 3-month copper set a record $11,200/t on Oct 29, 2025 (breaking the May 2024 $11,104.50 high), then $11,210.50/t on Nov 28, 2025; COMEX hit a record ~$5.96/lb in July 2025 after a 50% US import-tariff announcement, driving a historic LME–COMEX arbitrage (spread reached >$2,500/t early 2025) and massive metal flows to US warehouses (US inventories >500,000 t while LME stocks fell below 100,000 t). Major mine disruptions in 2025 (Grasberg, Kamoa-Kakula, El Teniente) tightened the balance. 

Major producers (2024 output): **Codelco** (~1.33 Mt, world #1), **BHP** (~1.4 Mt attributable), **Freeport-McMoRan** (~1.26 Mt), **Glencore** (~0.95 Mt), **Southern Copper/Grupo Mexico** (~0.9 Mt), **Zijin Mining** (~0.81 Mt), **First Quantum** (~0.65–0.75 Mt, hit by the Cobre Panama shutdown), **Rio Tinto** (~0.65 Mt), **KGHM** (~0.57 Mt), **Antofagasta**, **Anglo American** (pursuing a ~$53bn merger with Teck), **Teck**. 

Major mines: **Escondida** (Chile, BHP-operated 57.5%; ~1.28 Mt 2024, record ~1.35 Mt 2025 — world's largest), **Collahuasi** (Glencore/Anglo/Japanese consortium), **Grasberg** (Indonesia, Freeport; ~816 kt 2024, but a deadly Sept 2025 mudslide halted operations, full capacity not expected until ~2028), **El Teniente** & **Chuquicamata** (Codelco), **Morenci** (Arizona, Freeport 72%/Sumitomo), **Kamoa-Kakula** (DRC, Ivanhoe/Zijin; record ~437 kt 2024, hit by a May 2025 seismic/flooding event), **Oyu Tolgoi** (Mongolia, Rio Tinto 66%; underground ramp-up delivered a ~61% production surge in 2025).

**1.6 The supply-gap / supercycle debate (CONTESTED + HYPE-WATCH).** The IEA *Global Critical Minerals Outlook 2025* projected "implied deficits of 30% for copper in the STEPS [Stated Policies Scenario]" by 2035, "widen[ing] to 35% under the Announced Pledges Scenario (APS), and over 40% in the Net Zero Emissions (NZE) Scenario"; the 2026 update narrowed the base case to ~25%. S&P Global's "Copper in the Age of AI: The Challenges of Electrification" (Jan 8, 2026) projects demand rising from 28 Mt (2025) to 42 Mt by 2040 (a 50% increase), production peaking ~33 Mt in 2030, leaving a 10 Mt deficit — "25% below projected demand"; co-chair Daniel Yergin framed it: "copper is the great enabler of electrification, but the accelerating pace of electrification is an increasing challenge for copper." Structural arguments: ore grades down ~40% since 1991, ~17-year average discovery-to-production timeline, and a collapse in major discoveries (S&P: only ~12–14 of ~228 major deposits found in the last decade, none in 2022 or 2023). CRITICS/nuance: ICSG near-term balances often show surpluses; Roland Schodde (MinEx Consulting) argues the world is not running out of copper — 2010–2024 brownfield exploration delivered ~4x greenfield discoveries and global resources kept growing (after 296 Mt mined, resources grew 946 Mt); higher prices, substitution, and recycling can close gaps. Treat "supercycle" language as HYPE-WATCH.

**1.7 Copper AI/ML (light).** Autonomous haulage (BHP, Rio Tinto, Fortescue fleets, including large Chilean and Pilbara operations), predictive maintenance, ore sorting, computer-vision froth-flotation control (reagent/airflow/pulp-level optimization), grinding-circuit and SX-EW optimization, ML-driven porphyry exploration (landscape-evolution + ML prospectivity models). Roles at the intersection: process data scientists, remote-operations-center engineers, geometallurgy/exploration ML.

### PHASE 2 — ALUMINUM

**2.1 Chemistry & physics.** Low density (~2.7 g/cm³, lightweighting), good conductivity (~61% IACS — used in transmission because it's lighter/cheaper per unit conductance than copper), corrosion resistance via a self-passivating Al₂O₃ oxide layer, near-infinite recyclability. Bauxite ore = a mix of hydrated aluminum oxide/hydroxide minerals: gibbsite (Al(OH)₃), boehmite and diaspore (AlO(OH)), plus iron/silica/titania impurities; bauxite typically contains ~50% aluminum oxide (the highest-grade commercially mined metal ore). Alumina = Al₂O₃.

**2.2 The three-stage value chain (the core of the aluminum story).**
1. **Bauxite mining** — mostly surface/strip mining of tropical laterite blankets; simple relative to hard-rock mining. USGS: ~85% of global bauxite goes to alumina via the Bayer process.
2. **Alumina refining — the Bayer process:** digest crushed bauxite in hot caustic soda (NaOH) under pressure → clarify (the insoluble residue is red mud / bauxite residue, the major waste stream) → precipitate aluminum hydroxide → calcine to alumina. Approximate mass ratios: ~4–5 t bauxite → ~2 t alumina → 1 t aluminum.
3. **Aluminum smelting — the Hall-Héroult process:** electrolysis of alumina dissolved in molten cryolite (Na₃AlF₆) at ~950–980°C in reduction cells ("pots"), typically drawing ~200,000–450,000 amperes at ~4–4.5 V, with consumable carbon anodes. Overall: 2Al₂O₃ + 3C → 4Al + 3CO₂. Energy intensity: modern smelters use roughly **13–15 kWh per kg of aluminum** (industry average ~15.4 kWh/kg; theoretical minimum ~6.2 kWh/kg), a superb physics/thermodynamics teaching case (Faraday's law gives ~2.98 kg Al per 1000 Ah at 100% efficiency; real current efficiency ~90–95%). The **anode effect** (a voltage spike from alumina depletion, historically emitting potent PFC greenhouse gases) and carbon-anode consumption (~0.4–0.45 kg C per kg Al, ~1.5 t CO₂/t Al in modern plants) are key operating concepts.

**2.3 Geography (China dominance is the central theme).** Bauxite (2025): Guinea (world #1, ~150 Mt, ~34% of mine supply), Australia (~97 Mt, ~22%), China (~87 Mt, ~20%), Brazil (~33 Mt), India (~25 Mt), plus Indonesia and Jamaica; Guinea holds the largest reserves (~7.4 bn t). Alumina refining: China dominant, then Australia (Australia sets the FOB benchmark, supplying >30% of global exports). Primary aluminum smelting: China ~55–60% of world output (~44.2–45.0 Mt in 2025 against a self-imposed 45 Mt cap, at ~97% utilization), then India, Russia (~4 Mt), Canada (~3.3 Mt, mostly Québec hydro), the Gulf/Middle East (EGA), Norway. Indonesia is the main new growth center outside China (often via Chinese investment). Smelting locates near cheap/stranded power (hydro in Canada/Norway/Iceland; coal in China/India; gas in the Gulf) because electricity is the dominant cost.

**2.4 Energy & carbon.** Aluminum smelting consumes on the order of 2–3% of global electricity (primary aluminum is often the single largest industrial electricity consumer). The carbon footprint depends overwhelmingly on the power source: hydro-based (~4 t CO₂e/t) vs coal-based (~15–20 t CO₂e/t). The "green/low-carbon aluminum" narrative and premiums — CONTESTED on premium durability. **Inert anode technology (HYPE-WATCH):** ELYSIS (Rio Tinto–Alcoa JV, with Apple and the Canadian/Quebec governments) aims to replace carbon anodes and emit O₂ instead of CO₂. On November 13, 2025 ELYSIS started up its 450 kA inert-anode cell at Rio Tinto's Alma, Québec smelter — described by ELYSIS/Rio Tinto as "the first implementation of inert anode technology at this commercial-size scale"; the next step is "a multi-year, multi-cell testing program designed to prove the resilience, efficiency and durability of the technology" (CIM Magazine, Nov 2025), with an internal goal of maturity "by the end of the decade." Open questions remain on energy consumption, anode longevity, and cost. Note the physics catch: inert anodes forfeit the chemical energy of carbon, so theoretical electricity demand is actually higher (SINTEF: ~9.16 kWh/kg theoretical, ~3 kWh/kg greater in practice than carbon-anode cells).

**2.5 End uses & demand.** Transportation (autos, aerospace — lightweighting), packaging (beverage cans), construction, electrical transmission, consumer/industrial goods. Demand drivers: lightweighting, EVs, packaging, construction, grid. China accounts for >55% of global demand (a 1% rise in Chinese demand ≈ 460,000 t).

**2.6 Market structure.** LME aluminum contract; the alumina market priced largely as a percentage of the LME aluminum price (Platts/Fastmarkets Australia FOB indices; smelter-grade alumina, ~99.5% Al₂O₃), which spiked above $780/t in late 2024 then fell to the mid-$300s in 2025 (cost-driven, illiquid, opaque pricing). **Regional premiums** layered on LME: US Midwest premium, European duty-paid/unpaid, Japan MJP (Main Japanese Port). **Primary vs secondary (recycled)** — recycling is central because secondary aluminum uses only ~5% of primary energy (95% saving) and matches primary quality; ~75% of all aluminum ever produced is still in use, and >80% of US production is now from scrap. **Russian metal/sanctions:** after April 12, 2024 US/UK sanctions, the LME banned warranting of Russian aluminum, copper and nickel produced on/after April 13, 2024; Russian-origin metal had been ~91% of LME aluminum stocks in March 2024; the EU moved toward its own gradual ban in 2025. **Section 232 tariffs:** the US restored 25% aluminum import tariffs (Feb 2025) then raised them to 50% (June 2025), sending the Midwest premium to record highs — it climbed to ~99 c/lb by January 2026 (a near-fivefold rise from the ~20 c/lb 2024 baseline) and to a record ~$2,182/t in Feb 2026; evidence suggests the tariff functioned mainly as a price increase on US consumers rather than reviving domestic smelting. Major producers: Chalco (Aluminum Corp. of China), China Hongqiao, Rio Tinto, Alcoa, Rusal, Norsk Hydro, Emirates Global Aluminium, Hindalco/Novelis, South32, Century Aluminum. **2025–2026 price action:** LME aluminum climbed from ~$2,460–2,720/t in H1 2025 toward ~$2,900–3,300/t by late 2025/2026 on the China cap, Middle East supply disruptions, and inventories near multi-decade lows.

**2.7 Aluminum AI/ML (light).** Smelter pot/cell control and optimization is huge given the energy intensity: anode-effect prediction, energy/current-efficiency optimization, bath chemistry/temperature and anode-cathode-distance (ACD) control; Bayer-process optimization; predictive maintenance on potlines and refineries; and emerging robotics (automated anode-change and superstructure maintenance, e.g., Autoa, AlumatIQ; Rio Tinto's robotic anode-yoke repair at its Tiwai Point smelter). Roles: process control/optimization engineers, smelter data scientists, industrial-automation/robotics roles.

## Recommendations

**Stage 1 (weeks 1–2, shared base):** Work Phase 0. Read the USGS copper and bauxite/alumina Mineral Commodity Summaries cover to cover; read one mining-valuation primer (Corporate Finance Institute or Sell Side Handbook on NAV/P-NAV) and the LME education pages. Benchmark to advance: you can explain the resource/reserve ladder, define C1 vs AISC, and walk through a P/NAV calculation.

**Stage 2 (weeks 3–5, copper):** Read the USGS porphyry model + a Sillitoe overview; then the flotation→smelting→electrorefining and SX-EW flowsheets from *Extractive Metallurgy of Copper* (borrow/library if avoiding purchase). Track TC/RCs and the LME/COMEX arbitrage weekly. Benchmark: you can contrast the sulphide vs oxide routes, explain why negative TC/RCs signal concentrate scarcity plus Chinese smelter overbuild, and argue both sides of the supply-gap debate citing IEA/S&P and Schodde.

**Stage 3 (weeks 6–8, aluminum):** Master the three-stage value chain; do the Hall-Héroult energy-balance and Faraday's-law calculations yourself (this is your differentiator). Read IAI and Aluminum Association materials; follow the alumina index, Midwest premium, and the China cap. Benchmark: you can quantify why aluminum is "congealed electricity," explain red mud, and give a calibrated take on inert anodes and green premiums.

**Stage 4 (ongoing):** Add the light AI/ML layer last, anchored to your ML background; skim 1–2 review papers on AI in mineral processing and on smelter pot control.

**Minimum-viable fast-track (≈15–20 hours):** (1) USGS MCS copper + bauxite/alumina [2 hrs]; (2) one NAV/P-NAV primer + LME basics [3 hrs]; (3) copper: porphyry model + the two processing routes + current price/TC-RC/supply-gap narrative [5 hrs]; (4) aluminum: Bayer + Hall-Héroult + energy balance + China cap + premiums/tariffs + ELYSIS [5 hrs]; (5) memorize the two glossaries and the company league tables [2 hrs].

**Thresholds that change the plan:** If your conversations skew investor-heavy, deepen Stage 1 valuation and the Stages 2/3 market-structure sections and lighten process detail; if operator/metallurgist-heavy, invert. If inert anodes pass multi-cell/multi-year validation (watch ELYSIS Alma results), upgrade that topic from HYPE-WATCH toward CONTESTED. If IEA/S&P deficit forecasts converge with actual sustained deficits and >$12k copper, treat the supply-gap as closer to SETTLED. If China formally raises or abandons its 45 Mt aluminum cap, re-rate the whole aluminum supply narrative.

## Caveats
- **Fast-moving data:** All 2025–2026 prices, premiums, TC/RCs, and company output figures change constantly; treat specific numbers as of the dates cited and re-verify before any conversation. Copper and aluminum both set records in this window amid tariff-driven distortions, so "record high" claims are time-stamped, not permanent.
- **Conflicting figures:** Mine-level output varies by source depending on capacity vs actual, attributable share vs 100%, and calendar vs fiscal year. Company production ranks (Codelco vs BHP for #1) are close and definition-dependent. The 2025 disruptions at Grasberg and Kamoa-Kakula materially cut their 2025 output versus 2024 records.
- **Energy-intensity numbers:** Hall-Héroult figures are quoted variously as the electrolysis-only step (~13–15 kWh/kg) or with upstream steps; theoretical minimum ~6.2 kWh/kg. Always state your basis.
- **Forward-looking claims:** Supply-gap forecasts, green-premium durability, and inert-anode timelines are projections/aspirations, not facts — the curriculum's epistemic tags exist precisely to keep these honest.
- **Not investment advice:** Company and price references are for conversational fluency, not recommendations.
