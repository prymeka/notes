# German Self-Study Curriculum: Verified Resource Report (A0 → Balanced B2)

## TL;DR
- **The strongest self-study spine is Schubert Verlag's Begegnungen (A1+→B1+) → Erkundungen (B2), backed by Hammer's German Grammar and Usage (6th ed., Durrell, Routledge, 2016, ISBN 978-1138853713) as the English-language reference and the free Deutsche Welle "Nicos Weg" (A1–B1) course** — this stack is built for disciplined adult autodidacts and exploits all three of the learner's accelerators (English cognate density, Slavic case/gender intuition, Frankfurt immersion).
- **For the balanced-B2 goal, the right certificate is the Goethe-Zertifikat B2 (four independently bookable/retakable modules, 2019 modular format, offered at the Goethe-Institut Frankfurt) or telc Deutsch B2 (telc gGmbH is headquartered at Bleichstraße 1, 60313 Frankfurt am Main)** — the resident Integrationskurs/DTZ path caps at B1 and is not sufficient for a B2 target.
- **Frankfurt immersion is the single greatest asset for reaching symmetric spoken+reading B2.** German is FSI Category II — the FSI's current published table lists ~36 weeks / ~900 class hours (older FSI charts list German separately at ~750 hrs / 30 weeks) for diplomatic learners to reach roughly upper-B2/low-C1 (ILR S-3/R-3). Combined with Slavic case intuition and high English–German cognate density, this should compress the productive timeline; a realistic self-study band to balanced B2 is roughly 1,000–1,500 total hours.

## Key Findings
1. **Schubert Verlag (Begegnungen/Erkundungen)** is the canonical self-study DaF spine; **Hammer's/Durrell** is the definitive English reference grammar; **Schubert A/B/C-Grammatik** and **Dreyer-Schmitt** are the graded exercise grammars.
2. **Deutsche Welle is the best free national-language resource in the world** for this profile — full free courses A1–B1 (Nicos Weg, Deutsch Interaktiv) plus B2/C1 news/audio series, all free.
3. **The VHS-Lernportal** — a platform of the Deutscher Volkshochschul-Verband e.V. (DVV), funded federally under the Nationale Dekade für Alphabetisierung und Grundbildung 2016–2026, and recognized by the BAMF as the only digital offering officially approved as a course-carrying Lehrwerk (36 Lektionen, ~3,400 exercises for A1–B1; free, A1–C1) — and **Schubert-Verlag's free online exercises** are additional free pillars.
4. **Certification:** Goethe-Zertifikat B2 or telc Deutsch B2 — both available in Frankfurt; DTZ caps at B1.
5. **Contrastive leverage is real and central:** English and German are both West Germanic and share a large proportion of vocabulary (commonly cited at ~60%, far above English–French at ~27%); the main hurdles are word order and case/gender, not vocabulary. The learner's Polish/Russian case-and-gender intuition transfers directly to German's four cases, gender, adjective declension, and final devoicing.

### 1. Core Coursebook / Method Series (Lehrwerke)

**Schubert Verlag — Begegnungen (A1+, A2+, B1+) and Erkundungen (B2, C1) — TOP RECOMMENDATION for this learner.**
- Authors: Anne Buscha, Szilvia Szita (with Susanne Raven for higher levels). Publisher: Schubert Verlag (Leipzig).
- Begegnungen A1+ ISBN 978-3929526868; B1+ ISBN 978-3941323209. Integrated Kurs- und Arbeitsbuch (course + workbook in one), with an embedded answer key (Lösungsteil) and online audio — explicitly designed for self-study. Begegnungen's three levels are pitched "plus" (A1+, A2+, B1+), so completing B1+ gives a seamless transition to B2 with no bridge course; Schubert's own Sprachkurs (B2/C1/C2 = Erkundungen) connects directly.
- Verdict: the most self-learner-friendly major DaF series — systematic grammar, integrated answer keys, adult/professional content. Best fit for an analytical autodidact.

**Hueber — Menschen (A1–B1).** Handlungsorientiert (action-oriented), storytelling-led, strong media integration; designed for the classroom and BAMF-approved (as "Menschen hier") for Integrationskurse. Good input but classroom-dependent; weaker as a standalone self-study grammar spine.

**Hueber — Schritte international neu / Schritte plus neu (A1–B1).** The standard Integrationskurs workhorse (used at many VHS including locally); six volumes mapping to the six course modules. Classroom-oriented.

**Klett — Netzwerk neu (A1–B1).** Complete new edition; guides to A1/A2/B1, exam-prep integrated, strong digital offering. Solid, modern, classroom-leaning.

**Klett — Aspekte neu (B1+, B2, C1).** The standard Mittelstufe series; Band 1 = B1+, Band 2 = B2, Band 3 = C1. Prepares for Goethe-Zertifikat B2, telc Deutsch B2/C1, ÖSD. Note: Klett's newer **Kontext** (B1+→C1) is the successor to Aspekte, built around the CEFR Companion Volume (mediation etc.). Either works as a B2 classroom-style coursebook.

**Hueber — Sicher! / Sicher! aktuell (B1+, B2, C1).** Mittelstufe series; B2 split into B2.1/B2.2. Exam-oriented. Good alternative to Aspekte neu at B2.

**Cornelsen — Studio [21] / studio express (A1–B1).** Competent, classroom-oriented. Lower priority for this learner vs. Schubert.

**Klett — Linie 1 (A1–B1)** and **DaF kompakt neu (A1–B1 in one volume, Klett).** DaF kompakt neu is aimed at fast learners/academic adults (compresses A1–B1), which suits this profile as an accelerated alternative to Begegnungen if he wants speed.

*Currency note:* DaF Lehrwerke are frequently revised as "neu" editions — always buy the "neu"/current edition (Netzwerk **neu**, Schritte **plus neu**, Sicher! **aktuell**). Schubert's Begegnungen/Erkundungen are stable and current.

### 7. Pronunciation & Phonetics
**Genuine German difficulties (with L1 interference notes for this learner):**
- **ich-Laut [ç] vs. ach-Laut [x]** — rule-governed: ich-Laut after front vowels (e, i, ä, ö, ü) and consonants; ach-Laut after back vowels (a, o, u, au). English speakers wrongly use the "loch" sound everywhere. **Polish helps:** Polish "ś/ć/ź" and "ch" give him articulatory reference points closer than English has.
- **Uvular R [ʁ] vs. English R** — English R interferes strongly; German uses uvular/vocalized R. **Polish/Russian trilled/alveolar R also differs**, so he must learn the German uvular R fresh, but he is not anchored to the English retroflex.
- **Umlaut vowels ü [y] and ö [ø]** — absent in English; **Polish lacks them too**, so this is genuinely new (though Russian has some closer approximants).
- **Phonemic vowel length (long/short)** — meaning-distinguishing (Staat vs. Stadt); needs explicit drilling.
- **Final devoicing (Auslautverhärtung)** — **Polish and Russian both have final devoicing**, so this comes essentially for free (Rad/Rat homophony is intuitive to him). A clear accelerator.
- **Glottal stop before initial vowels** — German inserts it before word-initial vowels; Slavic/English speakers under-produce it.
- **Letter-sound values:** z = [ts], w = [v], v = [f], sch = [ʃ], sp-/st- = [ʃp]/[ʃt] word-initially, ei = [aɪ] vs. ie = [iː], eu/äu = [ɔʏ]. **Polish orthography is also phonemic**, so he adapts quickly to German's regular spelling-to-sound.
- **Word stress** — first-syllable default; separable prefixes stressed (ánkommen), inseparable prefixes unstressed (bekómmen); loanword stress varies.

**Resources:**
- **Hueber "Phonetik aktuell" / "Phonothek intensiv"** — the standard DaF pronunciation trainer with audio.
- **DW pronunciation materials** (within its free courses).
- **IPA-based guides** — Hammer's/Durrell has a pronunciation chapter; the **Duden Aussprachewörterbuch (Duden Band 6)** is the authoritative pronunciation reference.
- Flag: most resources are English-L1-oriented; his Slavic L1 means he should skip drills on final devoicing and focus effort on ü/ö, uvular R, and the glottal stop.

### 8. English→German and Slavic→German Leverage / Contrastive Layer

**(a) Cognates and false friends.** English–German cognate density is very high — both are West Germanic and share a large share of vocabulary (commonly cited at roughly 60%, versus ~27% between English and French). The main hurdles are word order and case/gender, not vocabulary. **Major false friends (falsche Freunde) to over-drill:**
- **bekommen** = to get/receive (NOT become; "become" = werden) — the most destructive one.
- **Gift** = poison (NOT gift; gift = Geschenk).
- **also** = so/therefore (NOT also; "also" = auch).
- **aktuell** = current/up-to-date (NOT actual; "actual" = eigentlich/tatsächlich).
- **eventuell** = possibly/perhaps (NOT eventually; "eventually" = schließlich/letztlich).
- **sensibel** = sensitive (NOT sensible; "sensible" = vernünftig).
- **Chef** = boss (NOT chef; chef = Koch).
- **Rat** = advice/council (NOT rat = Ratte).
- **Kind** = child (NOT kind = nett/Art).
- **Handy** = mobile phone.
- **Rock** = skirt.
- **See** = lake (der See) / sea (die See).
- **Art** = kind/type (NOT art = Kunst).
- **bald** = soon (NOT bald = kahl/glatzköpfig).
- **fast** = almost (NOT fast = schnell).
- **Gymnasium** = grammar/academic secondary school (NOT gym = Fitnessstudio/Turnhalle).
- **Note** = grade/mark or musical note (NOT note = Notiz/Zettel).
- **spenden** = to donate (NOT spend = ausgeben).
- **Mist** = manure/rubbish/"damn" (NOT mist = Nebel).
- **brav** = well-behaved (NOT brave = mutig/tapfer).
- Others: **Fabrik** = factory (not fabric = Stoff); **Rente** = pension (not rent = Miete); **Billion** = trillion (not billion = Milliarde).

**(b) Structural traps (for an English/Slavic speaker):**
- **V2 (verb-second) word order** in main clauses and **verb-final** order in subordinate clauses — the biggest word-order hurdle.
- **Satzklammer (sentence bracket)** and **separable-verb split** (Ich rufe dich morgen an) — conjugated verb in position 2, non-finite/prefix part at clause end.
- **Four-case system** (Nominativ, Akkusativ, Dativ, Genitiv) — **direct transfer from Polish (7 cases) and Russian (6 cases)**; German's four cases are a subset his intuition already handles. Major accelerator.
- **Grammatical gender (der/die/das)** — arbitrary and must be memorized per noun; **Polish/Russian gender intuition helps with the concept but not the specific assignments** (which often differ from Slavic). Learn every noun WITH its article.
- **Adjective declension (strong/weak/mixed endings)** — the single most error-prone area for all learners; **his Slavic adjective-agreement intuition is a real advantage** here, more than for any English monolingual.
- **Two-way (Wechsel) prepositions** taking accusative (motion/direction) vs. dative (location) — Slavic prepositional-case governance gives him strong intuition.
- **Genitive** — receding in speech (replaced by von + dative colloquially) but required in formal writing.
- **Konjunktiv II** (subjunctive/conditional) — würde + infinitive and irregular forms; B1–B2 milestone.
- **Modal particles (doch, mal, ja, eben, halt, schon)** — a hallmark B2 challenge; **Russian/Polish have discourse particles (же, ведь; no/przecież)**, so the concept transfers even though the specific words must be learned.

**Contrastive resources:** There is no single canonical Slavic→German contrastive textbook in wide print for self-learners; assemble from (i) Hammer's/Durrell (English-contrastive throughout), (ii) false-friend lists (Yabla, DWDS usage notes), and (iii) his own Polish/Russian intuition applied deliberately to case/gender/agreement. Note explicitly in the curriculum where Slavic transfers (cases, agreement, devoicing, particles-as-concept) and where it doesn't (specific genders; aspect — German has none).

### 12. Germany / Frankfurt Context & Certification

**Goethe-Zertifikat B2 (Goethe-Institut) — RECOMMENDED milestone.**
- **Four modules: Lesen (~65 min), Hören (~40 min), Schreiben (75 min), Sprechen (~15 min, paired oral).** Total written portion ~3 hours 15 minutes. Each scored 0–100; **pass = 60 points / 60% per module.** Verbatim (goethe.de): "A maximum of 100 points = 100% can be attained in each module. A module is deemed to have been passed if at least 60 points or 60% can be attained."
- **Modules can be booked and taken individually, and a failed module can be retaken on its own** — you do not repeat the whole exam. Verbatim, official Durchführungsbestimmungen (Stand 1. September 2025): "Die Materialien liegen als vier Module vor. Die Module können einzeln oder in Kombination abgelegt werden." This modular flexibility is ideal for a self-learner who may hit balanced B2 in reading before speaking (or vice versa).
- Current **modular format since 2019** (01.01.2019 selected centers, 01.08.2019 worldwide; governing Durchführungsbestimmungen updated to Stand 1 September 2025).
- **Offered at the Goethe-Institut Frankfurt** (dedicated page: goethe.de/ins/de/de/m/prf/ort/fra/gzb2.cfm). Standard adult B2 fee (Germany) ~359 € digital, or ~99 € per individual module (2026 price list); ~10% discount if you attended a Goethe course within 6 months.
- Internationally recognized; suitable for university applications and professional recognition.

**telc Deutsch B2 (telc gGmbH) — strong local alternative.**
- **telc gGmbH is headquartered at Bleichstraße 1, 60313 Frankfurt am Main** — a gemeinnützige (non-profit) subsidiary of the Deutscher Volkshochschul-Verband e.V., founded 1968; "telc" = "The European Language Certificates," with exams at 3,000+ centres in 20 countries and exams assessed centrally at telc HQ. A natural local tie-in.
- Structure: a **written part** (reading, listening, language elements/grammar, writing) + an **oral part** (usually paired). Tests all four skills. Written ~2h20m; oral ~15 min with 20 min prep. **Pass = 60% in each part.**
- Not four fully independent modules like Goethe; but if you pass one part (written or oral) you may retake only the other (for telc B1/B2/C1 Hochschule). Widely recognized by employers, universities, authorities.
- Since February 2026, telc no longer mails/prints physical certificates (digital only).
- Fees typically ~€190–250 depending on center.

**TestDaF and DSH** — university-admission exams (TestDaF: standardized, B2–C1; DSH: university-run). Relevant **only if he pursues German university study.** Not needed for a general balanced-B2 goal. (Note the University of Frankfurt's DSH ~90 € is separate from Goethe.)

**ÖSD (Österreichisches Sprachdiplom)** — Austrian equivalent, recognized; less locally relevant in Frankfurt.

**Resident-oriented BAMF system:**
- **Integrationskurs** (BAMF): Basis- + Aufbausprachkurs (6 modules × 100 UE = 600 UE) + Orientierungskurs (60 UE) ≈ 660 hours; run locally by **VHS Frankfurt** and other providers. Requires eligibility/entitlement (from 2023, DTZ restricted to Integrationskurs participants).
- Final exams: **"Deutsch-Test für Zuwanderer" (DTZ)** — tests A2–B1 (**caps at B1**), developed by Goethe-Institut + telc/g.a.s.t. for BAMF; and **"Leben in Deutschland"** test (33 questions, 60 min, orientation/civics).
- **Because the DTZ caps at B1, it is NOT sufficient for a B2 goal** — for balanced B2 he needs Goethe-Zertifikat B2 or telc Deutsch B2.

**VHS Frankfurt (vhs.frankfurt.de)** — local course + exam provider: German A1–C2, Integrationskurse (BAMF), Berufssprachkurse (§45a), telc exams; online placement test and in-person/online formats. Standard A1–B1 path ≈ 600 UE over ~36 weeks (~1 year with holidays). A cost-effective way to get in-person speaking practice and structured accountability locally.

**Recommendation: Goethe-Zertifikat B2** best fits this learner because (a) its four independently bookable/retakable modules match a self-learner whose four skills may mature at different rates; (b) it is the most internationally portable; and (c) it is offered locally at the Goethe-Institut Frankfurt. **telc Deutsch B2** is an excellent, slightly cheaper, locally-headquartered alternative (and is what VHS Frankfurt administers), so if he takes VHS courses, telc is the path of least friction.

## Recommendations (staged)

**Phase 0 — MVP/fast-path diagnostic (Week 1).** Take the free DW and VHS-Lernportal placement tests and a Goethe/telc B2 model test cold. If daily Frankfurt life has given him passive vocabulary/reading, he may test into A2 reading immediately — in which case compress Phase 1. *Threshold: if he scores ≥A2 on reading, skip A1 grammar drilling and start at A2/B1.*

**Phase 1 — Foundation A1→A2 (spine: Begegnungen A1+/A2+ + DW Nicos Weg A1/A2 + Schubert free exercises + Anki).** Front-load: (i) der/die/das learned with every noun; (ii) the four cases (leverage Slavic intuition explicitly); (iii) V2 word order; (iv) pronunciation clinic on ü/ö, uvular R, glottal stop (skip final-devoicing drills — Slavic gives it free). Begin italki 1–2×/week from week 2 and attend one Frankfurt Sprachcafé/Stammtisch weekly. *Threshold to advance: comfortable A2 on a model test + 15+ min sustained conversation.*

**Phase 2 — Core B1 (spine: Begegnungen B1+ + Schubert B-Grammatik + DW Nicos Weg B1 + Dino lernt Deutsch readers + Slow German).** Drill adjective declension hard (his highest-leverage Slavic advantage), Konjunktiv II, separable verbs/Satzklammer, two-way prepositions. Add Deutsch perfekt (mittel) and nachrichtenleicht. Ramp speaking to 2–3×/week + weekly in-person. *Threshold: B1 model test pass + reading a Dino/Baumgartner reader with ease.*

**Phase 3 — B2 push, balanced (spine: Erkundungen B2 or Aspekte neu B2 + Grammatik aktiv B2/C1 + Hammer's/Durrell as reference).** Reading: transition to authentic — Schlink/Kaminer, tagesschau, then Zeit/FAZ. Listening: Easy German podcast, DW slow news → normal-speed tagesschau, ARD/ZDF Mediathek. Speaking: intensive — daily real-world use in Frankfurt + 3×/week tutoring + Stammtisch; shadowing loops. Writing: forum posts + formal emails (Goethe B2 formats) corrected via italki/langcorrect. Add modal particles (doch/mal/ja/eben/halt/schon) explicitly. *Threshold: consistent 60%+ across all four Goethe B2 modules in practice tests.*

**Phase 4 — Certification.** Book **Goethe-Zertifikat B2 at the Goethe-Institut Frankfurt** (or **telc Deutsch B2** if going through VHS Frankfurt). Use the modular structure: sit reading/listening first if those mature earlier, then writing/speaking — retaking only any module below 60%.

**Benchmarks that change the plan:** if speaking lags reading by more than one sublevel, shift budget to tutoring/immersion and reduce textbook time; if reading races ahead (likely given cognate density + Slavic case intuition), let it, and sit the Lesen module early.
