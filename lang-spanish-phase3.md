## How to use this series

### The arc of Phase 3

Phase 1 built the **indicative**; Phase 2 built the **subjunctive**. Phase 3 does two distinct jobs. First, it **completes the verb system** — the remaining compound tenses, the counterfactual (Type-3) conditional, and the archaic future subjunctive — so that by the end of Module 18 you have seen **every finite form Spanish has**. Second, and this is where most of the reading payoff lies, it shifts the center of gravity from **verb morphology** to the **syntax of complex prose**: passive and impersonal voice, verbal periphrasis, the full preposition system, the complete relative-pronoun inventory, and — above all — the **discourse connectors** that hold argumentative and literary writing together. The six modules map onto the weeks:

| Module | Grammar | Curriculum weeks |
|---|---|---|
| 16 | Passive & impersonal constructions | 41–42 |
| 17 | Verbal periphrasis | 43–44 |
| 18 | Completing the verb system: compound tenses, counterfactual conditionals, future subjunctive | 46, 47, 49, 50, 51, 60 |
| 19 | Advanced subjunctive nuances + relative pronouns | 52–53 |
| 20 | Prepositions in depth | 54–55 |
| 21 | Discourse connectors + advanced reported speech | 57–59 |

Weeks 45, 48, 56, and 61–64 are review, integration, and self-assessment; they appear under **Checkpoints**.

### The two organizing ideas of Phase 3

**One — you are finishing the paradigm.** After Phase 2 you were missing only a handful of forms: the compound conditional (*habría hablado*), the pluperfect subjunctive (*hubiera hablado*), the future perfect (*habré hablado*), the compound infinitive (*haber hablado*), and the near-extinct future subjunctive (*hablare*). Module 18 supplies them all, and with them the **complete conditional system** (Types 1–3) and the **complete probability system** (future = present guess, conditional = past guess, future perfect = recent-past guess, compound conditional = past-perfect guess). Once Module 18 closes, there is no Spanish verb form you have not met.

**Two — grammar now serves the leap from graded to authentic text.** The features in Modules 16, 17, 19, 20, and 21 are not new tenses; they are the **connective tissue of real prose**. A newspaper editorial or a literary novel is dense with passives and *se*-constructions, aspectual periphrasis, stacked relative clauses, governed prepositions, and logical connectors. These are precisely what separates B2 reading (you can decode sentences) from C1 reading (you follow an argument). This is why the reading track now moves to **authentic novels and journalism**: the grammar of this phase is what makes that possible.

A theme that runs through both ideas: **register**. Many Phase-3 features are register-marked. The *ser*-passive, *el cual*, the future subjunctive, and connectors like *no obstante* and *asimismo* are **formal/written**; the *pasiva refleja*, *que*, and *así que* are **neutral/spoken**. Reading authentic prose means reading register — noticing when a writer reaches for the formal variant and what that signals.

### How this builds on Phase 2 and feeds Phase 4

- The **pluperfect subjunctive** (Module 18) is the Phase 2 **imperfect subjunctive** (Module 12) plus the **compound architecture** (Module 2) — *hubiera* is just the imperfect subjunctive of *haber*. The payoff promised at the close of Phase 2.
- **Type-3 conditionals** (Module 18) combine that pluperfect subjunctive with the compound conditional — the counterfactual-past capstone of the conditional system begun in Phase 2.
- The **passive/state contrast** (Module 16) completes the *ser*/*estar* + participle work started in Phase 2 Module 15.
- **Feeding Phase 4:** with the full grammar in place, Phase 4 is **reading-driven** — grammar becomes *reactive*, studied only to close a gap that authentic reading exposes. Phase 3 is the last systematic grammar phase.

### Scaffolding (as established, with Phase-3 changes)

The **weekly two-session template** is unchanged. Three shifts for this phase:

- **Grammar-to-reading ratio** moves to about **30/70** — reading now dominates.
- **Reading track:** begin your **first authentic novels** — recommended order *Sin noticias de Gurb* (Mendoza, the canonical "first real novel") → *Manolito Gafotas* (Lindo, colloquial Madrid Spanish) → *Marina* (Ruiz Zafón). A powerful technique: read a **Spanish translation of a novel you already know in English** alongside. **News:** move to ***El País*** (elpais.com), starting with culture/lifestyle sections; add the **Readlang** browser extension for click-to-translate.

# Module 16 — La pasiva y las construcciones impersonales
### *(Weeks 41–42)*

Three ways Spanish removes or backgrounds the agent — and only one of them looks like the English passive.

## 16.1 The *ser*-passive *(Week 41)*

**ser + past participle (+ *por* + agent).** Here the participle is **adjectival**, so it **agrees** in gender and number with the subject — the direct continuation of the Module 15 diagnostic:

> *La casa **fue construida** en 1900.* · *Las casas **fueron construidas**.* · *El libro **fue escrito** por Cervantes.* · *Los libros **fueron escritos** por Cervantes.*

It works in any tense (*es/fue/será/ha sido/había sido construido*). But note a fact that surprises English speakers: **the *ser*-passive is comparatively uncommon in Spanish**, largely confined to formal writing and journalism, and used mainly when the agent is expressed or emphasised. Everyday Spanish prefers the active voice or the construction in §16.2.

**The action/state contrast** (completing Phase 2 Module 15): *ser* + participle is a **process/event** (*La puerta **fue abierta*** — "was opened," an action, by someone), whereas *estar* + participle is the **resultant state** (*La puerta **estaba abierta*** — "was open," a condition). Same participle, opposite meaning; the auxiliary decides.

## 16.2 The *pasiva refleja* — the default passive *(Week 42)*

**se + verb (3rd person) + subject.** The patient becomes the grammatical subject, and the verb **agrees** with it. This is the **normal** way to express a passive idea in Spanish, far outstripping the *ser*-passive:

> *Se **construyó** una casa.* / *Se **construyeron** casas.* · *Se **vende** piso.* / *Se **venden** pisos.* · *Se **habla** español.* · *Se **necesitan** camareros.*

No agent is expressed (you generally cannot add *por* + agent to a *pasiva refleja*). The signal for a reader is a plural verb agreeing with a following noun: *se venden pisos* = "flats are sold."

## 16.3 The impersonal *se* *(Week 42)*

**se + verb, always 3rd person *singular*, with no grammatical subject** — a generic "one / you / they / people":

> *Se **vive** bien aquí.* · *Se **come** muy bien en España.* · *Se **dice** que…* · *En España se **cena** tarde.* · *Aquí no se **puede** fumar.*

When the verb has a **human direct object**, the personal *a* appears and the verb stays **singular** (which also disambiguates it from a reflexive/*pasiva refleja* reading): *Se **contrató a** dos ingenieros.* *Se **ayuda a** los necesitados.*

**Distinguishing the two *se*-constructions:** with an inanimate patient that can serve as subject, you get the **agreeing** *pasiva refleja* (*se venden pisos*); with an intransitive verb or a generic statement, you get the **invariable singular** impersonal (*se vive bien*).

## 16.4 Reading & calibration

**Reading:** *se* + a 3rd-person verb is everywhere in authentic prose — decide from context whether it's **reflexive** (*se lava*), **pasiva refleja** (subject follows: *se publican libros*), or **impersonal** (generic, singular: *se trabaja mucho*); in the passive readings, the noun after the verb is usually the **subject**, not the object. **Register:** the *ser*-passive reads as **formal/journalistic**; the *pasiva refleja* and impersonal *se* are **neutral**. **Settled** — the constructions are standard, with the one genuine subtlety being singular-vs-agreeing *se* with human objects.

**Sources & work (Weeks 41–42).** Wk 41: the *pasiva con ser* section in **PSG** + **B&B** passive chapter. Wk 42: the *pasiva refleja* / *se impersonal* section; **B&B** (index "se," "passive"). **Own:** *ser*-passive agreement and its action-vs-state contrast with *estar*; the agreeing *pasiva refleja* vs the singular impersonal *se*. **Self-test:** convert *Alguien construyó la casa* into all three (*La casa fue construida / Se construyó la casa / …*) and explain which is neutral; then read a page and classify every *se*.

# Module 17 — Las perífrasis verbales
### *(Weeks 43–44)*

A **periphrasis** is an auxiliary verb + a non-finite form (gerund or infinitive) that together add **aspect** (how an action unfolds) or **modality** (obligation, probability). You met one already — *estar* + gerundio (Phase 1 Module 7). Here is the rest of the system, all high-frequency in real prose.

## 17.1 Gerund-based periphrasis — aspect *(Week 43)*

Built on the gerund (formation as in Phase 1 Module 7, including *i→y* and the vowel-raising verbs):

- **seguir / continuar + gerundio** — "keep / continue doing": *Sigue **lloviendo**. Continúa **trabajando**.*
- **llevar + [time] + gerundio** — "have been doing for [time]" (this is how Spanish renders the English present-perfect continuous — very high value): *Llevo dos años **estudiando** español. ¿Cuánto tiempo llevas **viviendo** aquí?*
- **ir + gerundio** — gradual progression, "to be gradually doing": *Va **mejorando**. Poco a poco voy **entendiendo**.*
- **venir + gerundio** — "have been doing (up to now)": *Vengo **diciéndolo** desde hace años.*
- **andar + gerundio** — "to go around doing" (often pejorative): *Anda **diciendo** tonterías.*
- **acabar / terminar + gerundio** — "to end up doing": *Acabó **aceptando**.*

## 17.2 Infinitive-based periphrasis — aspect & modality *(Week 44)*

- **ir a + infinitive** — near future / intention: *Voy a **estudiar**.*
- **acabar de + infinitive** — "to have just done": *Acabo de **llegar**.*
- **volver a + infinitive** — "to do again": *Volví a **leerlo**.*
- **dejar de + infinitive** — "to stop doing": *Dejé de **fumar**.*
- **empezar / comenzar a + infinitive** — "to start doing": *Empezó a **llover**.*
- **ponerse a + infinitive** — "to set about / suddenly start": *Se puso a **llorar**.*
- **echarse a + infinitive** — "to burst into": *Se echó a **reír**.*
- **llegar a + infinitive** — "to manage to / go so far as to": *Llegó a **ser** presidente.*
- **estar a punto de + infinitive** — "to be about to": *Estoy a punto de **salir**.*

**The obligation/probability set — and one contrast to pin:**

- **tener que + infinitive** — obligation, "have to": *Tengo que **irme**.*
- **hay que + infinitive** — impersonal obligation, "one must": *Hay que **trabajar**.*
- **deber + infinitive** — obligation, "should/must": *Debes **estudiar**.*
- **deber de + infinitive** — **probability/supposition**, "must (be)": *Deben de **ser** las tres.*

The **deber vs deber de** distinction — obligation without *de*, probability with *de* — is worth knowing precisely, even though speakers increasingly blur it. *Debes estudiar* ("you must study") ≠ *Debes de estar cansado* ("you must be tired [I infer]").

## 17.3 Reading & calibration

**Reading:** read each periphrasis as a **single verbal idea** carrying aspect or modality — *llevo tres años trabajando* is one concept ("I've been working for three years"), not two clauses; recognise *acabar de* (just done) and *volver a* (done again) instantly. **Settled**, with *deber/deber de* the one prescriptively-distinct pair. (Clitics attach as with any auxiliary + non-finite: *Voy a **hacerlo*** = *Lo voy a hacer*; *Sigo **haciéndolo*** = *Lo sigo haciendo*.)

**Sources & work (Weeks 43–44).** Wk 43: the *perífrasis con gerundio* section in **PSG** + **B&B** (index "periphrasis," "gerund"). Wk 44: the *perífrasis con infinitivo* section. **Own:** *llevar* + gerundio for duration; the aspectual gerund periphrases; the *deber/deber de* contrast. **Self-test:** render "I've been studying Spanish for two years," "she's gradually improving," "he's just left," "they must be about forty" as periphrases; then read a page and mark each periphrasis with its meaning.

# Module 18 — Completando el sistema verbal: tiempos compuestos, condicionales irreales y futuro de subjuntivo
### *(Weeks 46, 47, 49, 50, 51, 60)*

The morphological core of Phase 3. This module supplies every remaining verb form and, with them, the complete conditional and probability systems. When it closes, you have seen the entire Spanish verb.

## 18.1 The compound conditional *(Week 46)*

**Conditional of *haber* + participle:** *habría, habrías, habría, habríamos, habríais, habrían* + participle.

> *habría hablado, habrías comido, habrían vuelto*

**Uses:** "would have done" — the hypothetical past result (the main clause of Type-3 conditionals, §18.3); probability about a past-before-past event; and future-perfect-in-the-past in reported speech (*Dijo que **habría terminado** para el lunes*).

## 18.2 The pluperfect subjunctive *(Week 47)*

**Imperfect subjunctive of *haber* + participle** — *hubiera* is simply the imperfect subjunctive of *haber*, so this is Phase 2 Module 12 plus the compound architecture:

| | *haber* (imperf. subj.) | + participle |
|---|---|---|
| yo | hubiera / hubiese | + hablado |
| tú | hubieras / hubieses | |
| él | hubiera / hubiese | |
| nosotros | hubiéramos / hubiésemos | |
| vosotros | hubierais / hubieseis | |
| ellos | hubieran / hubiesen | |

Both **-ra and -se** as always. **Uses:** completed action in a past subjunctive context (sequence of tenses — *No creía que **hubieras venido***); *como si* + pluperfect subjunctive (*Me miró como si **hubiera visto** un fantasma*); and the *si*-clause of Type-3 conditionals.

## 18.3 Conditional sentences, Type 3 — and the complete conditional system *(Week 49)*

**si + pluperfect subjunctive + compound conditional**, for conditions **contrary to past fact**:

> *Si **hubiera sabido**, **habría venido**.* ("If I had known [but I didn't], I would have come.") · *Si **hubieras estudiado**, **habrías aprobado**.*

**Mixed conditionals** cross the time frames: a past condition with a **present** result (*Si **hubiera estudiado** medicina, ahora **sería** médico*), or a present condition with a **past** result (*Si **fuera** más cuidadoso, no **habría cometido** ese error*). Note that in the main clause the **-ra pluperfect subjunctive may replace the compound conditional**, especially in speech and literary prose: *Si hubiera sabido, **hubiera** venido*.

This completes the conditional system begun in Phase 2:

| Type | Condition | *si*-clause | Main clause | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1 — real** | likely | *si* + present **indicative** | present / future / imperative | *Si llueve, me quedo.* |
| **2 — hypothetical** | contrary to present | *si* + **imperfect subjunctive** | conditional simple | *Si tuviera dinero, viajaría.* |
| **3 — counterfactual** | contrary to past | *si* + **pluperfect subjunctive** | compound conditional | *Si hubiera sabido, habría venido.* |

## 18.4 The future perfect — and the complete probability system *(Week 50)*

**Future of *haber* + participle:** *habré, habrás, habrá, habremos, habréis, habrán* + participle.

> *Para mañana **habré terminado**.* · *Cuando llegues, ya **habré salido**.*

Its **probability** use completes a four-way system you began in Phase 1 (Module 6): the compound tenses conjecture about *completed* events, the simple ones about *ongoing/simple* ones —

| Form | Conjecture about | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **futuro simple** | the **present** | *Serán las tres.* ("It's probably three.") |
| **futuro compuesto** | the **recent past** | *Habrá perdido el tren.* ("He must have missed the train.") |
| **condicional simple** | the **past** | *Serían las tres cuando llegó.* |
| **condicional compuesto** | the **past-before-past** | *Ya habría salido cuando llamaste.* |

## 18.5 The compound infinitive & infinitive constructions *(Week 51)*

**haber + participle** (the infinitive of *haber*) — a completed action expressed as an infinitive, when the infinitive's action **precedes** the main verb:

> *Me alegro de **haber venido**.* · *Por **haber llegado** tarde, perdió el tren.* · *Gracias por **haberme ayudado**.* · *Después de **haber comido**…*

Contrast the simple infinitive (general/simultaneous) with the compound (prior): *Me alegro de **venir*** vs *…de **haber venido***. This is also the moment to consolidate **infinitive constructions** generally: **al + infinitive** ("on/when doing" — *Al **llegar**, la vi* = "when I arrived"), **de + infinitive** (a conditional flavour — *De **haberlo sabido**…* = "had I known"), the infinitive **as subject/noun** (*(El) **fumar** es malo*), and the infinitive after any preposition (*sin **decir** nada, antes de **salir**, para **empezar***).

## 18.6 Completing the paradigm: the future subjunctive — recognition only *(Week 60)*

The last finite form in Spanish, now essentially extinct in speech. **Formed from the imperfect-subjunctive stem** (3rd-person-plural preterite) with *-re* endings: *hablare, hablares, hablare, habláremos, hablareis, hablaren*; *tuvieron → **tuviere***, *fueron → **fuere***. You **recognise but do not produce** it. It survives in three places: **legal/administrative** language (*el que **infringiere** esta ley…*), **fixed expressions and proverbs** (*sea lo que **fuere*** = "be that as it may"; *adonde **fueres**, haz lo que **vieres*** = "when in Rome"; *venga lo que **viniere***), and **older literature**. With this form, you have now seen **every simple and compound tense in both indicative and subjunctive**, plus the imperative and the non-finite forms — the complete Spanish verb.

## 18.7 Reading & calibration

**Reading:** read the compound tenses as *haber* (in its various tenses/moods) + the familiar participle — the participle and its irregulars never change; only the auxiliary shifts. Read *si* + pluperfect subjunctive as a **past counterfactual**, and note when a future/conditional-perfect is really a **probability** rather than a time reference (*habrá salido* = "he's probably left," not "he will have left"). Read the future subjunctive as an **archaic/legal** signal. **Settled and pan-Hispanic**, with the future subjunctive the one recognition-only, register-restricted form.

**Sources & work (Weeks 46–51, 60).** The compound-tense and conditional-sentence sections in **PSG** + **B&B** (index each: "conditional perfect," "pluperfect subjunctive," "future perfect," "infinitive"); the future subjunctive via **B&B** (index "future subjunctive"). **Own:** all four remaining compound forms; the full conditional table (Types 1–3, incl. mixed); the four-way probability system; the compound infinitive; recognition of the future subjunctive. **Self-test:** conjugate *hacer* in compound conditional, pluperfect subjunctive, future perfect, and compound infinitive; build one conditional of each type plus a mixed one; and translate the four probability sentences (*Serán/Habrá/Serían/Habría…*).

# Module 19 — Matices avanzados del subjuntivo y los pronombres relativos
### *(Weeks 52–53)*

Two skills for parsing the complex clauses of authentic prose.

## 19.1 Advanced subjunctive contrasts *(Week 52)*

Beyond the clear triggers of Phase 2, several structures make finer mood distinctions:

- **el hecho de que + subjunctive** — "the fact that," which takes the subjunctive when the clause is **commented on** rather than asserted as new information: *El hecho de que **sea** difícil no cambia nada.*
- **Verbs that change meaning with mood.** *sentir que* + **subjunctive** = "to regret" (*Siento que **estés** enfermo*) vs + **indicative** = "to sense/perceive" (*Siento que algo **va** mal*). *comprender/entender que* + **subjunctive** = "to find understandable/accept" vs + **indicative** = "to realise (a fact)." And the *decir/insistir* contrast from Phase 2 (report vs command).
- **After superlatives and negatives**, an emphatic/literary subjunctive appears in the relative clause: *Es la mejor novela que **haya** leído* (emphatic) alongside factual *Es la mejor novela que **he** leído*.

The reading skill is to treat the mood as **information about the writer's stance** even in these subtle cases — subjunctive still signals non-assertion, comment, or emphasis rather than plain fact.

## 19.2 Relative pronouns in depth *(Week 53)*

Complex prose stacks relative clauses; the full inventory lets you parse them:

- **que** — the workhorse, for people and things, in defining and (with commas) non-defining clauses: *el libro **que** leí*; *mi hermano, **que** vive en Madrid…*
- **quien / quienes** — people only, chiefly after prepositions or in non-defining clauses: *la persona con **quien** hablé*; *mi jefe, **quien** es muy amable…*
- **el que / la que / los que / las que** — "the one(s) who/which," after prepositions and for emphasis or disambiguation: *el amigo del **que** te hablé*; ***los que** quieran venir…*
- **el cual / la cual / los cuales / las cuales** — more **formal**, after (especially longer) prepositions and in non-defining clauses: *la razón por **la cual**…*; *el edificio, **el cual** fue restaurado…*
- **cuyo / cuya / cuyos / cuyas** — "whose," the relative **possessive**, agreeing with the **thing possessed**, not the possessor: *el escritor **cuyas** novelas leemos*; *una casa **cuyo** tejado…* (Frequently misread — the agreement is with what follows.)
- **lo que / lo cual** — neuter, referring to a whole idea or an unspecified thing: ***Lo que** dijiste es verdad*; *Llegó tarde, **lo cual** me molestó.*
- **donde, cuando, como** as relatives: *la ciudad **donde** nací*; *el día **en que** nos conocimos*; *la manera **en que** lo hizo.*

## 19.3 Reading & calibration

**Reading:** the payoff is parsing long sentences — knowing that *cuyo* means "whose" (and agrees forward), that *lo cual* refers back to a whole clause, and that *el cual* is a formal relative after a preposition lets you keep the thread through dense subordination. **Register:** *el cual* and *cuyo* are **formal/literary**; *que* is neutral. **Settled.**

**Sources & work (Weeks 52–53).** Wk 52: the advanced *subjuntivo/indicativo* sections in **PSG** + **B&B** subjunctive chapter (finer cases). Wk 53: the *pronombres relativos* section; **B&B** relative-clause chapter. **Own:** the meaning-changing mood verbs; the full relative inventory, especially *cuyo* and *lo que/lo cual*. **Self-test:** explain the mood in *Siento que estés/está…* and *el hecho de que sea…*; and identify the relative in *la persona cuyo coche…, lo cual me sorprendió, la razón por la cual…*

# Module 20 — Las preposiciones en profundidad
### *(Weeks 54–55)*

The preposition system is a persistent source of misreadings; two weeks target the highest-yield areas.

## 20.1 *Por* vs *para* *(Week 54)*

The classic pair. The core intuition: **POR looks back to a cause, motive, means, or route; PARA looks forward to a purpose, destination, or goal.**

**POR** — cause/reason (*Lo hago **por** ti* = for your sake / because of you; *gracias **por** venir*); route/movement (*caminar **por** la calle*); duration/period (***por** la mañana*; ***por** dos horas*); exchange/price (*lo cambié **por** otro*; *pagué diez euros **por** él*); "on behalf of / in place of"; means (***por** teléfono, **por** correo*); agent in the passive (*escrito **por** Cervantes*); "per" (*cien km **por** hora*); "in search of" (*voy **por** pan*).

**PARA** — purpose/goal (*estudio **para** aprender*; ***para** ti*); destination (*salgo **para** Madrid*); deadline/future point (***para** el lunes*); recipient (*un regalo **para** ti*); opinion (***para** mí, es un error*); standard of comparison (***para** ser extranjero, habla muy bien*); employment (*trabaja **para** una empresa*).

The minimal pairs make it concrete: *Lo hago **por** ti* (because of / for your sake) vs *Lo hago **para** ti* (for you to have); *Estudio **por** la noche* (during) vs *Estudio **para** el examen* (with the exam as goal).

## 20.2 Verbs that govern a preposition *(Week 55)*

Many verbs require a specific preposition before their complement — largely **lexical**, learned per verb, but grouped for economy:

- **+ a:** *aprender a, empezar a, ayudar a, enseñar a, acostumbrarse a, atreverse a, negarse a, dedicarse a, volver a, obligar a.*
- **+ de:** *acordarse de, olvidarse de, tratar de, dejar de, depender de, quejarse de, darse cuenta de, enamorarse de, alegrarse de, arrepentirse de.*
- **+ en:** *pensar en, insistir en, confiar en, consistir en, tardar en, fijarse en, convertirse en.*
- **+ con:** *soñar con, contar con, casarse con, encontrarse con, conformarse con.*

Some verbs change meaning with the preposition: *pensar **en*** (think about) vs *pensar **de*** (have an opinion of) vs *pensar* + infinitive (intend to); *soñar **con*** (dream of).

## 20.3 Reading & calibration

**Reading:** read *por*/*para* for their **direction of logic** (cause vs goal) rather than by rote translation — it disambiguates many sentences; and let a **governed preposition** cue the construction (a verb + *de* + infinitive is a fixed frame, not a free combination). **Settled**, though a handful of governed prepositions vary regionally or are shifting.

**Sources & work (Weeks 54–55).** Wk 54: the *por/para* section in **PSG** + **B&B** (index "por," "para"). Wk 55: the verb-government sections; **B&B** prepositions chapter; ★ WordReference is excellent for checking a specific verb's preposition. **Own:** the *por/para* logic and its minimal pairs; the four governed-preposition groups; the *pensar en/de/*+inf and *soñar con* contrasts. **Self-test:** choose *por/para* in six sentences and justify by cause-vs-goal; supply the preposition for *acordarse ___, insistir ___, soñar ___, aprender ___.*

# Module 21 — Conectores del discurso y estilo indirecto avanzado
### *(Weeks 57–59)*

The reading capstone of Phase 3. Connectors are the **signposts of argument** — the single feature that most separates fluent C1 reading of journalism and essays from laborious B2 decoding. Learn to read them and you can follow, and anticipate, the logical shape of a text.

## 21.1 Connectors: contrast and consequence *(Week 57)*

- **Adversative / contrast:** *sin embargo* (however), *no obstante* (nevertheless), *en cambio* (on the other hand), *por el contrario* (on the contrary), *ahora bien* (that said), *con todo*.
- **Consecutive / result:** *por (lo) tanto* (therefore), *así que* (so), *de modo/manera que* (so that), *por consiguiente / en consecuencia* (consequently), *de ahí que* (hence — **+ subjunctive**: *De ahí que **sea** importante*), *entonces*, *pues*.

## 21.2 Connectors: cause, concession, addition, organisation *(Week 58)*

- **Causal / reason:** *porque, ya que, puesto que, dado que, debido a que, como* (clause-initial), *a causa de.*
- **Concessive:** *a pesar de que, aunque, aun así, si bien, pese a que.*
- **Additive:** *además* (moreover), *asimismo* (likewise), *es más* (what's more), *incluso* (even), *también.*
- **Organisational / reformulating:** *en primer lugar…, por un lado… por otro (lado)…, en cuanto a / respecto a* (as for), *es decir / o sea / en otras palabras* (that is), *por ejemplo, en resumen, en conclusión, finalmente.*

Reading these is a distinct skill from knowing them: a well-built editorial telegraphs its structure through connectors, so *sin embargo* warns of a coming reversal, *por lo tanto* of a conclusion, *es decir* of a restatement. Tracking them lets you hold a long argument in view.

## 21.3 Advanced reported speech *(Week 59)*

Deepening Phase 2 Module 14: **reporting verbs beyond *decir/preguntar*** — *afirmar, negar, admitir, sugerir, advertir, prometer, quejarse de que, insistir en que* — several of which govern the **subjunctive** (*sugerir que* + subj; *advertir que* + subj for a warning vs + indicative for informing). And a recognition skill for literature: **free indirect style** (*estilo indirecto libre*), where a character's thought is rendered in third-person past **without** an explicit "he said/thought," blending narrator and character voice — pervasive in fiction, and something you learn to hear rather than to build.

## 21.4 Reading & calibration

**Reading:** treat connectors as **structural markers** and read *for* them — annotate an *El País* column by underlining every connector and labelling the move (contrast / cause / result / restatement); the argument's skeleton emerges. **Register:** *no obstante, asimismo, por consiguiente* are **formal**; *pero, así que, o sea* are **neutral/spoken**. **Settled**, with *de ahí que* + subjunctive the one form to note.

**Sources & work (Weeks 57–59).** Wks 57–58: the *conectores / marcadores del discurso* sections in **PSG** + **B&B** (index "connectors," "discourse markers"); annotate authentic articles. Wk 59: advanced reported-speech sections; **B&B** reported-speech chapter. **Own:** the connector families and what each signals; the subjunctive-governing reporting verbs; recognition of free indirect style. **Self-test:** take an opinion column, mark every connector, and reconstruct the argument's outline from them alone.

# Checkpoints
### *(Weeks 45, 48, 56, 61–64)*

Consolidation and review are heavier in Phase 3 because the payoff — reading authentic text — depends on the whole system being fluent, not just newly-learned.

**Week 45 — *vosotros* systematic review.** Now that every tense and mood is known, drill the **Peninsular *vosotros*** form across **all** of them — present, all pasts, future, conditional, both subjunctives, compound tenses, and commands (*habláis, hablasteis, hablabais, hablaréis, hablaríais, habléis, hablarais/hablaseis, habéis hablado, hablad*). This is essential for reading Spanish from Spain and easily neglected.

**Week 48 — Self-Assessment 6 (compound tenses).** Redo the PSG sections for Modules 16–18. **Gate:** produce all four remaining compound forms and all three conditional types without hesitation.

**Week 56 — Self-Assessment 7 (prepositions & relatives).** Redo Modules 19–20. **Gate:** correct *por/para* and governed prepositions; parse a sentence with *cuyo*, *el cual*, and *lo que*.

**Weeks 61–63 — full verb-system integration + targeted gap-filling.** Mixed exercises across the entire verb system; return to any weak topic flagged by Self-Assessments 1–7. Whole-paradigm speed runs (conjugate a verb across every tense and mood).

**Week 64 — Self-Assessment 8 (solid B2).** **Phase-exit criteria — you should be able to:**

- form and recognise **every** Spanish verb form, simple and compound, indicative and subjunctive;
- read **10 pages of an unadapted contemporary novel** with only occasional dictionary use, parsing compound tenses, passives, and *se*-constructions without slowing;
- read an ***El País* opinion column** and **follow the argument**, including its connective logic;
- handle all conditional types, verbal periphrasis, the full relative-pronoun inventory, and *por/para*;
- read for **register**, noticing when a writer chooses the formal variant.

Meeting these means you are reading authentically and are ready for **Phase 4**, which is **reading-driven**: the systematic grammar is complete, and from here grammar is studied only reactively, to close a specific gap that real reading reveals.
