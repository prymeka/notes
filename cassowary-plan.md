# Project Plan: A Learned 3D Cassowary Prior as Synthetic Training Data for Rare-Species Detection

**Research question:** Can a category-level 3D generative model, learned from unstructured 2D photo collections (no CT scans, no CAD, no 3D ground truth), produce synthetic training data that closes the few-shot gap for a real, safety-critical wildlife detector — validated against the actual deployed CassDetect/LAARMA system?

---

## 0. Background & Motivation

The deployed reference system for this problem is **CassDetect / LAARMA** (Large Animal Activated Roadside Monitoring and Alert System), which already does a form of synthetic bootstrapping, but a shallow one: it expands OWL-ViT-detected web images via a custom label-augmentation NMS step, then self-trains on field data — 2D pseudo-labeling and augmentation of existing photos, not controllable 3D asset generation.

The wildlife-CV field has an established, competition-sanctioned tradition of using synthetic imagery for exactly this long-tail problem. Two foundational references:

1. **Beery, Liu, Morris, Piavis, Kapoor, Joshi, Wigness & Perona (2020), *Synthetic Examples Improve Generalization for Rare Classes* (WACV).** The seminal paper: <cite index="65-1">recognition algorithms struggle to learn classes with limited data, and correctly classifying rare occurrences — such as endangered species — is crucial, while simulated data, which is plentiful and comes with free annotation, had not yet been explored in the long-tailed setting</cite>. Tested on Caltech Camera Traps using 3D-animated animal models.
2. **Beery, Morris & Yang (2019/2020), the iWildCam Challenge Dataset papers (2020, 2021).** These institutionalized the idea: <cite index="59-1">the natural world is long-tailed, with rare classes observed orders of magnitude less frequently than common ones, sometimes with only handfuls of examples</cite>, and the competition rules explicitly permit <cite index="58-1">transfer learning from iNaturalist images and synthetic images generated by Microsoft's TrapCam-AirSim simulation environment</cite> to fill species gaps.

**The gap this project targets:** nobody has substituted a *learned, diffusion-distilled* category-level 3D animal prior (rather than hand-modeled 3D assets or CAD geometry) into this pipeline, and nobody has validated the result against a real, deployed, safety-critical detector for the specific species being modeled.

---

## 1. Pipeline Stage A — Learning a Controllable 3D Cassowary Prior from 2D Image Collections

This is the technically newest part of the plan and the part with the most active, fast-moving literature. The goal: given only unstructured, single-view internet/iNaturalist photos of cassowaries (no video, no multi-view rigs, no 3D scans), learn a deformable, poseable 3D shape+texture model.

| Paper | What it contributes |
|---|---|
| **Wu, Li et al., *MagicPony: Learning Articulated 3D Animals in the Wild* (CVPR 2023)** | The core method: <cite index="72-1">given a collection of single-view images of an animal category, the model learns a category-specific prior shape using an implicit-explicit representation together with a feature field that fuses self-supervised DINO-ViT correspondences via a feature-rendering loss</cite>. Directly relevant: the public MagicPony codebase was already trained on bird image collections (alongside horses, giraffes, zebras, cows), confirming the method generalizes to avian body plans, not just quadrupeds. |
| **Yao, Chen et al., *LASSIE* (NeurIPS 2022)** | Introduced the sparse-image-ensemble setting this whole line of work targets: <cite index="75-1">automatically estimating articulated 3D shapes with only 20–30 in-the-wild images of an animal species, without any image-level 2D or 3D annotations</cite> — a highly relevant regime, since cassowary photo counts will be modest. |
| **Yao et al., *Hi-LASSIE* (CVPR 2023)** | Relaxes LASSIE's requirements further — only needs one reference image with visible body parts — and demonstrates <cite index="75-1">applications like animation and motion re-targeting enabled by the discovered 3D part representation</cite>. |
| **Yang, Wang et al., *ARTIC3D* (NeurIPS 2023)** | Adds robustness to noisy, occluded, truncated web images (realistic for scraped cassowary photos) by <cite index="77-1">leveraging diffusion model priors to support reconstruction on a wider variety of input images</cite> than MagicPony/LASSIE require. |
| **Wu et al., *DOVE* (analysis-by-synthesis from video)** and **Li et al., *3D-Fauna* (CVPR 2024, pan-category quadruped model)** | Show two alternative supervision signals — motion cues from video, and a single model spanning many quadruped species — worth benchmarking against the single-species MagicPony approach. |
| **Kim et al./various, *SyncDreamer for 3D Reconstruction of Endangered Animal Species* (2023)** | The most directly analogous prior work: applies diffusion-based novel-view synthesis (SyncDreamer) plus NeRF/NeuS specifically to address <cite index="42-1">the scarcity of image data for certain endangered species, which makes it difficult to create 3D models using existing generative AI-based approaches</cite>. Tested on stork, frog, dragonfly, and tiger — not cassowary, and not fed into a detector, but methodologically the closest published precedent to this entire project. |
| **Nguyen-Phuoc/Farm3D, *Learning Articulated 3D Animals by Distilling 2D Diffusion* (2023)** | Confirms the "train on generated data only" claim you'll be relying on: <cite index="44-1">even when training solely on synthetic images, comparable results to prior work are achievable</cite> across cows, sheep, horses, pigs, and dogs. |

**Decision point for this stage:** MagicPony (mature, public code, already bird-trained) vs. Hi-LASSIE/ARTIC3D (fewer images needed, more robust to noisy web photos) is your central design choice, and depends entirely on how many usable cassowary images you can assemble from iNaturalist/GBIF.

## 2. Pipeline Stage B — Domain-Randomized Rendering (3D Asset → Synthetic Training Images)

Once you have a controllable 3D asset, you need to render it into diverse, labeled synthetic training images.

| Paper | What it contributes |
|---|---|
| **Tobin, Fong, Ray, Schneider, Zaremba & Abbeel, *Domain Randomization for Transferring Deep Neural Networks from Simulation to the Real World* (IROS 2017)** | The foundational paper for this whole stage. Core finding: <cite index="89-1">with enough variability in the simulator, the real world may appear to the model as just another variation</cite>, and they showed <cite index="90-1">a real-world object detector trained only on simulated RGB images with non-realistic random textures, without any pretraining on real images, could still transfer successfully</cite>. Everything downstream in this stage is a descendant of this result. |
| **Hybrid SDG paper, *Hybrid Synthetic Data Generation with Domain Randomization Enables Zero-Shot Vision-Based Part Inspection Under Extreme Class Imbalance* (2025)** | A modern, directly-transferable recipe: <cite index="50-1">systematically varying CAD-based part geometry, surface texture, and lighting conditions, then compositing synthetic parts onto real backgrounds to reduce domain gap</cite>, reporting <cite index="51-1">a two-stage YOLOv8n + MobileNetV3 pipeline trained on 12,960 synthetic images generated in one hour</cite>. Good template for your rendering/compositing pipeline even though the domain is industrial parts, not animals. |
| **MegaPose / FoundationPose / SAM-6D family (6D pose estimation via render-and-compare)** | Relevant design pattern: <cite index="54-1">using geometry to constrain or verify recognition under real deployment conditions, not just making synthetic images look more realistic</cite> — i.e., the 3D asset itself is doing work at inference time, not only during training. Worth reading if you want to explore pose-aware detection rather than pure appearance-based detection. |
| **NeRF-based industrial digital-twin defect pipeline (2024)** | A worked example of the full loop — NeRF-reconstruct a real object, then generate synthetic defect/variant data from the mesh — structurally identical to what you're proposing for a cassowary, just in a factory-inspection context. Reports concrete YOLO mAP gains (1.5–18.8 points) from the synthetic-augmented training set, giving you a plausible benchmark for expected effect size. |

## 3. Pipeline Stage C — Wildlife-Specific Precedent & Baselines to Beat

These are the methods your 3D-generative approach needs to outperform in ablation, since they represent the current state of the art for synthetic wildlife data:

| Paper | What it contributes / why it's a baseline |
|---|---|
| **Beery et al., *Synthetic Examples Improve Generalization for Rare Classes* (WACV 2020)** — see above | The 3D-animated-model baseline; closest prior art directly targeting this problem. |
| **Copy-paste augmentation for camera traps (2024)** | Simple, strong baseline: <cite index="67-1">isolating animal segments from existing images and pasting them onto novel backgrounds improved species identification in unseen locations by 8% ± 2%</cite>. Critically, it also reports the failure mode most relevant to you: <cite index="62-1">mixed results when using copy-paste augmentation on models trained with very small numbers of images (1–8 per species)</cite> — likely your exact real-data regime. |
| **Style-migration augmentation for few-shot rare wildlife classification (2023)** | A GAN-based alternative to both copy-paste and full 3D rendering; useful as a second baseline, and the paper is explicit that <cite index="64-1">manually designing augmentation rules is labor-intensive and traditional GANs struggle with fine-grained small-sample classification of similar classes</cite> — a limitation your approach should be positioned against. |
| **DisCL — Difficulty-sensitive contrastive learning for long-tailed recognition with synthetic camera trap data (2024)** | A recent, more sophisticated synthetic-data method for the same long-tail camera-trap problem; a good "modern SOTA baseline" comparison point. |
| **iWildCam 2020/2021 Challenge Dataset papers** — see above | Source of the TrapCam-AirSim synthetic-image baseline, and the long-tail species-gap evaluation protocol your project should mirror. |

## 4. Pipeline Stage D — Few-Shot Object Detection (your existing expertise plugs in here)

This is where your prior few-shot detection research directly informs methodology and evaluation design. The synthetic data isn't the end product — it's the *source domain* for a few-shot detector that will be fine-tuned/validated on tiny amounts of real cassowary data (CassDetect's field images).

| Paper | What it contributes |
|---|---|
| **Wang, Huang, Darrell, Gonzalez & Yu, *Frustratingly Simple Few-Shot Object Detection (TFA)* (ICML 2020)** | The strong, simple baseline nearly every later paper compares against: <cite index="80-1">a two-stage fine-tuning approach where the feature extractor is trained on base classes, then only the box predictor is fine-tuned on a balanced subset of base + novel classes</cite>, improving rare-class (<10 images) AP by ~4 points on LVIS. Almost certainly your starting architecture. |
| **Yan, Chen, Xu, Wang, Liang & Lin, *Meta R-CNN* (CVPR 2019)** | Meta-learning approach to few-shot detection; a useful architectural contrast to TFA's simpler fine-tuning strategy. |
| **Qiao, Zhao, Li, Qiu, Wu & Zhang, *DeFRCN* (ICCV 2021)** | Decouples the RPN and classifier gradients in Faster R-CNN specifically to fix few-shot overfitting; reports <cite index="83-1">a 13.6% improvement over MPSR on a cross-domain COCO→VOC few-shot detection setting</cite> — directly relevant since your setup is also cross-domain (synthetic renders → real photos). |
| **Sun, Li, Cai, Yuan & Zhang, *FSCE* (CVPR 2021)** | Contrastive proposal encoding to address the classification-confusion failure mode common in few-shot detectors — worth including in your ablation grid. |
| **Zhang & Wang, *Hallucination Improves Few-Shot Object Detection* (CVPR 2021)** | Directly relevant precedent for "generate additional feature-space examples to help few-shot detection" — a lighter-weight cousin of your full 3D-rendering idea, useful as a mid-complexity baseline. |
| **Applied Intelligence (2023), *A Broader Study of Cross-Domain Few-Shot Object Detection*** | Explicitly studies the exact regime you're in — generalizing <cite index="78-1">both from one natural-image domain to another, and from a natural-image domain to a visually dissimilar one</cite> — good source of evaluation protocol and metrics design. |

**Why this stage matters for your pitch:** the interesting research question isn't "does synthetic data help" (answered: yes, repeatedly, since 2020) — it's "does a *learned 3D generative* source domain beat 2D copy-paste / GAN style-transfer as the pretraining source for few-shot fine-tuning," evaluated with a real few-shot detection method (TFA/DeFRCN) at varying k-shot budgets against real field data.

## 5. Validation Target — CassDetect / LAARMA

Your evaluation set is real and already published: **Li, Shan, Berrio Perez, Luo & Worrall, *Endangered Alert: A Field-Validated Self-Training Scheme for Detecting and Protecting Threatened Wildlife on Roads and Roadsides* (arXiv 2412.12222)**, with code at `github.com/acfr/CassDetect`.

Key numbers to design your evaluation around:
- Initial model trained on synthetic + web data alone reached only 4.2% true-positive rate at 0–100m; after field data accrued, this rose to 78.5% — this is your target performance curve to compare against with a better synthetic prior.
- Reported training set: 89 object-centric RGB images of cassowaries sourced from the internet and 7 thermal images — genuinely few-shot scale, meaning any real k-shot fine-tuning experiment you run is directly comparable to their reported regime.
- Field deployment stats (five months, Kuranda): over 60,000 vehicles observed, only 17 cassowaries per week on average — this is the extreme class-imbalance number to cite when motivating why synthetic augmentation matters at all.
- Final field precision/recall: 0.77 / 0.97.

**Proposed use:** treat CassDetect's own reported training images as your "real-shot" pool for k-shot fine-tuning experiments, and their field-deployment detections (or a held-out slice of their described data, if accessible via the repo) as your real-world test set — this makes your result directly comparable to a deployed system's numbers rather than an internal benchmark you invented.

## 6. Experimental Design (ablation matrix)

**Source domain (what you pretrain the detector on):**
1. Real photos only (iNaturalist/GBIF), no synthetic augmentation — lower bound
2. Copy-paste augmented real photos — Beery-style / 2024 copy-paste baseline
3. GAN style-migration augmented real photos — 2023 baseline
4. Domain-randomized renders of your learned MagicPony/Hi-LASSIE 3D cassowary asset — your proposed method
5. (4) + a small amount of real photos mixed in during pretraining — likely best performer, worth including

**Detection method (how you use the source domain):**
- TFA (simple, strong baseline)
- DeFRCN (better under cross-domain shift — probably your main result)

**Evaluation:**
- k-shot fine-tuning curve (k = 0, 1, 5, 10, 20 real CassDetect-style images) — mirrors the cross-domain FSOD evaluation protocols above
- Final metric: mAP / precision-recall against CassDetect's real field detections, directly comparable to their reported 0.77 / 0.97 and their range-binned true-positive rates

---

## 7. Risks, Honest Caveats, and Open Questions

- **The domain gap may not close.** Even sophisticated synthetic sources show a real ceiling: copy-paste augmentation's own paper reports unreliable gains specifically in the 1–8-real-image regime — you should design your experiment to detect this failure mode rather than assume it away.
- **3D reconstruction quality risk.** MagicPony/LASSIE-family methods were built and validated on quadrupeds and horses/birds in relatively "friendly" pose distributions; a cassowary's casque, neck posture, and unusual silhouette may stress the shape prior in ways not tested in the original papers — worth a qualitative check before investing in the full rendering pipeline.
- **No ground-truth 3D cassowary data to validate reconstruction fidelity against** (no cassowary CT scan confirmed yet on MorphoSource) — you're validating the *downstream detector*, not the 3D reconstruction itself, which is an important scoping decision to state up front.
- **CassDetect's exact training image set** may not be fully redistributable/downloadable from the repo — confirm data availability before finalizing the evaluation design.

---

## 8. Suggested Milestones

1. Assemble and clean a cassowary image collection (iNaturalist/GBIF, filtered for captive/cultivated tags) — feeds Stage A.
2. Train/fit a MagicPony or Hi-LASSIE model on the cassowary collection; qualitatively validate reconstructions.
3. Build the domain-randomized rendering pipeline (Blender/Isaac-style) producing labeled synthetic detection data.
4. Reproduce the 2020–2024 wildlife baselines (copy-paste, style-migration) on the same real data split, for fair comparison.
5. Run the k-shot fine-tuning ablation (TFA and DeFRCN) across all five source-domain conditions.
6. Evaluate against CassDetect's reported field numbers and/or repo test data.
7. Write up the domain-gap failure analysis regardless of outcome — this is often the most valuable part of the talk.
