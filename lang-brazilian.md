# Brazilian Portuguese for a Spanish-Leveraged Adult Self-Learner: An Annotated Curriculum and Phased Study Plan

## TL;DR
- **Anchor your curriculum on three books and one contrastive spine:** Simões, *Pois Não: Brazilian Portuguese Course for Spanish Speakers* (University of Texas Press, 2008, ISBN 9780292717817) as your Spanish→BP bridge; Whitlam & Silveira, *Modern Brazilian Portuguese Grammar: A Practical Guide* (Routledge, 3rd ed., 2023, ISBN 9781032244334) plus its Workbook as your working grammar; and Perini, *Modern Portuguese: A Reference Grammar* (Yale, 2002, ISBN 9780300091557) as your descriptive deep-reference. These three cover the entire verb system (including the future subjunctive and inflected infinitive that trip up Spanish speakers) and explicitly flag where Portuguese diverges from Spanish.
- **Your Spanish is a large accelerator, not a shortcut:** Spanish and Portuguese share an 89% lexical-similarity coefficient per Ethnologue's Swadesh-style wordlist comparison (the highest among major Romance pairs), so reading access comes fast — but that same overlap generates false friends and "portunhol" interference. The highest-value early work is deliberately memorizing ~50–100 false friends and un-learning a handful of grammar reflexes (future subjunctive, personal infinitive, BP proclisis, *estar + gerúndio*, ser/estar/ficar boundaries).
- **A reading-first MVP is realistic in ~8–12 weeks;** full reading/listening comprehension with functional conversation is a multi-year project. Prioritize BP throughout, with a thin but deliberate European Portuguese (EP) awareness sub-track (Practice Portuguese app + Hutchinson/Lloyd EP grammar) so you can read and understand EP material without diluting your BP core.

## Key Findings

**The Spanish base changes the optimal curriculum.** Standard "learn Portuguese" advice is written for English speakers starting near zero. For a B1–B2 Castilian speaker, the binding constraint is not vocabulary acquisition but (a) neutralizing interference and (b) retraining specific grammar reflexes. This is why the single most important resource is a *contrastive* one — Simões's *Pois Não* — which is explicitly built to teach "one year of college Portuguese in one semester" to Spanish speakers, contrasting the two languages point by point. No English-first textbook does this.

**All tenses/moods are fully covered by the recommended stack.** The Whitlam grammar and Perini reference between them cover present, pretérito perfeito, pretérito imperfeito, both pluperfects (compound *tinha feito* and synthetic *-ra* *fizera*), all futures/conditionals, present/imperfect/**future subjunctive**, imperative, gerund, and past participles including double participles (*pego/pegado*, *aceito/aceitado*). The future subjunctive (*futuro do subjuntivo*) is the critical one: it is alive and obligatory in Portuguese (*se você quiser*, *quando eu puder*, *enquanto houver*) where Spanish uses present subjunctive or indicative. Verb-form drilling is best offloaded to *501 Portuguese Verbs* (Barron's, 3rd ed.) and the free online conjugator Conjuga-me.

**The BP↔EP divergence is real but asymmetric for your goals.** For *reading*, the two varieties are ~98% orthographically unified after the 1990 Acordo Ortográfico (up from ~96% previously) and highly mutually intelligible; the gap is modest. For *listening*, the gap is large — EP vowel reduction makes it sound very different from BP. For *grammar*, the visible divergences are: clitic placement (BP colloquial proclisis "me dá" vs EP enclisis "dá-me", with mesoclisis "dar-te-ei" as very formal EP only), present continuous (*estar + gerúndio* in BP vs *estar a + infinitivo* in EP), and address (BP *você* default + *a gente*; EP living *tu*). Prioritize BP; keep EP as a recognition-only sub-track.

**The prescriptive/spoken gap in BP is unusually wide** — "almost two grammars." Written BP follows European-style prescriptive rules (enclisis, *nós* + agreement, third-person clitics *o/a*); spoken BP uses proclisis by default, *a gente* + 3rd-singular for "we," *você/cê*, tonic *ele* as object ("vi ele"), and largely drops the *o/a* object clitics. A reader needs the prescriptive system; a listener/speaker needs the colloquial one. Perini is the best single guide to the spoken reality; Whitlam explicitly emphasizes contemporary usage.

## Details

### Category A — Contrastive Spanish→Portuguese (your spine)
- **PRIMARY — Antônio R. M. Simões, *Pois Não: Brazilian Portuguese Course for Spanish Speakers, with Basic Reference Grammar*.** University of Texas Press, revised ed., 2008. ISBN 9780292717817. ~571 pp. Contrasts BP with Spanish throughout, includes answer keys, and offers **free downloadable audio** (native BP speakers) via the UT Austin box link. This is the keystone: it exploits your overlap while systematically flagging false friends and divergent grammar. *Sequencing rationale: comes first because it is the only resource optimized for exactly your starting point; everything else supplements it.*
- **SUPPLEMENTARY — Free false-friends lists.** The Aporte Services list ("Más de 350 Falsos Amigos") and the Spanish-Wikipedia *Anexo:Falsos amigos* are free and immediately usable for building an Anki deck. For a scholarly reference: Feijóo Hoyos & Hoyos-Andrade, *Diccionario de falsos amigos (español-portugués/português-espanhol)* (Luso-Española de Ediciones, 2011, ISBN 978-84-9375-277-4). *Rationale: false friends are the single highest-ROI early vocabulary task; front-load them.*
- **SUPPLEMENTARY — Simões, *Spanish and Brazilian Portuguese Pronunciation*** (Springer, 2019, ISBN 9789811319952). Contrastive phonetics for Spanish speakers; more academic, use selectively.

### Category B — Comprehensive BP course textbooks (integrated skills)
- **PRIMARY — John Whitlam & Agripino S. Silveira, *Modern Brazilian Portuguese Grammar: A Practical Guide*.** Routledge, 3rd ed., 2023. ISBN 9781032244334 (pbk); 9781032244396 (hbk). Part A = traditional categories; Part B = function-based. The 2nd edition onward includes "Notes for Spanish speakers pointing out the main grammatical differences." Pair with the **Workbook** (2nd ed. ISBN 9780415566469; a 3rd-edition workbook accompanies the 2023 grammar). BP-exclusive, contemporary-usage focus. *Rationale: your day-to-day working grammar and exercise engine.*
- **PRIMARY (self-study spine) — *Colloquial Portuguese of Brazil: The Complete Course for Beginners*** (Routledge, Colloquial series). Current beginners edition by **Viviane Gontijo**, ISBN 9780415743969; reaches CEFR A1–A2. **Free native-speaker audio** at routledge.com/cw/colloquials. Follow-on: *Colloquial Portuguese of Brazil 2* (Osborne & McIntyre, ISBN 9781138960145). *Rationale: structured, answer-keyed, audio-backed — ideal for solo pacing before you graduate to native material.*
- **SUPPLEMENTARY — *Ponto de Encontro: Portuguese as a World Language*** (Jouet-Pastre et al., Pearson, 2nd ed., ISBN 9780205782765). The standard US college text; uniquely offers parallel BP/EP tracks (content ~75% BP) and is updated for the 1990 Acordo. Good for the EP-awareness sub-track. Heavy/expensive; optional.
- **SUPPLEMENTARY — *Complete Brazilian Portuguese* (Sue Tyson-Ward, Teach Yourself/Hodder, 2010, ISBN 9781444104165).** Reaches ~B1. A gentler self-study alternative to Colloquial.

### Category C — Reference grammars (comprehensive)
- **PRIMARY (BP, English-medium) — Mário A. Perini, *Modern Portuguese: A Reference Grammar*.** Yale University Press, 2002. ISBN 9780300091557. Written by a linguist; descriptive (documents real educated BP usage, spoken vs written), strong on subjunctive, articles, preterite vs imperfect. *Rationale: your "why does BP actually do this" reference; complements the more practical Whitlam.*
- **PRIMARY (authoritative, Portuguese-medium, both norms) — Celso Cunha & Lindley Cintra, *Nova Gramática do Português Contemporâneo*.** Lexikon, 7th ed., 2016/2017, updated to the new orthography. The prestige reference grammar of the *norma culta*, with systematic attention to EP↔BP differences. In Portuguese — use once you can read comfortably. *Rationale: the definitive advanced reference; also serves the EP-awareness track since it treats both norms.*
- **SUPPLEMENTARY (EP-focused, English-medium) — Hutchinson, Lloyd & Sousa, *Portuguese: An Essential Grammar*.** Routledge, 3rd ed., 2018, ISBN 9781138234352. The 3rd edition is European-Portuguese-focused and reflects the orthographic agreement. *Rationale: your compact EP reference for the awareness sub-track.*

### Category D — Verb references / conjugation
- **PRIMARY — John J. Nitti & Michael J. Ferreira, *501 Portuguese Verbs*.** Barron's, 3rd ed., ISBN 9781438005232. Full conjugations in all tenses/moods, plus review of object pronouns, passive, progressive, irregular participles.
- **FREE tools — Conjuga-me** (conjuga-me.net) and **Reverso Conjugation** for instant lookups; both free. *Rationale: reference, not study — use to resolve doubts, not to memorize tables cover-to-cover.*

### Category E — Listening input (graded beginner → native)
BP learner podcasts (ladder low→high):
- **Todo Mundo Pod** (BP, bilingual explanations, ~10-min episodes; free, transcripts paid) — beginner/low-intermediate.
- **Carioca Connection** (hosts Alexia & Foster; natural BP conversation, transcripts via paid club) — intermediate; widely rated the best for the transition to real speech.
- **Speaking Brazilian** (Virginia Langhammer; grammar/pronunciation, free worksheets since 2024) — beginner→intermediate.
- **PortuguesePod101** — structured, leveled; freemium.
- Native audio: **Café da Manhã** (Folha, daily news, accessible), **Durma com Essa** (Nexo, one topic/evening), **Rádio Novelo** (narrative journalism, transcripts), **Café Brasil** (Luciano Pires, culture) — advanced.

Audio courses:
- **Pimsleur Brazilian Portuguese** (5 levels; audio-first, recall-based; excellent for speaking reflexes; paid) — best-in-class for your secondary speaking goal.
- **Michel Thomas** — note the Foundation Portuguese course teaches **European** Portuguese (Virginia Catmur + Portuguese native), so it is off-target for BP-primary; skip unless you want EP exposure.

EP listening (awareness sub-track): **Practice Portuguese** (Rui & Joel; the best EP resource, A1–B2, free Learning Notes + paid units) and **Fala Português/Português com Carla**.
*Rationale: ladder from bilingual-scaffolded pods → native learner pods → native news audio; BP throughout, EP only for recognition.*

### Category F — Reading (graded → parallel → native)
1. **Graded readers:** Olly Richards, *Short Stories in Brazilian Portuguese for Beginners* (Teach Yourself/John Murray, 2019, ISBN 9781529302806; CEFR A2–B1; per-chapter glossaries and comprehension questions). Note: **no Olly Richards *intermediate* BP volume exists** — do not wait for one; move to native material instead.
2. **Reader tools (tap-to-translate):** **Readlang** (freemium; free unlimited single-word lookups, phrase cap on free tier, Premium ~$5–6/mo) and **LingQ** (freemium, subscription for full use). Import BP news/EPUBs and read with instant gloss + SRS. Given your Spanish, you will often skip the gloss.
3. **Native news (free):** **G1** (Globo; clear, accessible BP, with video/audio), **Folha de S.Paulo**, **Nexo Jornal** (excellent explanatory journalism), **Estadão**. *Start with G1, graduate to Nexo/Folha.*
4. **Reading ladder into literature:**
   - *First native books:* **José Mauro de Vasconcelos, *Meu Pé de Laranja Lima*** (1968; child narrator, simple, emotional) and **Paulo Coelho, *O Alquimista*** (short sentences, linear) — accessible B1 on-ramps.
   - *Then the giants:* **Machado de Assis** — *Dom Casmurro*, *Memórias Póstumas de Brás Cubas* (irony, C1); **Jorge Amado** (*Capitães da Areia*, regional NE vocabulary); **Graciliano Ramos** (*Vidas Secas*); **Clarice Lispector** (stylistically hard — save for last).
   - *Free/public-domain:* **Domínio Público** (dominiopublico.gov.br, Brazilian government portal; hosts Machado de Assis incl. *Dom Casmurro* and much more) and **Project Gutenberg** (Machado's *Quincas Borba*, *Papéis Avulsos*, etc.).
   - *EP reading (awareness):* José Saramago, Eça de Queirós (via Gutenberg/Domínio Público).

### Category G — Apps, SRS, vocabulary
- **Anki** — free on desktop/Android/AnkiWeb; one-time ~$24.99 on iOS. Build a false-friends deck and a BP-frequency deck. *Rationale: your retention engine; the iOS cost is a one-time purchase, not a subscription.*
- **A Frequency Dictionary of Portuguese** (Davies & Preto-Bay, Routledge, 2007, ISBN 9780415419970). 5,000 most-common words with register/dialect tags. **Caveat: the corpus mixes EP and BP sources** — useful but not BP-pure; cross-check dialect tags. Former CD data now free at the Routledge page.
- **Duolingo** (BP course; free/freemium) — light supplement only, useful for daily-habit streaks, not a core resource.

### Category H — Pronunciation / phonetics (BP-specific)
- **PRIMARY — Mário A. Perini, *Talking Brazilian: A Brazilian Portuguese Pronunciation Workbook*** (Yale, 2004, ISBN 9780300100211). Practical shadowing/drills.
- **SUPPLEMENTARY — James P. Giangola, *The Pronunciation of Brazilian Portuguese*** (ISBN 9783895864469) — thorough sound-spelling rules, explicitly noting BP is less phonetically transparent than Spanish.
- **SUPPLEMENTARY — Teschner & Simões, *Pronouncing Brazilian Portuguese*** — compares BP sounds to Spanish/French/English equivalents (ideal for you).
- Free: **Forvo** (crowd-sourced pronunciations). Key BP targets: nasal vowels/diphthongs (*ão, ões, ãe*), open/closed vowels, the BP *R* (guttural/aspirated in many positions), *S/Z*, final *-l* → /w/ (*Brasil* ≈ "Braziw"), and palatalization of *ti/di* → [tʃi]/[dʒi] (*dia* ≈ "djia").

### The 1990 Orthographic Agreement (Acordo Ortográfico)
Signed in Lisbon on 16 December 1990 by the seven lusophone countries; in force in Brazil from 1 January 2009 and mandatory from 1 January 2016. As the Practice Portuguese Learning Note confirms, it "did not unite Brazilian and European Portuguese spellings… it maintains the distinct existence of both graphic systems, while trying to bring the two closer together… covered by a single regulatory text." Per the Academia Brasileira de Letras survey (reported by *Folha de S.Paulo*, 2008), it affected 0.45% of words in the Brazilian norm and ~1.6% in the European norm, raising common orthographic unity to ~98% (from ~96%). Changes were mainly dropping silent consonants in EP (*óptimo→ótimo*), abolishing the trema (*ü*) in BP (*qüinqüênio→quinquênio*), and revising hyphen/accent rules. **Practical implication:** buy post-2016 editions; anything pre-2009 uses old spelling. Whitlam 3rd ed. (2023), Cunha & Cintra 7th ed. (2016), Hutchinson 3rd ed. (2018), and Ponto de Encontro 2nd ed. all conform.

## Calibration: Settled / Contested / Overstated
- **SETTLED:** The future subjunctive and inflected personal infinitive are genuinely Portuguese, absent in modern Spanish, and must be actively learned — they are the two clearest "un-learn your Spanish reflex" points. Post-Acordo, BP/EP reading is ~98% orthographically unified. Spoken BP is heavily proclitic ("me dá") and uses *a gente* + 3rd-singular for "we."
- **CONTESTED / nuanced:** How much to invest in prescriptive clitic rules (enclisis/mesoclisis). For *reading* formal BP and any EP you need to recognize them; for *speaking* BP you will almost never use them. Weight toward recognition. Also contested: whether *tu* matters in BP — it is regional (South, Northeast) and often takes *você*-style verb agreement in speech; learn to recognize, default to *você*.
- **OVERSTATED:** The "BP and EP are almost different languages" claim — true for *listening* (EP vowel reduction), overstated for *reading*. Also overstated: the difficulty of Portuguese for a Spanish speaker — the US State Department Foreign Service Institute rates Portuguese Category I (24–30 weeks / ~600–750 class hours for English speakers to reach "Speaking-3/Reading-3," Professional Working Proficiency ≈ CEFR B2–C1); your Spanish cuts the reading portion of that substantially. Finally, the risk of "portunhol" is real but manageable — it is a front-loadable problem (false friends + a few grammar points), not a pervasive one.

## Spanish → Portuguese Transfer Map
**Transfers nearly for free:** the 89% lexical-similarity coefficient (Ethnologue's Swadesh-style comparison, the highest among major Romance pairs); sentence structure and word order; the ser/estar distinction as a *concept* (though boundaries differ — BP uses *ficar* for location/becoming, e.g. *fica na esquina*); the subjunctive as a *system*; gender/number agreement; most prepositional logic.

**Highest-value false friends to memorize first (BP meaning):** *exquisito→esquisito* (strange, not delicious), *embarazada→grávida* (embaraçada = embarrassed), *oficina* (workshop/garage, not office = *escritório*), *rato* (moment OR mouse), *cena* vs *jantar* (dinner), *pelado* (naked, not bald), *ligar* (to call/turn on), *acordar* (to wake up, not agree), *brincar* (to play, not jump), *borracha* (eraser/rubber, not drunk), *salsa* (parsley; sauce = *molho*), *presunto* (ham, not "presumed"), *polvo* (octopus, not dust = *pó/poeira*), *cola* (glue, not tail/queue = *fila*), *escova* (brush), *vaso* (pot/toilet), *logo* (soon, not place), *ninho* (nest).

**Grammar to deliberately un-learn/re-learn:**
1. **Future subjunctive** — use it after *se/quando/enquanto/assim que/logo que* for future events (*Quando eu tiver tempo…*), where Spanish uses present indicative/subjunctive.
2. **Personal (inflected) infinitive** — *para nós chegarmos*, *é importante vocês virem*; Spanish forces *para que + subjuntivo*. Writing *para nós chegar* is a dead giveaway you're translating from Spanish.
3. **Present continuous** — BP *estar + gerúndio* (*estou fazendo*), not the Spanish-like EP *estar a + infinitivo* (*estou a fazer*).
4. **Clitic placement** — BP speech is proclitic and can start a clause with a clitic (*Me passa o sal*), the opposite of Castilian/EP enclitic instincts.
5. **"We" in speech** — *a gente* + 3rd-singular verb, not *nós*.
6. **Contractions** — Portuguese contracts prepositions with articles/demonstratives far more than Spanish: *de+o=do*, *em+a=na*, *em+um=num*, *por+o=pelo*, *de+este=deste*, *em+aquele=naquele*. Spanish only has *al/del*.
7. **Personal "a"** — Portuguese does **not** use the Spanish personal *a* before direct-object people.

## Phased Curriculum

**Phase 0 — Interference inoculation & sound system (2–3 weeks).** Read *Pois Não* front matter + phonology; drill BP sounds with Perini *Talking Brazilian* / Teschner-Simões; build the false-friends Anki deck. *Benchmark to advance: can read a G1 headline aloud with correct nasal/palatalization and recognize the top 50 false friends.*

**Phase 1 — Spanish-leveraged foundation (2–4 months).** Work through *Pois Não* systematically alongside *Colloquial Portuguese of Brazil* (free audio). Add Pimsleur BP Level 1 for speaking reflexes and Todo Mundo Pod for listening. Start Olly Richards graded reader. *Benchmark: finish Colloquial (A2), read graded stories comfortably, hold a basic conversation.*

**Phase 2 — Grammar consolidation & full verb system (3–6 months).** Whitlam grammar + Workbook cover-to-cover; nail future subjunctive, personal infinitive, all pluperfects, double participles (drill with *501 Verbs*/Conjuga-me). Listening ladders to Carioca Connection; reading moves to G1 → Nexo via Readlang. *Benchmark: read a full Nexo article without constant lookups; use future subjunctive and personal infinitive correctly in writing.*

**Phase 3 — Native reading & listening immersion (6–18 months).** First native novels (*Meu Pé de Laranja Lima*, *O Alquimista*) → Machado de Assis short stories (Domínio Público). Native news audio (Café da Manhã, Durma com Essa). Perini as reference for hard constructions. *Benchmark: finish a Machado novel; follow a native podcast at normal speed.*

**Phase 4 — Advanced & EP awareness (ongoing).** Literary range (Lispector, Amado, Graciliano Ramos); Cunha & Cintra as prestige reference. Activate the EP sub-track: Practice Portuguese app + Hutchinson EP grammar; read some Saramago/Eça. *Benchmark: read across registers and both varieties; converse comfortably.*

**Dependency map:** Phase 0 (sounds + false friends) → Phase 1 (course + contrastive spine) → Phase 2 (systematic grammar) → Phase 3 (native input) → Phase 4 (advanced + EP). Phases 0–1 are strictly sequential; listening and reading ladders run in parallel from Phase 1; the EP sub-track can begin any time after Phase 2 but is deliberately deferred so it does not muddy BP acquisition.

## The Time-Compressed MVP (Reading Access First)
For fastest reading access, compress to ~8–12 weeks:
1. **Week 1:** *Pois Não* phonology + false-friends deck (Aporte/Wikipedia lists → Anki). Skip speaking.
2. **Weeks 2–4:** Read only the grammar-summary and verb chapters of Whitlam; memorize the future-subjunctive, personal-infinitive, and contraction paradigms. Leverage Spanish to skip vocabulary drills.
3. **Weeks 3–10 (parallel):** Read G1 daily via Readlang; add Olly Richards graded reader; move to Nexo by week 8.
4. **Week 10+:** First native novel (*Meu Pé de Laranja Lima*).

Defer Pimsleur, pronunciation polish, conversation, and the entire EP sub-track to a later expansion. This exploits the fact that your Spanish makes *reading* the fastest skill to unlock; listening and speaking are added once reading is self-sustaining.

## Recommendations
1. **Buy now (core):** *Pois Não* (9780292717817); Whitlam *Modern Brazilian Portuguese Grammar* 3rd ed. (9781032244334) + Workbook; Perini *Modern Portuguese* (9780300091557); *501 Portuguese Verbs* (9781438005232). Start free: Colloquial audio, G1, Domínio Público, Conjuga-me, Anki.
2. **Sequence:** Do Phase 0 fully before anything else — interference control is the whole game for a Spanish speaker. Do not skip the false-friends deck.
3. **For reading-first:** run the MVP; add Pimsleur + pronunciation only once you read G1 comfortably.
4. **For EP awareness:** subscribe to Practice Portuguese and add Hutchinson's EP grammar *after* Phase 2 — not before.
5. **Thresholds that change the plan:** if listening lags reading badly (common for Spanish speakers), add daily Café da Manhã + shadowing and slow to 0.75× on native audio; if "portunhol" persists in output, pause production and do a month of input-only + targeted false-friend review; if you find you only ever want to *read*, drop Pimsleur and conversation entirely and invest that time in volume reading.

## Caveats
- **Editions/currency:** Always buy post-2016 printings for Acordo-compliant spelling. The Whitlam grammar is now in its 3rd edition (2023, with Silveira as co-author); earlier editions (2011/2017) are fine but less current. *Pois Não* remains the 2008 revised edition — still the standard, but its "current cultural references" are now ~18 years old.
- **Out-of-print / hard to obtain:** Perini's reference grammar and *Ponto de Encontro* can be expensive or intermittently out of stock; check AbeBooks/used. *Pois Não* is available new. Cunha & Cintra is easy to obtain in Brazil/PDF but pricier abroad.
- **Author correction:** the current Routledge *Colloquial Portuguese of Brazil* beginners course is by **Viviane Gontijo**, not the older Osborne/McIntyre team (who wrote the level-2 book) — verify the exact edition when purchasing.
- **Freemium creep:** Readlang and LingQ are freemium, not free; verify current pricing before subscribing. Practice Portuguese and Pimsleur are paid.
- **Source-quality note:** podcast rankings and app reviews here draw partly on aggregator/blog sources; treat specific "best" claims as directional. The frequency dictionary's EP+BP mixed corpus means its dialect tags matter for a BP-pure vocabulary.
