# Verified Resource Dossier for a Phased Spanish→Italian Self-Study Curriculum (A0→B2 Reading / B1 Speaking)

## TL;DR
- The strongest canonical spine for a disciplined adult autodidact is **Nuovo Espresso / Nuovo Espresso Plus (Alma Edizioni, A1–C2)** as the core method, paired with **Susanna Nocchi's Grammatica pratica della lingua italiana (Alma, A1–B2, edizione aggiornata)** for graded exercises and **Maiden & Robustelli's A Reference Grammar of Modern Italian (2nd ed.)** as the English-language deep reference — all current and verifiable in 2026.
- The learner's ~B1 Spanish is a large accelerator (Italian is FSI Category I, at the fast end of the tier — ~24 weeks / ~552–600 class hours to Speaking-3/Reading-3, vs. 30 weeks for Spanish/French), but the central risk is interference: auxiliary essere/avere, passato remoto in literature, articulated prepositions, ne/ci and combined clitics, and a well-documented set of falsi amici (burro, salire, aceto, largo, caldo, gamba, subire) must be attacked contrastively.
- For certification in Frankfurt, **CILS (Università per Stranieri di Siena)** is the recommended milestone chain (B1 then B2): it is sat locally through the Frankfurt Consulate under IIC Cologne coordination, and its per-skill "capitalizzazione" (bankable abilities, 11/20 pass, 18-month window) best matches the asymmetric read-higher-than-speak target.

## Key Findings
- Free pillars genuinely worth a serious learner's time: **Treccani vocabolario** (authoritative monolingual, free), **Il Post** and **Easy Italian News** (clear contemporary Italian, free), **Podcast Italiano (Davide Gemello)** with free transcripts, **RaiPlay / RaiPlay Sound** (free authentic media), and **Liber Liber / Progetto Manuzio** (free public-domain literature + audiobooks).
- The best English-language reference grammar is Maiden & Robustelli; the best learner-facing bilingual "practical guide" is Proudfoot & Cardo (Modern Italian Grammar, 3rd ed., with a separate Workbook).
- Certification systems differ meaningfully in whether skills are independently bankable — this is decision-relevant for the asymmetric target, and it favors CILS and PLIDA over CELI.
- **Important correction to a premise:** on Ethnologue's lexical-similarity coefficient, Italian–Spanish is 0.82 but Italian–**French** is 0.89 — i.e., by that specific metric Italian is lexically *closer to French than to Spanish*. The Spanish accelerator for this learner rests more on high mutual intelligibility and the learner's existing ~B1 Spanish than on Spanish being the single lexically closest language. This is flagged in Caveats.

## Details

### 1. Core coursebook / method series (A1→B2)
- **Nuovo Espresso (Alma Edizioni)** — six levels A1–C2, aligned to CEFR (Volume 4 = B2). Communicative/inductive method. Audio, videocorso and videogrammatica are provided in the online web area (some free; some in the ALMA.tv Premium/ebook area). **Nuovo Espresso Plus** is the "completely updated" edition (A1–C2) with interactive ebook (14-month license), video course, and videogrammatica. **Verdict:** best all-round spine for a disciplined self-learner because of the integrated self-correcting ebook and free audio. Publisher: Alma Edizioni, Florence. Confirmed current on almaedizioni.it in 2026.
- **Nuovissimo Progetto italiano (Edilingua)** — per the publisher, "the completely updated version of the world's best-selling Italian language course for foreigners." Vol 1 = A1–A2 (ISBN 9788899358501, author T. Marin), Vol 2 = B1–B2, Vol 3 = B2+/C1, Vol 4 = C2. Inductive + communicative. The **i-d-e-e** access code (valid 18 months) gives free access to the interactive student book, workbook, audio (including a deliberately "slowed" audio track for learners whose L1 is distant from Italian), video course, games and quizzes; exercises are modeled on CELI/CILS/PLIDA formats. A German-speakers' edition ("per studenti germanofoni") and an English-mediated "The New Italian Project" exist. **Verdict:** strong alternative spine; slightly more grammar-systematic than Espresso.
- **Nuovo Contatto (Loescher), Arrivederci! (Edilingua), Al dente (Casa delle Lingue), Dieci (Alma)** — all reputable current communicative series; Dieci is A1–B2, strongly practical/task-based with comic episodes and video. These are more classroom-oriented or supplementary for a solo learner; treat as optional secondary spines.

### 2. Standalone grammar references
**(a) Learner-oriented graded grammars with exercises (A1→B2):**
- **Susanna Nocchi, Grammatica pratica della lingua italiana (Alma Edizioni), edizione aggiornata, A1–B2.** ISBN-13 9788861827363 (updated edition, libro + audio online); older ISBN-13 9788861822474 (2012). Clear grammar cards + graded exercises + tests + the comic "Chi ha ucciso l'italiano?". ~288 pp. The single best exercise-driven grammar for this learner. Current in 2026.
- **Grammatica avanzata della lingua italiana (Alma)** — B1/C2 companion for the push to B2 reading. ISBN-13 9788889237281.
- Edilingua alternatives: Via della grammatica, Grammatica italiana più, Una grammatica italiana per tutti.

**(b) Comprehensive reference grammars in English:**
- **Martin Maiden & Cecilia Robustelli, A Reference Grammar of Modern Italian**, 2nd edition (Routledge/Hodder; McGraw-Hill in US). ISBN-13 9780340913390 (paperback, 2007); the later Routledge reissue carries ISBN-13 9781138170872 (hardcover, ~512 pp). This is the Italian analogue to Butt & Benjamin — authoritative, comprehensive, near-native, jargon-free, with formal/archaic vs. everyday usage distinguished and regionalisms flagged. Maiden is Professor of Romance Languages at Oxford; Robustelli is Professor of Italian Linguistics at Modena and Reggio Emilia. **Verdict:** the reference of record for this analytically-minded learner.
- **Anna Proudfoot & Francesco Cardo, Modern Italian Grammar: A Practical Guide**, 3rd edition, Routledge, ISBN-13 9780415671866 (pbk) / 9780415671873. Combines traditional + function-based grammar; no prior grammatical terminology assumed. Companion: **Modern Italian Grammar Workbook**, 2nd edition, ISBN-13 9780415331654. **Verdict:** the best mid-weight bilingual practical grammar; use as a bridge to Maiden & Robustelli.

### 4. Reading progression

These cover Dante, Manzoni (I Promessi Sposi), Verga (I Malavoglia), Pirandello (Il fu Mattia Pascal), etc.

### 5. Italian press for a B1→B2 reader

- **Mainstream (intermediate→advanced, aimed at educated natives):** **Corriere della Sera**, **la Repubblica**, **La Stampa** (all metered paywalls; substantial free content). Il Sole 24 Ore for business (harder). Metro is an even simpler free daily for early B1.
- **Free news:** **ANSA** (clear agency Italian, free) and **RAI News / RaiNews24** (video + text, free). **Verdict:** sequence Il Post + ANSA at B1, add Corriere/Repubblica features at B1→B2.

### 6. Listening / audio / media
- **Structured learner podcasts:** **Coffee Break Italian** (absolute beginner→lower-intermediate; free episodes, paid transcripts) — hosted by Mark Pentleton with a native speaker; **News in Slow Italian** (intermediate; mostly paid, some free on Spotify; transcripts gated); **Podcast Italiano (Davide Gemello)** (B1–B2; free transcripts for almost all episodes — best value, more linguistically focused, covers dialects/regional Italian); **Learn Italian with Lucrezia**, **Italiano automatico** (near-native pace from the start), **Salvatore says**, **Ci vuole orecchio**, **Italiano Bello** (transcripts gated to paid / German+English notes).
- **Authentic media:** **RaiPlay** (video, free, accessible in the EU/Germany), **RaiPlay Sound** (radio/podcast, free), **RAI Radio**. "Una lingua per amico" is a legacy RAI item — treat as historical, not a current pillar.
- **Verdict:** the free three-pass loop (listen → read transcript → re-listen) with Podcast Italiano is the highest-yield free listening pillar; the single biggest paid feature worth buying is transcripts if a plateau appears.

### 7. Pronunciation & phonetics
Italian orthography is largely transparent, so the load is lighter than French. The genuine difficulties: **geminate/double consonants** (phonemic: fato/fatto, ano/anno, pene/penne), **open vs. closed e/o** ([e]/[ɛ], [o]/[ɔ]) — seven stressed vowel phonemes from five letters; **stress placement** (default penultimate; final stress marked with accent, città; antepenultimate telefono, parlano must be learned); the palatal sounds **gli [ʎ]** and **gn [ɲ]** (single sounds, not clusters); **c/g softening** before e/i (with h/i as diacritics: che/ce, ghi/gi); **s and z voicing** ([s]/[z], [ts]/[dz]); and intonation. For a **Spanish L1**, the highest-value targets are gemination (Spanish lacks phonemic double consonants) and open/closed e-o (Spanish has only five vowels); gli/gn map roughly to Spanish ll/ñ, so are easier for him than for an English speaker. Any careful IPA-based guide (Maiden & Robustelli's pronunciation chapter is authoritative) plus shadowing native audio covers this. Italian is syllable-timed with no vowel reduction to schwa — a Spanish speaker already has this rhythm, a real transfer advantage.

### 8. Spanish→Italian contrastive resources and interference traps — CENTRAL
- **Resources:** Academic contrastive linguistics exists (San Vicente / J.C. Barbero, "Contrastiva. Grammatica della lingua spagnola per italofoni," CLUEB — B2–C1, but oriented Spanish-for-Italians, i.e. the reverse direction; still valuable for its systematic contrast maps, phonology, gender/number divergence, verb uses, indicativo/congiuntivo). "Italiano per ispanofoni" materials (e.g., italianosemplicemente.com) exist but are non-canonical. There is a substantial didactic-research literature on teaching Italian to Spanish speakers ("linguistica contrastiva e didattica di lingue affini"), which stresses that similarity speeds initial progress but raises the risk of fossilized near-miss forms. **Verdict:** there is no single canonical Espresso-caliber "Italian-for-Spanish-speakers" textbook; the learner should use a mainstream method plus a deliberate falsi-amici/interference layer.
- **Key structural traps (verify against Maiden & Robustelli):**
  - **Auxiliary essere vs. avere** in the passato prossimo — Spanish has only haber, so auxiliary selection + past-participle agreement with essere (è andata, si sono lavati) is a genuine trap; parallels French être/avoir.
  - **passato prossimo vs. passato remoto** — the passato remoto appears heavily in literature and (regionally) in the south; maps imperfectly onto Spanish pretérito perfecto/indefinido. Essential for B2 reading of novels.
  - **Definite/indefinite articles and articulated prepositions** (del, nel, sul, alla, dai, negli…) — Spanish fuses only al and del; Italian fuses di/a/da/in/su + article across the board.
  - **Plural formation** (-i/-e vs Spanish -s) and **gender mismatches** between cognates (e.g., il latte, il sale, il sangue, il fiore are masculine; la coppia; many -ione nouns are feminine).
  - **Clitics:** combined clitics (glielo, gliene, ce ne), and the particles **ne** and **ci**, which have no direct Spanish equivalent — a persistent production gap for Spanish speakers.
  - **Subjunctive:** broadly parallel to Spanish (an area of positive transfer), but distribution and sequence-of-tenses differ.
- **Falsi amici (Spanish ↔ Italian), enumerated for a spaced-repetition set:** burro (It. butter / Sp. donkey), salire (It. to go up / Sp. salir = to go out), subire (It. to undergo, suffer / Sp. subir = to go up), aceto (It. vinegar / Sp. aceite = oil), largo (It. wide / Sp. largo = long), caldo (It. hot / Sp. caldo = broth), gamba (It. leg / Sp. gamba = prawn), seta (It. silk / Sp. seta = mushroom), guardare (It. to look at / Sp. guardar = to keep), carta (It. paper / Sp. carta = letter), nata (It. born / Sp. nata = cream), imbarazzata (It. embarrassed / Sp. embarazada = pregnant), equipaggio (It. crew / Sp. equipaje = luggage), successo (It. success / Sp. suceso = event), officina (It. workshop / Sp. oficina = office), tienda vs. tenda (Sp. shop / It. curtain, tent), esposto/esposa. A robust starter set for the interference layer.

### 9. Dictionaries & reference tools
- **Monolingual (free online):** **Treccani** (vocabolario + Enciclopedia dell'Italiano + synonyms — authoritative, free), **il Nuovo De Mauro** (dizionario.internazionale.it, 160,000+ lemmas, free), **Sabatini-Coletti** (dizionari.corriere.it, free), **Garzanti** (free, registration). **Paywalled/print:** **lo Zingarelli** (Zanichelli, updated annually, the standard desk dictionary), **Devoto-Oli**.
- **Bilingual:** **Collins**, **Oxford-Paravia**, **WordReference** (free), **Reverso Context** (free, example-based), **Linguee** (free). **Verdict:** Treccani + De Mauro (monolingual, free) plus WordReference/Reverso (bilingual, free) fully cover this learner; migrate to monolingual-only as early as B1 to force deeper engagement.

### 10. Speaking practice & output (method-level)
- **1-on-1 tutoring marketplaces** (italki-type) for structured conversation from ~A2; **language exchange / tandem** once at A2–B1; **shadowing** native audio from day one; **self-recorded output loops** (record → compare to model → re-record). For a Spanish speaker, prioritize early, corrected speaking to *suppress* Spanish phonology/lexical interference before it fossilizes. Aim for ~2 structured conversation sessions/week from A2 onward to reach B1 speaking.

### 11. Free / open educational resources — verdicts
- **One World Italiano (oneworlditaliano.com):** free course of 37 units elementary→advanced, plus a video course with "Veronica," dialogues with native audio, grammar sheets, and end-of-lesson quizzes (61+ beginner/intermediate lessons). Somewhat unorganized and mostly in Italian, but genuinely substantial. **Verdict: worth it** as a free structured supplement.
- **RaiPlay / RaiPlay Sound:** free authentic media — **worth it** for listening.
- **Impariamo l'italiano, LibreTexts Italian, Anki shared decks, adaptive grammar sites:** variable; Anki decks (frequency lists, falsi amici) are high-value for this learner; most adaptive sites are shallow. **Verdict:** use Anki seriously; treat generic grammar sites as disposable.
- **"Una lingua per amico" (RAI):** legacy; not a current pillar.

### 12. Italy / Germany context & certification
- **Institutional presence:** The **Istituto Italiano di Cultura Frankfurt closed in 2014**; competences passed to **IIC Cologne (Köln)** — which covers the Land of Hesse/Assia containing Frankfurt — plus IIC Stuttgart and IIC Munich. Active **Società Dante Alighieri committees in Darmstadt (~30 km) and Wiesbaden (~40 km)** offer Italian courses. Critically, the **Frankfurt Consulate itself organizes two annual CILS exam sessions** (June and December), coordinated by IIC Cologne — so the learner can sit CILS locally without travelling.
- **The four systems (all CLIQ members, all CEFR-aligned, all state-recognized, no expiry):**
  - **CILS (Università per Stranieri di Siena):** five abilities — ascolto (listening), comprensione della lettura (reading), analisi delle strutture di comunicazione (grammar), produzione scritta (writing), produzione orale (speaking). At B1/B2 the total is 100 points, each ability max 20; **pass = 11/20 (60%) per ability**; you must pass all. **Abilities are independently bankable ("capitalizzazione"): failed abilities can be re-sat within 18 months paying only the per-ability fee** (standard B1/B2 — not the B1 Cittadinanza module, where capitalization is disallowed). Official 2026 dates: **B1 standard on 16 June and 3 December; B2 on 14 April, 16 June, and 3 December.** Overseas IIC/consular centers typically run only the June and December sessions.
  - **CELI (Università per Stranieri di Perugia):** CELI 2 = B1, CELI 3 = B2. Two macro-parts: Prova Scritta + Prova Orale. **CELI 2 pass = 72/120 written AND 22/40 oral; CELI 3 pass = 84/140 written AND 33/60 oral.** Both must be passed; capitalization only at the written-vs-oral level (re-sit the failed macro-part within one year), NOT sub-skill by sub-skill. Roughly three sessions/year (confirm exact 2026 count on cvcl.unistrapg.it — one official source described the capitalization window as "un anno / due sessioni," so the 3/year figure carries mild uncertainty).
  - **PLIDA (Società Dante Alighieri):** four skills (ascoltare, leggere, scrivere, parlare); no separate grammar section. Score in trentesimi; **pass = 18/30 in each of the four skills**; failed skills (up to three) are independently bankable for 18 months. Nearest confirmed PLIDA exam center to Frankfurt is **Dante Alighieri Düsseldorf**; Darmstadt/Wiesbaden Dante committees offer courses — verify their PLIDA exam status via the official center finder at plida.dante.global.
  - **cert.it (Roma Tre):** ascolto, lettura, produzione scritta, produzione orale (+ "Usi e forme dell'italiano" grammar at C1/C2); **pass = 60% of total**; certificate issued only when all abilities are passed; Rome-based and delivered on computer — least convenient from Frankfurt.
- **Recommendation:** **CILS B1 then CILS B2** as the milestone chain. It is sittable locally in Frankfurt, and its per-ability capitalizzazione uniquely rewards the asymmetric target (the learner can bank strong reading/listening while re-sitting weaker production if needed). Levels are independent exams — B2 need not immediately follow B1. PLIDA is the strong runner-up if he prefers its four-skill structure and can reach the Düsseldorf center; CELI is less attractive here because it only banks written-vs-oral, not individual skills.

### Pedagogy debate (calibration tags)
- **Communicative / action-oriented approach** (dominant in Alma/Edilingua materials): **settled** mainstream for adults.
- **Role of explicit grammar for adult learners:** **settled** that explicit grammar helps adults (especially analytical ones) — hence the reference-grammar spine — though the optimal *proportion* is contested.
- **Comprehensible-input / immersion (strong Krashen "acquisition over learning"):** **contested / partly hype** in its strong form; the evidence-based consensus is a blend (rich input + output practice + some explicit grammar and corrective feedback). For this learner, input-heavy reading + explicit contrastive grammar + early corrected output is the defensible synthesis.

### Sequencing & timelines
- Italian is **FSI Category I**, at the fast end of the tier: ~24 weeks / ~552–600 class hours to Speaking-3/Reading-3 for English speakers, tied with Portuguese, Dutch, Danish, Norwegian, Romanian and Swedish — while French and Spanish sit slightly longer at ~30 weeks (~690 hours). FSI-style figures assume intensive small-group instruction; **self-study typically needs 1.5–2× those hours.**
- **Romance/Spanish head-start discount:** the learner's existing ~B1 Spanish plus high Italian–Spanish mutual intelligibility (Ethnologue lexical coefficient 0.82) delivers a large discount on vocabulary and especially *reading* acquisition — while raising interference risk rather than lowering it. (Note the metric caveat in Caveats: Ethnologue actually rates Italian–French higher at 0.89.)
- **Realistic self-study hour bands for this learner:**
  - **A0→B1 speaking:** roughly **250–400 hours** of active study (vs. ~350–500 for a monolingual English speaker), given the Spanish accelerator and disciplined habits — but speaking gains specifically require dedicated, corrected output, so this band assumes real conversation practice.
  - **A0→B2 reading:** roughly **300–450 hours**, and reading can outrun the hour count substantially for a Spanish speaker because passive comprehension transfers fastest — **B2 reading is the most attainable of the four targets.**
- **Four-skill sequencing:** front-load reading + listening (comprehensible input) leveraging Spanish; run explicit contrastive grammar in parallel from the start; begin output (writing, then speaking) by A2 to pre-empt fossilized interference; push reading hardest toward authentic literature/press in the B1→B2 phase.

## Recommendations
1. **Phase 1 (A0→A2):** Nuovo Espresso Plus 1–2 as spine + Nocchi Grammatica pratica (A1–A2) + Alma Italiano facile / Edilingua Primiracconti graded readers (A1–A2) + Coffee Break Italian + One World Italiano free units. Start a falsi-amici Anki deck and shadowing from week one. **Advance when:** comfortable A2 self-test in Espresso and can read A2 graded readers unaided.
2. **Phase 2 (A2→B1):** Nuovo Espresso Plus 3 + Nocchi (B1) + Proudfoot & Cardo for targeted grammar + Podcast Italiano (with transcripts) + Il Post / ANSA + italki 2×/week. Attack the interference traps explicitly (essere/avere + participle agreement, articulated prepositions, ne/ci, combined clitics). **Milestone: CILS B1** (Frankfurt Consulate June or December session).
3. **Phase 3 (B1→B2 reading):** Nuovo Espresso Plus 4 (B2) + Maiden & Robustelli as reference + Grammatica avanzata (Alma) + authentic literature (Calvino → Ginzburg → Buzzati → Ferrante) + Corriere/Repubblica features + RaiPlay. Introduce the passato remoto explicitly for literary reading. **Milestone: CILS B2**, playing to reading/listening/writing strengths with production held to solid B1 (capitalizzazione lets him bank the strong abilities). Camilleri only once secure.
4. **Thresholds that change the plan:** if speaking lags (can't sustain B1 conversation by end of Phase 2), add a second weekly tutoring session and a self-recording loop before attempting CILS B2. If reading races ahead (comfortable with unabridged Calvino early), skip additional graded readers and move straight to authentic press/literature. If the Düsseldorf PLIDA center proves more convenient than expected, PLIDA B1/B2 is a fully equivalent substitute milestone.

## Caveats
- **Verified vs. not:** Editions/ISBNs for Nuovo Espresso, Nocchi's Grammatica pratica, Maiden & Robustelli, Proudfoot & Cardo (+ Workbook), 501 Italian Verbs (6th ed., June 2024), and Nuovissimo Progetto italiano were confirmed against publisher/retailer listings current in 2026. CILS/CELI/PLIDA/cert.it structures, pass thresholds, and 2026 CILS dates were confirmed against official sources (cils.unistrasi.it, unistrapg.it PDFs, esteri.it IIC/consulate pages, plida.dante.global, uniroma3.it). The exact **CELI session count (3/year) carries mild uncertainty** and should be re-checked on cvcl.unistrapg.it.
- **Lexical-similarity premise flagged:** the widely-cited ~80–82% Italian–Spanish figure is real (Ethnologue coefficient 0.82), but the same source gives Italian–**French** 0.89 — so the claim that Spanish is the single lexically closest major Romance language to Italian is *not* supported by Ethnologue's metric. The genuine Spanish advantage for this learner comes from (a) his existing ~B1 Spanish and (b) high Spanish–Italian mutual intelligibility, not from Spanish being lexically closest. Treat the "closer than French" framing as **contested**, and keep the interference focus regardless.
- **Free vs. paywalled:** Treccani, De Mauro, Sabatini-Coletti, WordReference, Reverso, Il Post, ANSA, RaiPlay, Liber Liber, One World Italiano, and Podcast Italiano transcripts are free. Zingarelli/Devoto-Oli, News in Slow Italian transcripts, Coffee Break premium, and all print coursebooks/grammars are paid. Mainstream newspapers are metered.
- **Contrastive gap:** there is no canonical Espresso-caliber "Italian for Spanish speakers" textbook; the contrastive layer must be assembled from the enumerated falsi-amici set, the grammar traps above, and the reference grammar. This is the area with the thinnest single-source coverage.
- **Camilleri caveat:** Sicilian-inflected language — not representative of italiano standard; defer until B2 reading is secure.
- **A few method series (Nuovo Contatto, Al dente, Arrivederci!) were identified but not edition-verified** in this pass; confirm current editions on the Loescher / Casa delle Lingue / Edilingua sites before purchase.
