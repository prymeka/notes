# European Spanish B1 → C1: step-by-step reading curriculum

A week-by-week execution plan derived from the B1-to-C1 reading plan. The goal is unchanged — reach C1 reading comprehension in Peninsular Spanish — but here every week tells you exactly which book to open, which grammar topic to study, what to drill, and what to read. Built for **2–3 hours/week across two sessions**.

---

## How to use this curriculum

**The two-session week.** Each week has two study blocks. Keep them distinct:

- **Session A — Grammar (60–75 min):** 10 min verb warm-up → study the week's topic in the primary grammar book → do its exercises → check answers against the key.
- **Session B — Apply (60–75 min):** 10 min verb warm-up → extensive reading (the week's assignment) → log new vocabulary → optional listening.

**The grammar-to-reading ratio shifts as you progress:** ~50/50 in Phase 1, 40/60 in Phase 2, 30/70 in Phase 3, 20/80 in Phase 4. Grammar study tapers as reading takes over — that's by design.

**On unit numbers.** I map each week to a grammar **topic**, not a fixed unit number. Printings and editions renumber units, so open your book's *índice* (table of contents) and find the unit with that topic title. This keeps the plan valid whichever printing you own.

## The MVP spine (if time gets compressed)

If a deadline or trip squeezes your schedule, these are the **non-skippable** weeks — the minimum path that still produces functional reading ability. Everything else is reinforcement.

> **Phase 1 spine:** Weeks 1, 3, 5, **6–7 (indefinido/imperfecto contrast — the single most important block for reading)**, 9, 12.
> **Phase 2 spine:** Weeks 17–18 (subjunctive forms), 19–22 (core triggers), 29–31 (imperfect subjunctive + si-clauses), 36 (indirect speech).
> **Phase 3 spine:** Weeks 41–42 (passive/pasiva refleja), 49 (Type-3 conditionals), 57–58 (discourse connectors).
> **Phase 4:** reading itself is the spine.

A learner who does only the spine reaches comfortable B2 reading; the full plan reaches C1.

# Phase 1 — B1 consolidation (Weeks 1–16 · Months 1–4)

**Goal:** rebuild a rock-solid indicative-mood foundation and meet the imperative. By the end you can read B1 graded readers comfortably and follow simple news. **Primary book:** GdU. **Drill support:** Castro-I + PMP.

**Reading track (Phase 1):** Start with B1 graded readers — SGEL *Lecturas: Literatura hispánica de fácil lectura* (e.g., *Leyendas* by Bécquer, *Lazarillo de Tormes*), Edinumen *Lecturas graduadas* B1, or Black Cat *Leer y aprender* B1. Aim for **one reader every 3–4 weeks** (~1 chapter per Session B). **News:** ★ Practica Español, one B1-tagged article per week, read aloud.

**Verb warm-up (Phase 1):** Each session, conjugate 5 high-frequency verbs in the tense you're currently studying, using 501 to check. Priority verbs: *ser, estar, ir, hacer, tener, poder, decir, poner, ver, dar, saber, querer, venir, salir, poner*.

| Wk | Grammar focus | Study (Session A) | Drill | Done when… |
|----|---------------|-------------------|-------|------------|
| 1 | **Presente de indicativo** — regular + stem-changing + irregular yo-forms | GdU: units on *presente de indicativo* (regular, irregular, stem-changing). ★ Castro-I: *presente* units | PMP ch. on present tense; 501 for the 15 priority verbs | You can conjugate any regular + top-20 irregular verb in the present without hesitation |
| 2 | **Pretérito perfecto compuesto** (he hablado) + Peninsular usage (hoy, esta semana, ya) | GdU: *pretérito perfecto* unit + past participle formation | PMP present-perfect section | You use *he/has/ha…* correctly with Peninsular time markers |
| 3 | **Pretérito indefinido** — regular | GdU: *pretérito indefinido* regular unit; Castro-I indefinido unit | PMP preterite (regular); 501 | Regular -ar/-er/-ir preterite endings are automatic |
| 4 | **Pretérito indefinido** — irregular (estar, tener, hacer, poder, poner, venir, decir, traer, ir/ser, dar) | GdU: *indefinido* irregular unit(s) | Drill the "strong" preterites daily; 501 | You produce *estuve, tuve, hice, pude, puse, vine, dije, fui* on demand |
| 5 | **Pretérito imperfecto** (hablaba) — regular + ser/ir/ver | GdU: *pretérito imperfecto* unit | PMP imperfect section | Imperfect forms (incl. *era, iba, veía*) are automatic |
| 6 | **Indefinido vs. imperfecto — contrast (1):** narration vs. description, completed vs. ongoing | GdU: *contraste indefinido/imperfecto* unit; ★ Spanish Experiment notes; B&B chapter on preterite vs. imperfect | PMP contrast exercises | You can justify *why* each verb in a short narrative is one tense or the other |

| 8 | **Consolidation + Self-Assessment 1** | Redo flagged GdU autoevaluaciones; mixed past-tense exercises | Mixed tense drill | Buffer week — catch up if behind |
| 9 | **Pretérito pluscuamperfecto** (había hablado) | GdU: *pluscuamperfecto* unit | PMP pluperfect | You handle "había + participio" for the past-before-the-past |
| 10 | **Futuro simple** (hablaré) + future of probability (*serán las tres*) | GdU: *futuro simple* unit | PMP future; 501 irregular futures (*tendré, haré, diré, podré*) | Future endings + irregular stems automatic; you recognise conjecture use |
| 11 | **Progressive / continuous forms** (estar + gerundio, across tenses) + gerund formation | GdU: *perífrasis con gerundio* / *estar + gerundio*; B&B continuous-forms chapter | Drill gerunds incl. irregulars (*leyendo, durmiendo, diciendo*) | You form and place gerunds correctly in present/past continuous |
| 12 | **Condicional simple** (hablaría) + conditional of conjecture/politeness | GdU: *condicional simple* unit | PMP conditional; 501 | Conditional forms automatic; you recognise *would*/probability uses |
| 13 | **Imperativo afirmativo** (tú, vosotros, usted, ustedes) + pronoun attachment (dímelo) | GdU: *imperativo afirmativo* unit | Drill affirmative commands + clitic attachment | You give affirmative commands incl. *vosotros* (¡hablad!) |
| 14 | **Imperativo negativo** (→ first subjunctive forms) + pronoun placement (no me lo digas) | GdU: *imperativo negativo* unit | Contrast affirmative vs. negative command pronoun position | You see how negative commands borrow subjunctive forms — bridge to Phase 2 |
| 15 | **Indicative-system integration** — all tenses mixed | GdU: cumulative review; mixed exercises across units | Mixed-tense narration drill | You move between all indicative tenses fluidly in reading |
| 16 | **Self-Assessment 2** + buffer | Re-test weak spots; finish current graded reader | — | **Phase 1 checkpoint** (see below) |

**▣ Phase 1 self-assessment (end of Wk 16).** Read an unfamiliar B1 graded-reader chapter. Can you (a) identify every verb's tense and explain the indefinido/imperfecto choices, (b) read it at roughly conversational pace, (c) summarise it aloud in past tenses? If the indefinido/imperfecto contrast still feels shaky, repeat Weeks 6–7 before advancing — it gates everything.

# Phase 2 — Subjunctive entry & B2 threshold (Weeks 17–40 · Months 5–10)

**Goal:** acquire the full present and past subjunctive and the conditional sentence system — the grammar that unlocks complex prose. This is the steepest climb in the whole plan; the present subjunctive alone gets two months. **Primary book:** GdU. **Drill support:** Castro-A. **Reference:** B&B subjunctive chapter (read it slowly — it's the best explanation in English).

**Reading track (Phase 2):** Move to **B2 graded readers** (SGEL adapted *Don Quijote*, *El Buscón*; Edinumen B2). Add the **Penguin Parallel Text *Short Stories in Spanish*** (King, ed.) as a bridge to authentic prose — read the Spanish first, use the facing English only to check. **News:** graduate to *20 Minutos* (20minutos.es), 2 short articles/week.

**Verb warm-up (Phase 2):** conjugate verbs in the subjunctive forms you're learning; keep periodic indicative review so it doesn't decay.

| 18 | **Presente de subjuntivo** — stem-changing + irregular (sea, vaya, haga, tenga, sepa, dé, esté, haya) | GdU: *presente de subjuntivo* irregular | Drill irregular subjunctives daily; 501 | You produce all common irregular present-subjunctives |
| 19 | **Trigger: desires / wishes** (querer que, esperar que, ojalá) | GdU: *expresión de deseos*; Castro-A relevant unit | Sentence-building: main + que + subj | You reflexively switch to subjunctive after a wish verb + *que* |

**▣ Phase 2 self-assessment (end of Wk 40).** Read a Penguin Parallel Text story (Spanish side only). Can you (a) read it without constant recourse to the English, (b) explain every subjunctive, (c) handle the conditional sentences and reported speech? Re-do the relevant weeks for any structure that still trips you. You should now be at a genuine B2 reading level.

# Phase 3 — B2 consolidation (Weeks 41–64 · Months 11–16)

**Goal:** master compound tenses, passive constructions, advanced periphrasis, and — critically — the discourse connectors that hold literary and journalistic prose together. **Primary book:** PSG (its exercises use authentic newspaper and literary extracts). **Reference:** B&B throughout.

**Reading track (Phase 3):** Begin your **first authentic novels**. Recommended order: *Sin noticias de Gurb* (Mendoza — short, hilarious, the canonical "first real novel") → *Manolito Gafotas* (Lindo — colloquial Madrid Spanish) → *Marina* (Ruiz Zafón — accessible gothic YA). Powerful technique: read a **Spanish translation of a novel you already know in English** (e.g., *Harry Potter y la piedra filosofal*) alongside. **News:** *El País* (elpais.com) — start with culture/lifestyle sections; add the **Readlang** browser extension for click-to-translate. **★ Listening:** Notes in Spanish Intermediate/Advanced.

**Verb warm-up (Phase 3):** shift to whole-paradigm speed runs (conjugate a verb across all tenses/moods in 60 seconds) to keep everything active while reading takes over.

# Phase 4 — C1 bridge (Months 17–30 · reading-driven)

**Goal:** consolidate C1 reading through volume and range. Grammar study is now **reactive** — you study a point only when reading exposes a gap. The week-by-week table gives way to **monthly modules**: a reading target plus a focused grammar/vocabulary theme. Cadence stays at two sessions/week, but Session A is now "reading + targeted look-up" rather than systematic textbook progression.

**Primary materials:** *Gramática de uso del español C1-C2* (Aragonés & Palencia — the 106-unit upper volume) for gap-filling; PSG advanced sections; B&B as reference. **Reading:** full authentic literature and journalism.

## Notes on Peninsular-specific features (woven through the plan)

- **Pretérito perfecto** is drilled early (Wk 2) and revisited because central/northern Spain uses *he hecho* for recent past where Latin America uses *hice*. The reading materials are Peninsular, so this matches what you'll see.
- **Vosotros** appears in every imperative and gets a dedicated systematic review (Wk 45). Both Castro and GdU include these forms throughout.
- **Both -ra and -se** imperfect-subjunctive forms are taught together (Wk 29); the *-se* form is more frequent in formal Peninsular writing, so you need to read it fluently.
- **Leísmo** (le for lo as direct object referring to people) is standard in central Spain — flagged at the relative/pronoun stages so it doesn't confuse you in authentic texts.
